# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 49, remains the strongest result:** it achieved a **0.99 combined score and 1.00% mean gap** in 32.32 seconds with 2,000 LP calls. Its combination of repaired, deduplicated, energy-ranked starts; a diverse archive; and surrogate-screened LP batches remains the best-supported pattern.
- **Diverse, energy-screened search outperformed the newer dual-guided variants.** Generation 68, *Diverse LP-Guided Topology Search*, reached a **1.48% gap** and 0.99 score in 33.69 seconds. This is the closest new result to Generation 49, but still has a larger gap. Its diverse seeds and prioritization by surrogate energy alongside LP price signals align with the current best’s strongest features.
- **Diversity and multiple move types help, but do not guarantee comparable quality.** Generation 65’s dual-guided archive search reached a **2.21% gap** (0.98 score), Generation 67’s adaptive dual-guided search reached **2.11%** (0.98), and Generation 69’s link-swap search reached **2.31%** (0.98). Their switches, rotations, kicks, and archives support broad exploration, but the results favor the current best’s more selective energy-and-diversity screening.
- **QAOA initialization can help, but its benefit depends on the search and seed.** Generation 66’s QAOA starts beat random starts on all three evaluated seeds (mean best-known/best **0.9240 vs. 0.9021**). Generation 68 also showed a substantial average advantage (**0.9855 vs. 0.9712**). The current best’s QAOA runs averaged **0.9901 vs. 0.9730** for random starts, although random starts narrowly won on seed 0.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Adding adaptive or dual-guided machinery did not displace the current best.** Generations 65 and 67 used 2,000 LP calls yet had **2.21% and 2.11% gaps**, respectively, versus Generation 49’s **1.00%**. Generation 67’s adaptive parent selection and rotation sampling did not translate into better aggregate solution quality.
- **QAOA-seeded search is not consistently better than random-start search.** In the current best, random starts achieved a slightly smaller seed-0 gap (**0.84% vs. 0.94%**); Generation 65 also found QAOA slightly worse on average (**0.9786 vs. 0.9811** best-known/best), while Generation 67’s averages were close (**0.9794 vs. 0.9771**) and varied by seed. These comparisons show that initialization gains are program- and instance-dependent.
- **Using more search mechanisms or the full LP budget is not sufficient for near-optimality.** Every new program used about 2,000 LP calls, but gaps ranged from **1.48%** in Generation 68 to **8.24%** in Generation 66. Generation 66’s switches, rotations, kicks, dual proxies, and diverse batches still underperformed, indicating that candidate quality and LP selection matter more than move count or budget use alone.
- **The broader historical evidence also cautions against proposal-heavy search.** Generation 53 timed out at 150 seconds and scored 0.0 after generating excessive proposals and failing to make progress. The newer programs did not repeat that failure, but their runtimes of **21.57–45.9 seconds** and larger gaps than Generation 49 illustrate that added search machinery alone is not a quality improvement.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 49 makes LP evaluation selective rather than simply exhaustive.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, ranks starts by `tools.energy`, and chooses a topology-diverse initial subset. Its search screens moves by surrogate-energy change, selects up to six mutually diverse candidates, then evaluates batches of up to nine. This implementation achieved the best observed result: **1.00% mean gap with 2,000 calls in 32.32 seconds**.
- **Its archive balances quality and coverage.** `archive()` first retains up to 24 networks separated by topology distance, then fills to 32 with the best remaining networks; parent selection mixes the top six with the wider archive. This supports both incumbent-focused search and exploration of distinct regions, a pattern consistent with Generation 68’s strong **1.48%** result from diverse starts and LP-guided topology search.
- **Dual prices work best as one proposal signal among several.** The current best uses positive-price links to focus switches and rotations, while also sampling unrestricted switches, stalled-parent rotations, and multi-switch kicks. This avoids relying entirely on potentially degenerate dual guidance. Newer dual-guided programs reached gaps of **2.11–2.31%**, supporting the previous finding that dual information is most useful when paired with surrogate ranking and exploration—not when it dominates selection.
- **Caching and bounded fallback logic help preserve runtime control.** The current best avoids repeated local LP scoring with `seen`, filters cached candidates with `tools.is_cached()`, tries alternate archive members or random kicks when a neighborhood is exhausted, and stops after prolonged lack of progress. This is consistent with its full-budget runtime of **32.32 seconds** and contrasts with Generation 53’s timeout from unbounded or unproductive proposal generation.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt the diversity quota in `screen_batch` to the proposal pool.** Measure pairwise topology distances among the top surrogate-ranked moves; if they cluster tightly, select more mutually distant candidates before filling by energy rank, and if they are already diverse, spend more of the batch on the best-ranked moves. This refines the current program’s effective energy-plus-diversity screening without increasing LP calls.

2. **Track LP improvement yield by archive parent and use it to tune parent selection.** Record each parent’s evaluated batches and improvements, then modestly favor parents with recent gains while reserving a fixed share of selections for distant or underexplored archive members. This builds on the successful diverse archive while testing whether search effort can be allocated more productively without sacrificing coverage.

3. **Tune proposal counts by move type using bounded, observed returns.** Track whether focused switches, unrestricted switches, rotations, and kicks lead to improvements, then shift proposal effort toward productive types while retaining a minimum quota for each and a strict total cap. The current blend is effective, but this can reduce screening work on unproductive proposals; keep surrogate ranking and topology diversity as the final selection criteria.

4. **Preserve QAOA-led starts but explicitly hedge with a small random-start quota.** Repair and deduplicate both pools, choose most initial candidates through energy-ranked diversity from QAOA samples, and reserve a minority for random topologies. QAOA starts have a clear aggregate advantage, but random starts narrowly won on seed 0, so a controlled hedge may improve robustness across instances.

5. **Make stagnation kicks targeted, bounded, and archive-aware.** After repeated stalls, apply a small capped number of degree-preserving switches to non-elite archive members, biasing proposals toward positive-price or high-load links, then pass them through the existing surrogate-and-distance screen. This retains dual prices as one useful proposal signal rather than letting them dominate selection, consistent with the stronger results from balanced energy, diversity, and LP guidance.
