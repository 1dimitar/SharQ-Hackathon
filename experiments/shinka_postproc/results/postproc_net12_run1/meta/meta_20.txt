# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Diverse Archive Topology Local Search (Generation 16), is the strongest evaluated approach:** it achieved a **0.99 combined score**, **1.37% mean gap**, and **0.97 control score** with 2,000 LP calls in **15.08 seconds**. Its combination of a quality-and-diversity archive, degree-preserving swaps and rotations, LP-price/load guidance, and surrogate-ranked LP batches substantially outperformed the other newly evaluated variants.
- **Diversity is most effective when paired with selective evaluation.** Generation 16 ranks candidate batches by surrogate energy but still includes randomized alternatives, and retains topologies that differ from the elite set. This balances cheap screening with protection against surrogate misranking. It improved on Generation 17’s related surrogate-screened archive search (**2.48% gap, 0.98 score, 22.82 seconds**) and Generation 14’s archive/kick search (**2.85%, 0.97, 33.39 seconds**).
- **LP-price or load guidance remains a useful way to focus neighborhood search, especially when combined with broader exploration.** Generation 16 prioritizes hot links but falls back to load-based guidance and has an unfiltered move fallback when targeted proposals dry up. Generation 18’s bottleneck-guided search also performed well (**1.97% gap, 0.98 score**), while its mixed targeted and randomized batch strategy is consistent with the earlier finding that bottleneck focus should not be the sole source of moves.
- **Repaired QAOA starts provide an aggregate advantage, but not a per-seed guarantee.** Generation 16 averaged **0.9865 best-known/best** from QAOA starts versus **0.9722** from random starts. The gap advantage was pronounced on seeds 0 and 1, but reversed on seed 2. This extends prior results showing that QAOA can improve average search quality while individual seeds may favor random initialization.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Broader or more elaborate search does not automatically improve results.** Generation 14 combined two-switches, three-link rotations, dual guidance, an elite archive, kicks, and annealed acceptance, yet reached only a **2.85% gap and 0.97 score**, taking **33.39 seconds**. Generation 17 also used multiple move types and archive-based screening but reached **2.48%** in **22.82 seconds**. Both trailed Generation 16, showing that neighborhood breadth alone is not enough.
- **A strong QAOA-versus-random advantage does not by itself predict the best absolute result.** Generation 20 had a relatively large initialization difference (**0.9689 vs. 0.9317**), but its mean gap was **3.24%** and score **0.97**, versus Generation 16’s **1.37% gap and 0.99 score**. Search quality must be judged by the resulting gaps, not just the initialization comparison.
- **Dual guidance alone is not consistently sufficient.** Generation 14’s dual-guided search had a **2.85% gap**, and Generation 20’s dual-guided QAOA search had a **3.24% gap**. By comparison, Generation 16 also uses price/load guidance but couples it with archive diversity, energy-ranked batches, and randomized alternatives. The results do not isolate which component caused the difference, but they show that guidance without an equally effective exploration and evaluation strategy did not match the current best.
- **Initialization can lose on individual seeds even for the top-performing method.** In Generation 16, random starts slightly beat QAOA starts on seed 2 (**1.53% vs. 1.64% gap**). Similar seed reversals were reported for Generations 17 and 18, reinforcing that average initialization gains are not reliable for every instance seed.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 16’s archive explicitly balances quality and topology diversity.** It keeps up to 18 evaluated networks, ranks them by LP score, preserves an elite subset, and retains additional members only when their edge sets differ sufficiently. This gives the search multiple strong basins without discarding the incumbent; its **1.37% mean gap** is the best among the programs described here.
- **The program separates cheap proposal ordering from expensive LP calls.** It generates valid degree-preserving switches and three-edge rotations, sorts fresh candidates by `tools.energy`, evaluates a capped batch of up to nine, and sometimes adds a randomized tail candidate. This is a concrete instance of the earlier successful pattern: use surrogate screening to direct the fixed 2,000-call LP budget, but do not hard-filter everything except the lowest-energy proposals.
- **Bottleneck guidance is adaptive rather than exclusive.** Generation 16 builds a hot-link set from positive LP prices, falls back to high-load links when prices are unavailable, and retries without hot-link restrictions if targeted search produces no fresh candidates. This preserves targeted exploration while avoiding a stalled or overly narrow neighborhood.
- **The current best is both more accurate and faster than the other newly summarized variants.** At **15.08 seconds**, Generation 16 is faster than Generation 18 (**20.43 seconds**), Generation 17 (**22.82 seconds**), Generation 20 (**28.16 seconds**), and Generation 14 (**33.39 seconds**), while also achieving the lowest mean gap. The previous global best’s reported **37.28 seconds** makes this quality/runtime combination especially strong, although the evaluation details are not identical enough to attribute the difference to any single implementation choice.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 16 leads the current comparison on both score and mean gap:** **0.99 score and 1.37% gap**, versus Generation 18 at **0.98 and 1.97%**, Generation 17 at **0.98 and 2.48%**, and Generations 14 and 20 at **0.97 and 2.85% / 3.24%**. All used about the same 2,000-call LP budget, so the gap differences are not explained by a larger stated LP allowance.
- **Its per-seed results are consistently strong, though not uniform:** QAOA-start gaps were **0.94%, 1.53%, and 1.64%**. Random-start gaps were **3.68%, 3.40%, and 1.53%**. Thus QAOA initialization delivered a substantial benefit on two seeds, while random starts narrowly won on the third.
- **The current best’s quality advantage comes with no runtime penalty in this group.** Generation 16 averaged **15.08 seconds**, compared with **20.43 seconds** for Generation 18, **22.82 seconds** for Generation 17, **28.16 seconds** for Generation 20, and **33.39 seconds** for Generation 14. This contrasts with the previous global-best comparison, where the strongest reported gap came with higher runtime.
- **Across the evaluations, archive diversity and screened evaluation appear more predictive of strong results than adding move types or emphasizing initialization gains.** Generation 16’s archive-and-screening combination achieved the best reported result here, while searches with rotations, kicks, or dual guidance but weaker absolute outcomes did not surpass it. Earlier global evidence also favored diverse populations and surrogate-ranked LP batches, while cautioning that these components’ benefits depend on how they are combined.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make each LP batch topology-diverse as well as energy-ranked.** Keep the strongest `tools.energy` proposals, but reject near-duplicates by edge-set difference and fill the remaining batch slots with distinct candidates that have competitive energy. This extends the archive’s proven diversity strategy to the expensive evaluation step, reducing the chance that one batch spends many LP calls exploring the same local direction.

2. **Rank proposals using explicit directional LP-price benefit alongside energy.** For each switch or rotation, compute the sum of prices on removed links minus the sum on added links, then use that signal with surrogate energy to order candidates; reserve some batch slots for candidates favored by either signal. Generation 16’s price-guided neighborhoods performed strongly, and this can distinguish moves that actually relieve priced bottlenecks from moves that merely touch hot links.

3. **Use compound rotations selectively after local stagnation.** Track unsuccessful batches per parent, and after several without improvement generate additional three-edge rotations centered on that parent’s highest-price links; rank them with the same energy and price signals. This builds on the program’s existing rotations without paying their proposal cost uniformly, and provides a targeted escape from neighborhoods where ordinary switches are unproductive.

4. **Allocate LP effort according to archive-member productivity while preserving exploration.** Track evaluations and improvements for each parent, then select productive archive members more often while retaining a minimum share of searches for diverse, less-tested members. This could improve the use of the fixed LP budget while preserving the multi-basin exploration that distinguishes the current best from weaker guidance-only searches.

5. **Add bounded, stagnation-triggered kicks from diverse archive members.** After a longer period without improving the incumbent, perturb a high-quality but non-elite archive member with a small number of degree-preserving switches, increasing kick size only if progress remains stalled; return to ordinary moves after an improvement. This extends the existing adaptive `kick_size` mechanism into deliberate basin changes while retaining the strong archive and targeted local search.
