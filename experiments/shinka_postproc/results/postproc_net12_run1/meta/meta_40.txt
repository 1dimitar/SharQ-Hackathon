# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 16, Diverse Archive Topology Local Search, remains the clear leader:** 0.99 combined score, **1.37% mean gap**, 0.97 control score, and 15.08 seconds at the full 2,000 LP calls. Its quality-and-diversity archive, screened batches of degree-preserving switches and three-edge rotations, and fallback from guided to unrestricted proposals combine strong solution quality with the best reported runtime.
- **A bounded archive that protects elite networks while preserving distinct basins is repeatedly associated with strong results.** Generation 16 uses an explicit quality/diversity rule and performed better than the newer archive-based variants. Generation 39’s archive and path relinking achieved 0.98 and a 1.71% gap, while Generation 34 previously reached 0.99 and a 1.47% gap. These results support the archive pattern, though Generation 16’s balance and implementation were stronger.
- **Dual prices and loads work best as proposal guidance alongside broad search, not as hard constraints.** Generation 16 biases moves toward priced or hot links but falls back to unrestricted moves when guided proposals are exhausted. By comparison, the recent dual-guided programs achieved gaps of 1.64%–3.09% (Generations 36–40), generally worse than Generation 16.
- **Repaired QAOA starts provide a consistent average advantage, but search determines the final quality.** Generation 16’s QAOA starts averaged 0.9865 best-known/best versus 0.9722 for random starts. Its seed-level gaps were 0.94%, 1.53%, and 1.64%; random starts were worse on seeds 0 and 1 (3.68% and 3.40%) but slightly better on seed 2 (1.53%). Thus initialization helps on average but does not eliminate seed variation.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Adding move types and dual-guidance machinery has not reliably beaten the simpler, well-balanced search.** Generation 36 used two-link switches and targeted three-link rotations and achieved 1.68% mean gap in 43 seconds; Generation 37’s dual-price neighborhood search achieved 1.64% in 22.29 seconds; Generation 38 reached 3.09%; Generation 39 reached 1.71%; and Generation 40 reached 2.06%. All used 2,000 LP calls, yet none matched Generation 16’s 1.37% gap and 15.08 seconds.
- **More extensive exploration can cost time without improving accuracy.** Generation 36’s random kicks and multiple neighborhood operators took about 43 seconds, while Generation 16 reached a lower gap in roughly 15 seconds. Generation 40’s bottleneck-focused kicks and annealed moves also took 33.31 seconds for a 2.06% gap. These outcomes suggest that added search machinery is not beneficial unless its evaluations are allocated as effectively as Generation 16’s screened batches.
- **Full LP-budget use and strong QAOA initialization are not sufficient by themselves.** Generations 36–40 all spent the full 2,000 LP calls and generally showed QAOA advantages over random starts, but their gaps ranged from 1.64% to 3.09%. Earlier, Generation 35 likewise had QAOA starts that beat random starts but still reached a 3.92% gap. Final quality depends on neighborhood selection and search organization, not just budget consumption or seed quality.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 16’s archive combines an elite core with structural diversity.** It stores up to 18 scored networks, protects a high-quality portion, and uses edge-set symmetric difference to retain distinct alternatives. Parent selection favors strong archive members while sometimes sampling more distant ones. This gives the search access to multiple basins without allowing the archive to become a collection of near-duplicates, and accompanies the best observed gap.
- **Its evaluation pipeline separates cheap ordering from expensive scoring.** Repaired candidates are validated, deduplicated, and ordered by `tools.energy`; candidate moves are canonicalized and filtered against both local scores and the tool cache before LP evaluation. Fresh moves are sorted by energy and evaluated in batches of up to nine, with an occasional randomized tail candidate. Energy therefore guides LP allocation without strictly excluding alternatives.
- **Guidance remains a bias rather than a restriction.** Generation 16 uses positive LP prices or high loads to target switches and rotations, then retries without the hot-link constraint if guided proposals run dry. This contrasts with the weaker outcomes of several newer dual-guided searches and shows why fallback breadth matters alongside targeted proposals.
- **The current best uses adaptive exploration without sacrificing its screened evaluation strategy.** It selects parents from the best archive members, samples distant archive members more often after stagnation, and increases kick size as the search stalls. The evaluated newer variants also use kicks, annealing, or targeted rotations, but their weaker gaps and longer runtimes indicate that these mechanisms alone do not reproduce Generation 16’s effective combination of archive safeguards, screening, and fallback.

- **Generation 16 is the best evaluated program:** 0.99 combined score, **1.37% mean gap**, and **15.08 seconds** with 2,000 LP calls. The nearest reported results are Generation 34 at 0.99 and 1.47% in 25.93 seconds, Generation 36 at 0.98 and 1.68% in about 43 seconds, and Generation 39 at 0.98 and 1.71% in 27.08 seconds.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make each LP batch topology-diverse while preserving energy ranking.** After sorting fresh moves by `tools.energy`, greedily select up to nine candidates that differ sufficiently in edge-set symmetric difference, then fill any remaining slots with the best unselected candidates. This extends the archive’s proven diversity safeguard to expensive LP evaluations and may reduce redundant calls.

2. **Reserve a small number of batch slots for price-guided moves.** Estimate a move’s directional price benefit as the sum of prices on removed arcs minus the sum on added arcs, and use it to rank candidates or reserve one or two slots for high-benefit moves. Keep energy-ranked candidates and the unrestricted fallback, since the strongest results use dual information as guidance rather than a hard filter.

3. **Allocate parent searches using measured productivity, with a diversity floor.** Track each archive member’s LP evaluations and improvements, then sample productive parents more often while reserving a fixed share of searches for less-tested or structurally distant members. This builds on the strong archive and parent-selection scheme while using the fixed LP budget more deliberately.

4. **Trigger stronger rotations selectively after local stagnation.** Track unsuccessful batches for the chosen parent and, after several failures, increase the share of three-edge rotations involving its highest-price arcs; return to the normal move mix after an improvement. Rotations already complement switches in the leading program, so making them conditional could expand search only where it is most useful.

5. **Use a bounded, diversity-preserving kick when global progress stalls.** After sustained stagnation, select a strong non-elite archive member and apply a few degree-preserving switches before resuming the usual screened search; increase kick size gradually only if stagnation continues. Preserve the existing elites and archive alternatives so basin exploration does not discard the quality and diversity that underpin the current result.
