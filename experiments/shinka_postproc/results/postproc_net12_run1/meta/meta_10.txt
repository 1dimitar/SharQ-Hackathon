# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Dual-Guided Network Topology Search, leads on mean gap:** it achieved a **2.08% mean gap** and **0.98 combined score**, improving on the previous best’s **3.09% gap** at score **0.97**. Its dual-price bottleneck screening, surrogate-ranked neighborhoods, and archive-based exploration are associated with the strongest result in these evaluations.
- **Repairing and deduplicating QAOA candidates remains a strong initialization pattern, but its benefit varies.** The current best had a modest QAOA advantage over random starts (**0.9797 vs. 0.9746** mean best-known/best). The earlier Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search showed a larger advantage (**0.9768 vs. 0.9496**), while QAOA-Guided Bottleneck-Weighted Network Search also improved substantially (**0.9626 vs. 0.9224**).
- **Dual- or LP-guided move prioritization is common among the strongest programs.** The current best screens moves using surrogate energy and whether they remove a dual-priced link; Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search further prioritizes high-price or high-load links. Both achieved **0.98** combined score, though the former had the better mean gap (**2.08% vs. 2.38%**).

## Ineffective Approaches
- **QAOA initialization is not uniformly better on every seed.** In the current best, random starts beat QAOA on seed 0 (**1.64% vs. 3.19% gap**), but QAOA won on seeds 1 and 2 (**0.40% vs. 2.92%** and **2.66% vs. 3.26%**). Dual-Guided Network Topology Search therefore has only a modest aggregate QAOA advantage despite its strong overall result.
- **Broader or more elaborate search did not consistently improve on focused exchange search.** The current best’s two-switches plus sampled three-link rotations achieved a **2.08% gap**, but Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search, also scoring **0.98**, did slightly worse at **2.38%**. Earlier annealed search with broader exploration scored **0.95** at a **5.66% gap**, supporting the previous finding that added move types and annealing do not guarantee better outcomes.
- **The QAOA-guided repair-and-descent variants remain behind the current best on mean gap.** Generations 7–9 achieved gaps of **3.48%**, **3.28%**, and **3.91%**, respectively, with scores of **0.97**, **0.97**, and **0.96**. Their strong QAOA-versus-random comparisons did not translate into the best overall gap.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best combines several distinct controls on LP expenditure.** It repairs and deduplicates starts, caches scores, screens fresh neighbors first by surrogate energy, then adds dual-focused and random selections. This concentrates LP evaluation on promising and bottleneck-relevant moves while preserving exploration; it reached **2,000 LP calls** and a **2.08% mean gap**.
- **Its archive supports exploration without discarding the incumbent.** The program retains up to 12 high-quality, sufficiently diverse networks, branches from archive members, and periodically resets the search state to a promising archive entry. A simulated-annealing acceptance rule allows occasional uphill moves, while `best_net`/`best_score` separately preserve the best found solution.
- **The best result does not come from simply using more LP calls.** The current best, Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search, and the earlier leading QAOA-guided searches all used **2,000 LP calls**. The current best’s advantage over the previous **3.09%** leader is therefore associated with its neighborhood screening and archive/acceptance design, not a larger reported LP budget.
- **The scoring evidence favors dual information as a move-ordering signal.** The current best obtains active links from positive LP prices, falling back to near-maximum loads, then prioritizes moves that remove those links among surrogate-ranked candidates. This is consistent with the strong results of other price/load-prioritized searches, although the summaries do not isolate the contribution of each individual component.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best is the top reported result in this evaluation set by mean gap and combined score:** **2.08% gap, 0.98 score**, tied on score with Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search (**2.38% gap**) and ahead of the previous global best (**3.09% gap, 0.97 score**).
- **Its seed-level results show both strong performance and initialization sensitivity.** QAOA gaps were **3.19%, 0.40%, and 2.66%**; random-start gaps were **1.64%, 2.92%, and 3.26%**. QAOA wins on two seeds, yielding the small aggregate advantage (**0.9797 vs. 0.9746**), but is clearly not better on every instance.
- **The 2.08% mean gap improves substantially on the other new programs.** Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search had **2.38%**; QAOA-Guided Bottleneck-Weighted Network Search had **3.91%**; QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search had **3.48%**; and QAOA-Guided LP Local Search had **3.28%**. The lower gap, rather than a higher LP-call budget, distinguishes the current best.
- **Runtime is a tradeoff:** the current best averaged **23.47 seconds**, slower than Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search (**20.51 seconds**) and much slower than QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search (**11.75 seconds**). Its stronger gap is therefore accompanied by greater runtime, while LP-call use remains at the common **2,000-call** limit.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Rank exchanges by directional dual benefit.** For each proposed move, compute the change in bottleneck value from removed to added links—for example, sum the prices or normalized loads of removed links minus those of added links—and use it to order candidates before spending LP calls. The current best already benefits from dual-guided screening, while directional scoring may distinguish genuinely relieving moves from exchanges that merely remove one priced link and add another.

2. **Make kicks adaptive and archive-aware.** After repeated iterations without improving `best_score`, increase the number of swaps in a kick; after an improvement, return to smaller kicks. Start kicks from a diverse, high-quality archive member as well as the incumbent, preserving the current program’s archive-based exploration while testing a focused alternative to its fixed-size sampling and periodic resets.

3. **Allocate LP calls according to search productivity.** Track improvement yield and calls spent for each starting network or archive branch, then direct more descent effort toward productive branches while reserving a small minimum budget for others. This builds on the strong repaired-and-deduplicated initialization pattern and may use the fixed 2,000-call budget more effectively, especially given that QAOA’s advantage varies by seed.

4. **Use a targeted compound-move phase after two-switch stagnation.** When the screened two-switch neighborhood fails to improve, generate a bounded number of three-link rotations centered on the highest-price or highest-load links, and rank them using both surrogate energy and directional dual benefit. The current best already includes sampled rotations and achieves the best mean gap; triggering and targeting them specifically at local optima may capture useful escapes without broadening every search iteration.

5. **Improve candidate selection with a small, diversity-aware LP batch.** Keep the current surrogate-ranked and dual-focused selections, but remove near-duplicate top candidates and replace some with candidates that are farther from the current network or archive while still having competitive surrogate scores. This preserves the program’s effective screening and occasional exploration while reducing LP calls spent on similar moves and maintaining access to distinct promising topologies.
