# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **LP-priced bottleneck search combined with surrogate screening is the strongest pattern in the evaluations.** The current best, **LP-Guided QAOA Network Refinement**, achieved a **1.76% mean gap and 0.98 combined score**, improving on the previous best’s **2.08% gap** at the same score. It uses LP prices to identify hot links, screens candidate moves cheaply using QUBO energy and demand, and spends LP calls on a selected batch.
- **Maintaining a diverse set of strong solutions can support effective exploration.** The current best keeps a population of up to 24 networks, prefers members that differ substantially in their links, and periodically combines members with repaired crossovers. Its three QAOA-run gaps were **1.25%, 2.49%, and 1.53%**, all better than the previous leader’s reported mean gap.
- **Mixing targeted and unconstrained exchanges helps avoid over-reliance on bottleneck-only neighborhoods.** The current best prioritizes switches involving positive-price links, but also adds unconstrained switches every other iteration and occasional crossovers. This complements the prior evidence favoring dual-guided moves while retaining paths to escape locally focused neighborhoods.
- **Repaired QAOA starts remain useful in aggregate, though their benefit varies by seed.** The current best’s mean best-known/best ratio was **0.9827 for QAOA starts versus 0.9736 for random starts**. Other examples include Generation 13’s **0.9630 vs. 0.9229** and Generation 15’s **0.9701 vs. 0.9308**, though the current best lost to random starts on one seed.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **QAOA initialization is not a per-seed guarantee.** For the current best, random starts did better on seed 2 (**279,334** versus **280,715** for QAOA; gaps **1.03% vs. 1.53%**). Similar reversals occurred in earlier evaluations, including seed 1 for Generation 10 and seed 2 for Generation 12.
- **Strong initialization advantages do not necessarily yield the best overall gaps.** Generation 11 had a sizable QAOA-versus-random ratio advantage (**0.9626 vs. 0.9224**) but a **3.91% mean gap** and **0.96 score**. Generation 13 also had a large initialization advantage (**0.9630 vs. 0.9229**) but a **3.85% gap** and **0.96 score**, well behind the current best’s 1.76% gap.
- **Broader search mechanisms and additional move types have not reliably improved results.** Prior results found that annealed broader exploration reached a **5.66% gap and 0.95 score**, while the more focused current-best search reached **1.76% and 0.98**. Three-link rotations likewise did not beat the earlier focused-exchange leader, underscoring that added neighborhood breadth alone is not sufficient.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best separates cheap proposal ranking from expensive LP evaluation.** It deduplicates valid repaired candidates, generates switch and crossover proposals, orders fresh networks by surrogate energy and demand-on-selected-links, then LP-evaluates a capped batch (with occasional random picks). This directs the fixed **2,000-call budget** toward screened proposals while retaining some protection against surrogate misranking.
- **Its population and incumbent tracking serve different purposes.** The population is pruned for both quality and edge-set diversity, while `best_net`/`best_score` retain the best result found. This allows search to branch from multiple strong topologies without losing the incumbent; the program also caches and skips already evaluated networks.
- **LP details are used for move ordering rather than as the only search constraint.** Positive-price links define the hot set for targeted exchanges, but the program periodically adds all valid switches. That combination is consistent with the broader evaluation evidence: price/load prioritization is common in the strongest searches, while exploration beyond bottlenecks helps avoid an overly narrow neighborhood.
- **Runtime remains a notable cost of this implementation.** The current best used **37.28 seconds on average** for 2,000 LP calls, compared with **25.66 seconds** for Generation 15 and **23.57 seconds** for Generation 11. Its better gap is therefore not explained by a larger LP budget; it comes with substantially more wall-clock time among these evaluated variants.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best leads this evaluation set on mean gap:** **1.76%**, with a **0.98 combined score**. It improves on the previous global best, Dual-Guided Network Topology Search (**2.08%, 0.98**), and the prior 0.98-scoring Bottleneck-Guided Network Local Search (**2.38%**).
- **The current best is materially ahead of the other newly evaluated programs on gap.** Generation 10 had **2.54%** and score **0.98**; Generation 15 had **3.09%** and **0.97**; Generations 11 and 13 had **3.91%** and **3.85%**, both scoring **0.96**. The current best used the same **2,000 LP calls** as these programs.
- **Seed results show consistent quality but a modest QAOA-control aggregate advantage.** The current best’s QAOA gaps were **1.25–2.49%**, compared with random-start gaps of **1.03–4.07%**. Its mean ratio advantage (**0.9827 vs. 0.9736**) is positive, but smaller than some earlier QAOA-seeded searches and does not hold on every seed.
- **The gap improvement comes with higher runtime.** At **37.28 seconds**, the current best is slower than the earlier 2.08%-gap leader (**23.47 seconds**) and the 2.38%-gap search (**20.51 seconds**). The evaluation therefore shows a stronger quality result at the common LP-call limit, but with a meaningful time tradeoff.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Rank exchanges by directional dual benefit before surrogate energy.** For each two-switch candidate, calculate the sum of prices on removed links minus the sum on added links, and use this as an explicit ranking feature alongside `tools.energy` and demand on selected links. The current program successfully uses LP prices to focus proposals, but this could distinguish exchanges that actually relieve priced bottlenecks from those that merely involve them.

2. **Make LP batch selection diverse, not just top-ranked.** Keep the best energy-ranked candidates, but remove near-duplicates by edge overlap and reserve part of each batch for distinct candidates with competitive energy and strong directional dual benefit. This builds on the existing cheap screening and diversity-aware population, while reducing the risk of spending LP calls on similar proposals.

3. **Trigger targeted compound moves after local stagnation.** Track whether a parent’s screened two-switch neighborhood improves the incumbent; after several unsuccessful rounds, generate a bounded set of three-link rotations centered on its highest-price links and rank them with the same surrogate and dual signals. The current search already mixes targeted and unconstrained two-switches, so making compound moves conditional may add escape routes without paying their cost every iteration.

4. **Adapt search effort to branch productivity.** Track LP calls and incumbent or population improvements for each archive member, then allocate more neighborhood evaluations to productive branches while retaining a minimum budget for other diverse members. This extends the current round-robin archive search and may make better use of its fixed LP budget when some starting topologies are much more promising than others.

5. **Use adaptive, archive-based kicks when progress stalls.** After repeated iterations without improving `best_score`, generate a small number of multi-switch perturbations from a diverse, high-quality population member; increase kick size with continued stagnation and return to ordinary exchanges after an improvement. This preserves the strong archive and dual-guided local search while offering a controlled way out of bottleneck-focused local optima.
