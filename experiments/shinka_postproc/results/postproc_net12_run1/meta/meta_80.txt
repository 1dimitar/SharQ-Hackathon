# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

- **Implementation**: Seeds a topology pool from repaired QAOA candidates, selects diverse elite starts, and explores degree-preserving two-switches and three-cycle rotations. It prioritizes LP-priced links, ranks proposals with a surrogate energy, and evaluates valid candidates with LP calls during annealed walks with stagnation reheating.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 49, remains the strongest measured result:** 0.99 combined score, **1.00% mean gap**, 2,000 LP calls, and 32.32 seconds. Its combination of repaired and deduplicated starts, surrogate-energy ranking, topology-diverse selection, and selectively screened LP batches continues to outperform the newer evaluated variants.
- **Energy ranking paired with explicit diversity is repeatedly associated with strong performance.** Generation 49 selects starts by energy while seeking topology distance of at least 4, then screens neighborhood batches using energy change and distance. Generation 76’s diverse LP-guided search also achieved 0.99, with a **1.15% mean gap** in about 41 seconds. This supports the pattern, although Generation 49 has the better gap and runtime.
- **QAOA starts improve average outcomes, especially on difficult seeds, but do not win every seed.** In Generation 49, QAOA starts had a 0.9901 mean best-known/best ratio versus 0.9730 for random starts. They were better on seeds 1 and 2, but random starts narrowly won seed 0 (0.84% gap versus 0.94%). Generation 76 and Generation 82 also reported clear average QAOA advantages, while Generation 79’s advantage was smaller and less consistent.
- **Focused search signals work best when balanced with broader exploration.** Generation 49 uses LP prices to focus some moves but retains unrestricted swaps, rotations, and multi-switch kicks. This yielded a 1.00% gap, compared with 1.51–2.59% for Generations 82 and 79, which also used dual-guided adaptive or archive-based search but had higher gaps.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Unbounded proposal generation can invalidate an otherwise promising search.** Generation 70 scored 0.0 after exceeding the 150-second limit: it continued generating and filtering moves even when no fresh candidates remained for LP evaluation. This contrasts with Generation 49’s completed 2,000-call run in 32.32 seconds.
- **More search machinery has not reliably improved solution quality.** Generation 82’s adaptive move mix achieved 0.99 but had a 1.51% gap, worse than Generation 49’s 1.00%; Generation 78’s dual-guided search had a 1.80% gap, and Generation 79’s archive-kick approach had a 2.59% gap. Their use of the full LP budget did not overcome the quality difference.
- **Diversity and QAOA initialization do not guarantee a consistent advantage.** Generation 79 reported QAOA starts averaging 0.9748 versus 0.9673 for random starts, but QAOA beat random starts on only two of three instances. In the current best, QAOA also lost on seed 0. Across these results, the aggregate advantage can be driven by a subset of seeds.
- **Near-identical LP-call counts and runtimes do not imply comparable quality.** Generations 78–82 generally used 2,000 LP calls and ran in roughly 30–34 seconds, yet their gaps ranged from 1.51% to 2.59%; Generation 49 achieved 1.00% at 2,000 calls and 32.32 seconds. Search selection and candidate quality, rather than budget use alone, distinguish these outcomes.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 49’s initialization uses a concrete quality–coverage tradeoff.** It repairs and deduplicates candidate networks, sorts them by surrogate energy, and first selects networks at topology distance at least 4, then fills remaining slots with the best unselected candidates. This avoids spending all initial evaluations on structurally similar low-energy starts.
- **Its LP batch screening limits evaluation to promising, varied moves.** `screen_batch` filters already-seen and cached topologies, ranks fresh moves by energy change from the parent, selects up to six with pairwise distance at least 2, and fills a batch of at most nine. This selective policy coexists with full budget use and the best observed gap.
- **The archive combines high-quality solutions with coverage.** Generation 49 retains up to 24 networks separated by distance, then fills the archive to 32 with the best remaining networks. Parent selection chooses from the top six 48% of the time and otherwise samples the wider archive. This combines exploitation and exploration without sacrificing the strong 1.00% result.
- **Bounded fallback behavior supports predictable execution.** Generation 49 checks `lp_calls_left`, filters cached candidates, tries alternate archive parents when a neighborhood is exhausted, and bounds random-kick attempts. Generation 70’s timeout demonstrates the cost of continuing proposal work without fresh evaluable candidates; the current best’s layered fallback avoids that failure in practice.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt `screen_batch` diversity to the candidate pool.** Measure pairwise topology distances among the best energy-ranked moves, then choose more diverse candidates when those moves cluster and more low-energy candidates when they are already well spread. Keep the batch cap unchanged so this tests selection quality without increasing LP use.

2. **Hedge QAOA initialization with a small random-start quota.** Repair and deduplicate both pools, select most initial starts from the energy-ranked, topology-diverse QAOA candidates, and reserve a minority for random candidates. QAOA starts are better on average, but random starts narrowly win seed 0, so a quota could improve robustness without giving up the aggregate advantage.

3. **Allocate parent searches by observed improvement yield.** Track recent LP improvement rates for archive parents and modestly favor productive parents, while reserving a fixed share of searches for distant or infrequently selected archive members. This extends the successful elite-plus-diverse archive strategy without letting exploitation erase coverage.

4. **Tune move proposals using improvement yield while preserving a balanced mix.** Record which focused switches, unrestricted switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks produce LP improvements; adjust their proposal counts within fixed minimums and a strict total cap. Keep every move type available and continue screening by surrogate energy and topology distance, since overly focused dual-guided search has not matched the current best.

5. **Make stagnation kicks explore underrepresented archive regions.** After repeated stalls, choose a non-elite or infrequently selected archive parent and generate a small, bounded set of degree-preserving kicks; bias some toward positive-price or high-load links but leave others unrestricted. Pass all kicks through the existing surrogate-and-distance screen, retaining bounded retries to avoid wasting time when no fresh candidates remain.
