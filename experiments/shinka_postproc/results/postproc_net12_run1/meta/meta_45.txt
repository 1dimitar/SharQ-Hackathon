# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

It seeds this archive with repaired, deduplicated candidates ordered by surrogate energy, reducing wasted LP calls on near-duplicates.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make each LP batch structurally diverse while retaining its current rankings.** After ordering fresh proposals by dual benefit and surrogate energy, greedily select up to seven whose edge-set symmetric differences from already selected moves exceed a threshold, then fill remaining slots with the highest-ranked unselected proposals. This extends the archive’s proven diversity safeguard to costly LP evaluations and may reduce redundant calls.

2. **Allocate searches across archive members using measured productivity.** Track LP evaluations and incumbent improvements per parent, bias parent selection toward productive members, and reserve a fixed share of iterations for less-tested or structurally distant archive members. This could use the 2,000-call budget more effectively without losing the diverse restarts that distinguish the best program.

3. **Trigger stronger rotations selectively after parent-level stagnation.** Count unsuccessful batches for each parent and, after several failures, increase the number of three-edge rotations—especially those involving positively priced arcs—before returning to the normal mix when progress resumes. Rotations already complement switches in the best program, so conditional escalation may explore harder basins without paying their overhead on every iteration.

4. **Use a bounded, diversity-preserving kick when global progress stalls.** After sustained stagnation, choose a strong but non-elite archive member, apply a small number of valid degree-preserving switches, and resume screened search from the resulting topology; increase kick size gradually only if stagnation continues. Preserve the existing elites and archive alternatives so exploration does not discard the strong basins established by repaired QAOA seeds.

5. **Tune the balance of dual-guided and energy-ranked batch slots using observed results.** Compare small fixed allocations—for example, one versus two dual-best moves—while keeping energy-ranked, structurally distinct candidates and an occasional unrestricted alternative. The best program’s advantage comes from using dual prices as a bias rather than a hard filter, and careful tuning may improve LP-call efficiency without narrowing the neighborhood.
