# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Diverse Archive Topology Local Search (Generation 16), remains the strongest evaluated approach.** It achieves a **0.99 combined score and 1.37% mean gap** in **15.08 seconds**, using 2,000 LP calls. Its quality-and-diversity archive, screened batches of degree-preserving switches and three-edge rotations, and fallback from guided to unrestricted moves are associated with better results and lower runtime than the newer variants.
- **A quality-and-diversity archive works well when it retains both elite and structurally distinct networks.** Generation 16 keeps up to 18 scored networks, protects strong candidates, and favors alternatives with sufficient edge-set difference. The newer **Dual-Guided Diverse Network Local Search (Generation 34)** also used a quality/diversity archive and reached **0.99 score, 1.47% gap** in **25.93 seconds**. Its result is close to the leader, reinforcing archive value, though it is slower and slightly less accurate.
- **Price/load guidance is most effective when paired with broad search and screened evaluation.** Generation 16 uses positive dual prices or high loads to target moves, but falls back to unrestricted proposals when guided moves run dry. Generation 34’s price-guided batches and diverse rotations also performed strongly. This is consistent with weaker outcomes from guidance-heavy approaches that do not match the same search balance.
- **Repaired QAOA seeds provide a useful starting advantage, but are not sufficient by themselves.** Generation 16’s QAOA starts averaged **0.9865 best-known/best**, versus **0.9722** for random starts. The new programs show the same direction: Generation 34 reports **0.9857 vs. 0.9752**, Generation 32 **0.9701 vs. 0.9308**, and Generation 31 **0.9736 vs. 0.9591**. The strongest final results still align with the search strategy, not initialization advantage alone.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **More guidance and move types do not guarantee better results.** Generation 30’s QAOA-guided search with dual prices and two-edge swaps scored **0.96 with a 3.70% gap**; Generation 31’s cyclic reassignment and hot-link guidance scored **0.97 with a 2.72% gap**; Generation 32’s selective three-link rotations and archive scored **0.97 with a 3.09% gap**. All used the full 2,000 LP calls, yet none matched Generation 16’s **1.37% gap**.
- **Archive and QAOA seeding alone do not ensure efficient search.** Generation 35’s diverse QAOA-seeded search scored **0.96 with a 3.92% gap** and took **38.91 seconds**, despite QAOA starts beating random starts on all three instances. This contrasts with Generation 16, which couples diverse seeds to archive-preserving local search and completes in **15.08 seconds**.
- **Dual guidance without sufficiently effective exploration can leave substantial gaps.** The new variants’ mean gaps range from **2.72% to 3.92%** for Generations 30–35 (excluding Generation 34’s stronger 1.47%), while earlier dual-guided programs reached gaps as high as **5.75%–6.45%**. Full LP-budget use is therefore not enough to overcome weak basin exploration or an ineffective move/evaluation allocation strategy.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 16’s archive has explicit quality and diversity safeguards.** It admits scored networks, protects a high-quality portion of the archive, and preserves alternatives based on edge-set symmetric difference. Parent selection usually favors strong members but sometimes samples more distant archive members, helping balance exploitation with basin exploration. This pattern is consistent with its leading **1.37% gap**.
- **Generation 16 separates cheap screening from expensive LP evaluation.** It canonicalizes networks, caches scores, rejects previously evaluated or cached proposals, sorts fresh moves by `tools.energy`, and evaluates batches of up to nine. Energy is an ordering signal rather than a strict filter, with an occasional randomized tail proposal to preserve exploration. This accompanies both high solution quality and the best reported runtime.
- **Guidance is used as a proposal bias, not a restriction.** Generation 16 biases switches toward priced links and rotations toward hot links, then retries without the hot-link constraint if guided proposals are exhausted. In contrast, the newer programs also use dual prices to focus moves, but their weaker mean gaps show that targeting alone does not substitute for broad, diverse proposals.
- **The evaluated QAOA-start advantage is consistent but seed-dependent.** Generation 16’s QAOA results beat random-start results on seeds 0 and 1, but seed 2 is slightly worse (**1.64% vs. 1.53% gap**). Generation 34 likewise reports seed variation (**0.40%–3.17% gaps**). Thus, the seed pool helps on average, while the archive and continued search remain important for robustness.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 16 remains the overall leader:** **0.99 score, 1.37% mean gap, 0.97 control score, and 15.08 seconds** at 2,000 LP calls. The closest new result is Generation 34 at **0.99 and 1.47%**, but it takes **25.93 seconds**—about 10.9 seconds longer.
- **Among the other new programs, Generation 31 is the next strongest by mean gap:** **2.72%**, followed by Generation 32 at **3.09%**, Generation 30 at **3.70%**, and Generation 35 at **3.92%**. Their runtimes range from **25.18 to 38.91 seconds**, all slower than Generation 16.
- **All new programs used approximately 2,000 LP calls, but their outcomes differ substantially.** The new results span **1.47%–3.92% mean gap** (excluding the persistent Generation 16 leader at 1.37%), showing that budget consumption alone does not explain solution quality; search organization and proposal selection matter.
- **QAOA-versus-random comparisons are positive on average, but do not track final quality reliably.** Generation 35 has a clear initialization advantage (**0.9627 vs. 0.9303**) yet a **3.92% gap**, while Generation 34’s smaller advantage (**0.9857 vs. 0.9752**) accompanies a **1.47% gap**. Generation 16’s strong absolute result therefore reflects the full archive-and-search design, not just seed quality.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make each LP batch topology-diverse, not just energy-ranked.** After sorting fresh moves by `tools.energy`, greedily select proposals whose edge-set symmetric difference from already selected batch members exceeds a threshold, then fill unused slots with the best remaining proposals. This extends the archive’s successful diversity safeguards to the costly evaluation step and reduces redundant LP calls.

2. **Use LP price benefit to rank and reserve batch proposals.** For each switch or rotation, estimate its directional benefit as the sum of prices on removed arcs minus the sum on added arcs; combine this with energy ranking or reserve a small number of batch slots for high-benefit moves. Keep the unrestricted proposal fallback so prices guide exploration without ruling out useful moves.

3. **Allocate parent searches according to measured productivity while retaining exploration.** Track per-archive-member LP evaluations and improvements, sample productive parents more often, and reserve a fixed share of iterations for less-tested or structurally distant members. This could use the fixed LP budget more effectively without sacrificing the diverse-parent exploration associated with the leading result.

4. **Escalate rotations selectively when a parent’s search stalls.** Track unsuccessful batches per parent and, after several failures, increase the fraction of three-edge rotations touching that parent’s highest-price arcs; return to the normal switch/rotation mix after an improvement. This builds on a move type already present in the best program while avoiding the cost of applying aggressive rotations uniformly.

5. **Use small, bounded basin-change kicks during global stagnation.** After a sustained period without improving the incumbent, choose a strong but non-elite archive member and apply a few degree-preserving switches before resuming ordinary search; increase the kick size only if stagnation continues. This combines the current program’s archive diversity and adaptive kick sizing to explore new basins while preserving strong alternatives.
