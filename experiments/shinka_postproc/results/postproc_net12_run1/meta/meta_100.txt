# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

- **Implementation**: Seeds a topology pool from repaired QAOA candidates, selects diverse elite starts, and explores degree-preserving two-switches and three-cycle rotations. It prioritizes LP-priced links, ranks proposals with a surrogate energy, and evaluates valid candidates with LP calls during annealed walks with stagnation reheating.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best remains Generation 49**, scoring **0.99 with a 1.00% mean gap** in 32.32 seconds at the full 2,000-LP-call budget. Its combination of repaired/deduplicated QAOA starts, energy-ranked diversity, surrogate-screened batches, and a diverse archive remains the strongest measured result. Its specific policies—initial starts separated by topology distance ≥4, at most 30 starts, and batches selected for both energy and diversity—provide evidence that quality and coverage work well together.
- **The new neighborhood-search variants confirm the value of diverse initialization and archived search, but do not surpass Generation 49.** Generation 98, *Dual-Guided Diverse Network Local Search*, reached a **1.41% gap** (score **0.99**, 22.28 s); Generation 96, *Diverse Budgeted Network Neighborhood Search*, reached **1.48%** (score **0.99**, 36.98 s). Both use repaired QAOA pools and diverse, surrogate-ranked LP evaluations. Generation 99’s *Budgeted Diverse Topology Local Search* reached a **1.64% gap** (score **0.98**, ~23 s), likewise supporting this pattern while trailing on quality.
- **Dual guidance is useful as part of a broader search, not a consistent quality guarantee.** Generation 92 previously achieved a **1.49% gap** with diverse restarts and dual guidance, while Generation 97’s *Dual-Guided Network Neighborhood Search* reached **2.20%**. The current best also supports dual-guided moves, but its stronger result is associated with combining them with a carefully diversified archive and bounded batch screening rather than relying on move guidance alone.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **The current best’s QAOA-start advantage is aggregate, not uniform across seeds.** It averaged best-known/best **0.9901** versus **0.9730** for random starts, but on network12_seed0 random starts were slightly better: **278,786 (0.84% gap)** versus QAOA’s **279,077 (0.94%)**. QAOA was substantially better on seeds 1 and 2, so the aggregate advantage is driven by those runs rather than a per-seed guarantee.
- **New methods that add more move types or adaptation still did not beat the current best’s gap.** Generation 94’s dual-guided QAOA topology search had a **1.86% mean gap** and a weak seed at **4.01%**; Generation 97 had a **2.20% gap** despite using dual-price allocation and archive branching. Rotations, kicks, and dual-price move ranking are therefore not sufficient on their own to close the gap.
- **Spending all 2,000 LP calls is not by itself predictive of solution quality.** The current best, Generations 94–99, and prior valid searches generally used the full budget, yet their gaps varied from **1.00%** (Generation 49) to **2.20%** (Generation 97), and earlier Generation 88 reached **5.47%**. Full-budget use measures effort, not search effectiveness.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best uses explicit quality–diversity selection at both initialization and archive maintenance.** `choose_starts` sorts repaired candidates by surrogate energy, first selecting networks at least four arc-set edits apart, then filling remaining slots with the best unselected candidates (up to 30). The archive similarly preserves up to 24 separated solutions before filling to 32 by LP score. This helps retain strong but structurally distinct search regions.
- **Its LP batch screening balances local surrogate quality with coverage.** `screen_batch` removes already-seen and cached networks, ranks fresh proposals by energy change from the parent, selects up to six pairwise-separated candidates (distance ≥2), then fills a batch up to nine. This is a concrete quality/diversity policy accompanying the best observed **1.00% gap**, and contrasts with the less successful results of variants that also used surrogate ranking but different selection/adaptation schemes.
- **The search combines focused dual-guided moves with non-focused fallbacks and bounded recovery.** `make_moves` exhaustively considers two-switches involving positive-price links (or high-load links when prices are unavailable), adds 70 sampled non-focused switches, stall-adaptive rotations (up to 350 trials), and multi-switch kicks. If screening yields nothing, it tries another archive parent and then bounded random kicks; a `rounds > 10000` guard prevents an unbounded no-progress loop. This combines exploitation, escape routes, and a termination safeguard.
- **Caching and budget checks are paired with progress handling.** The current best tracks evaluated networks in `seen`, excludes cached proposals, respects `lp_calls_left`, and updates per-parent stall counts to increase rotation sampling after repeated failures. This is more robust than Generation 86’s no-progress retry behavior, which timed out despite budget checks; the latter shows why caches and validity checks alone do not bound runtime.

Its archive similarly keeps up to 24 structurally separated networks before filling to 32 by LP score.
- **Its LP batches use a concrete diversity-aware screening policy.** `screen_batch` excludes seen and cached candidates, ranks fresh proposals by energy change from the parent, selects up to six candidates separated by topology distance of at least two, then fills the batch up to nine. This is part of the current best’s quality–coverage design.
- **The neighborhood combines focused exploration with fallbacks.** The current best exhaustively considers two-switches involving positive-price links (or high-load links when prices are unavailable), adds 70 sampled non-focused switches, uses stall-adaptive rotations, and generates multi-switch kicks. If a batch is empty, it tries another archive parent and then bounded random kicks.
- **Caching is paired with explicit progress and budget controls.** The current best tracks evaluated networks locally, avoids cached proposals, checks the LP budget, and records parent stalls to adapt rotation sampling. These controls help distinguish its bounded search from Generation 86’s ineffective retry loop; caching and validity checks alone did not prevent that timeout.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 49 is still the benchmark:** **1.00% gap**, score **0.99**, 2,000 LP calls, 32.32 seconds. The closest new results are Generation 98 at **1.41%** (22.28 s) and Generation 96 at **1.48%** (36.98 s), both also scoring 0.99.
- **The other new programs trade runtime and gap without surpassing the benchmark.** Generation 99 achieved **1.64%** in about 23 seconds; Generation 94, **1.86%** in 37.23 seconds; and Generation 97, **2.20%** in 14.12 seconds. All used 2,000 LP calls, so neither runtime nor full-budget use alone tracks solution quality.
- **The current best’s QAOA results are strong overall but variable by seed.** Its gaps were **0.94%, 1.67%, and 0.40%**, compared with random-start gaps of **0.84%, 3.45%, and 4.10%**. The best QAOA result was **277,572** on seed 2, against a known best of **276,474**.
- **The new evaluations echo the aggregate QAOA advantage while showing seed variability.** Generation 96 reports best-known/best ratios of **0.9855 vs 0.9750** for QAOA and random starts; Generation 99 reports **0.9839 vs 0.9775**; and Generation 98 reports a **0.02** advantage for QAOA. Generation 97’s advantage was smaller (**0.9786 vs 0.9756**) and QAOA underperformed on two seeds. This is consistent with the current best’s aggregate advantage despite its seed-0 reversal.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reserve a small random-start quota in the initial pool.** Repair, deduplicate, and energy-rank QAOA and random candidates together, but reserve a few of the 30 start slots for the best random candidates. QAOA starts win strongly in aggregate, but lost to random starts on seed 0; a small quota could hedge against that seed-dependent weakness while preserving the successful energy-and-distance selection policy.

2. **Make batch diversity adaptive to the ranked candidates.** Keep the current batch cap of nine and energy ranking, but measure how clustered the top moves are before selecting them: enforce stronger distance separation when the top candidates are near-duplicates, and take more of the best-energy moves when they are already diverse. This tests whether the current quality–diversity balance can be improved without spending additional LP calls.

3. **Use bounded improvement history to guide archive-parent selection.** Track each parent’s recent LP improvement rate and modestly favor productive parents, while reserving a fixed share of selections for distant or rarely visited archive members. This adds targeted exploitation while retaining the diverse archive associated with the best measured result.

4. **Adapt move-generation effort by recent move-type yield.** Record whether focused switches, non-focused switches, rotations, or multi-switch kicks produce LP improvements, then shift proposal effort toward productive types while keeping a minimum allocation for each and a fixed cap on total proposals. This tests whether the current broad neighborhood can use its budget more effectively without relying on a single move type—a limitation suggested by the weaker results of variants that added guidance or move types without matching the benchmark.

5. **Direct stagnation kicks toward underexplored archive regions.** After repeated failures for a parent, choose a structurally distant or rarely selected archive member as the kick source, and generate a small bounded set of degree-preserving perturbations that mix unrestricted switches with the existing price/load guidance. Screen these candidates using the current energy-and-distance policy, preserving the existing progress guard and bounded retries.
