# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 20 (“Diverse Energy- and Routing-Guided Search”), remains the aggregate leader:** score **0.99**, mean gap **1.04%**, and best gap **−0.61%**. Its protected mix of energy, routing, learned predictions, novelty, and uncertainty selection is more reliable overall than the newer programs, all of which had mean gaps of **1.25% or higher**.
- **A diverse LP-selection portfolio consistently produces strong results.** Generation 20 combines energy- and routing-ranked candidates with learned predictions, uncertainty, and novelty. Generation 44 (“Diverse Archive Routing-Guided Search”) uses a similar combination and reached **0.99 / 1.25%**, with a **−0.30%** best gap. This is the strongest newer result and supports the value of combining independent rankings rather than relying on one screening signal.
- **Degree-preserving moves and a separated archive support effective topology exploration.** Generation 20’s two-switches, three-arc rotations, larger perturbations, and diversity filters yielded mean gaps below **1.5% at every size**. Generation 44 likewise uses focused two- and three-arc switches and a diverse archive; it achieved particularly strong n=14 results (**0.85% mean gap**), though its overall mean remained above Generation 20’s.
- **Routing proxies are most effective as a complement to energy and LP feedback.** Generation 20’s shortest-path routing features add demand-weighted distance and link-load information to surrogate energy and LP-informed search. Generation 44’s routing-guided screening also performed well. Earlier Generation 41, with energy, routing features, and an online ridge predictor, reached **0.98 / 1.58%**, showing promise but not matching the current best.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **The newer surrogate-screened variants did not improve on Generation 20’s aggregate results.** Generation 40 (“Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search”) scored **0.98 / 1.88%**, while Generation 41 scored **0.98 / 1.58%**. Both spent the full **2,000 LP calls**, so their weaker mean gaps reflect candidate search and selection quality rather than a reduced LP budget.
- **Dual/load guidance and archive relinking are not sufficient on their own to ensure consistent quality.** Generation 43 (“Adaptive Degree-Preserving Network Search”) scored **0.97 / 2.70%**; its mean gap worsened from **2.01% at n=13** to **3.54% at n=16**, despite using LP dual prices and loads alongside routing and energy signals. This supports the earlier finding that LP bottleneck signals work best within a broader selection portfolio.
- **A strong best run does not imply a strong average.** Generation 40 found a **−0.85%** best gap but had a **1.88%** mean; Generation 43 found a **0.80%** best gap but had a **2.70%** mean. Generation 20 is stronger on both dimensions: **−0.61% best** and the lowest mean gap, **1.04%**.
- **Implementation failures can erase the value of an otherwise promising design.** Generation 42 (“LP-Certified Adaptive Network Search”) scored **0.0** because `improve` used `math` without importing it, raising `NameError` before a valid result was produced.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best’s feature portfolio ties complementary signals to a guarded LP batch.** Its nine routing features include demand-weighted distance, peak and upper-tail link loads, load RMS, source/target work, direct traffic, and unreachable pairs, alongside Hamiltonian energy. It then reserves selection capacity for energy, routing, learned ranking, novelty, and uncertainty. This structure is consistent with its **1.04% mean gap**, compared with Generation 41’s **1.58%** using a related but differently allocated screen.
- **Diversity is applied at multiple points, not just in the archive.** Generation 20 filters near-duplicate repaired starts, maintains separated archive members, and prevents near-identical candidates from filling LP batches. Generation 44 also uses a diverse archive and reported a strong **0.99 score**, but its **1.25% mean gap** shows that diversity is valuable in combination with effective ranking, rather than sufficient by itself.
- **The current best combines inexpensive routing calculations with LP certification.** Batched all-pairs shortest paths and equal splitting across shortest paths provide cheap routing estimates; only selected candidates receive `tools.lp_detail` evaluation. The online ridge predictor is calibrated within the run and used with uncertainty, rather than replacing the other signals. Across the evaluated programs, all reported full-budget searches used **2,000 LP calls**, so the current best’s advantage is attributable to search and allocation quality, not more calls.
- **LP feedback is used as a move-generation signal, not the sole decision rule.** The current best targets positive-price or high-load arcs when generating switches, but candidate selection still draws from energy, routing, learned predictions, novelty, and uncertainty. Generation 43’s weaker **0.97 / 2.70%** result with dual/load guidance and adaptive allocation reinforces this distinction.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt LP-batch quotas using per-source improvement feedback.** Track whether candidates selected by energy, routing, learned prediction, novelty, and uncertainty improve the incumbent, then modestly increase the next-round quota for productive sources while preserving a minimum slot for each. This builds on the current program’s protected multi-signal portfolio without letting any single ranking dominate.

2. **Make hot-arc removal an explicit move-generation objective.** For positive-price arcs—or the highest-load arcs when prices are unavailable—prioritize valid two-switches and three-arc rotations that remove the target arc, while retaining untargeted moves for exploration. LP bottleneck signals have helped guide the current search, but Generation 43’s weaker result suggests using them as one focused move source rather than as the primary selection rule.

3. **Add tie-robust routing estimates to candidate selection.** For shortlisted candidates, compute routing loads under several deterministic shortest-path tie-breakings or splits, and rank a candidate using a high-percentile or worst-case load summary alongside the existing equal-split proxy. Treat this as an additional ranking signal, not a filter, to preserve the current mix of energy, learned prediction, novelty, and uncertainty.

4. **Keep archive diversity across both quality and feature groups.** Reserve archive capacity for LP elites, low-energy candidates, routing-proxy leaders, and structurally novel networks, then sample parents across those groups, especially after stalls. The strong results from the current program and Generation 44 support separated archives, while this extension reduces the risk that LP score alone narrows the search too early.

5. **Adapt perturbation size to progress and instance size.** After repeated non-improving rounds, increase multi-switch kicks or steps toward distant archive members; after an improvement, favor local switches and rotations, including hot-arc moves. Test a modestly larger exploration allowance at n=13 and n=15, where the current mean gaps are weakest, while preserving the existing move mix at n=14 and n=16.
