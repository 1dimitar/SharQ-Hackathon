# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Combining surrogate energy with demand-aware routing signals is the clearest success.** The current best program achieves a **0.99 combined score and 1.50% mean gap**. It screens candidate moves using QUBO energy plus batched shortest-path routing features, then uses an online ridge predictor to rank candidates for LP evaluation. This outperforms the earlier **Diverse Surrogate-Guided Network Search** (0.98, 2.32% mean gap) and **Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search** (0.98, 2.10%).
- **Diversified starting points and a diverse archive help retain strong results across instances.** The current program interleaves energy-ranked and demand-based initial candidates, filters near-duplicate topologies, and maintains an elite/diverse archive. Its mean gaps are relatively even across sizes: **1.98% at n=13, 1.29% at n=14, 1.33% at n=15, and 1.39% at n=16**. This is substantially more balanced than Generation 3’s dual-guided search, whose mean gap was 3.19% and whose n=15 results were weakest.
- **Multiple topology operators contribute useful search variety when combined with evaluation screening.** The current program uses degree-preserving 2-switches, three-link rotations, multi-switch perturbations, and relinking. This resembles successful earlier diverse-search approaches, while its routing and learned screens help choose which generated candidates merit LP calls. The current program reaches a best gap of **−0.12%**, improving slightly on the reference.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Dual guidance alone did not reliably improve candidate quality.** Generation 3’s **Dual-Guided Diverse Topology Search** used LP-dual congestion bounds and critical-link prioritization, yet scored **0.97 with a 3.19% mean gap**; n=15 reached a 3.81% mean gap and one run had a 6.29% gap. In contrast, the current program’s richer combination of surrogate, routing, learned, and novelty signals scores 0.99 with a 1.50% mean gap.
- **Diversity and relinking without sufficiently effective evaluation prioritization can leave material run-to-run errors.** Generation 2’s portfolio-relink program scored **0.98** with a **2.10% mean gap**, and some runs exceeded 3% despite using 2,000 LP calls. The current program also uses relinking and diversity, but its per-candidate routing features and online predictor accompany these mechanisms and correlate with better aggregate results.
- **Exhausting the LP budget is not by itself a performance advantage.** All summarized programs, including the current best, use the full **2,000 LP calls**. Yet scores range from **0.97 / 3.19% mean gap** (Generation 3) to **0.99 / 1.50%** (current best), indicating that candidate ranking and evaluation allocation—not call count alone—distinguish outcomes.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current program makes inexpensive candidate features serve as a staged filter before costly LP evaluation.** `routing_features` batches all-pairs shortest paths and estimates equal shortest-path flow, then extracts total work, peak and upper-tail link loads, load RMS, source/target work maxima, direct-demand usage, and unreachable pairs. These features complement `tools.energy`; an online ridge model predicts normalized LP score once enough samples are available. The program’s 0.99 score and 1.50% mean gap are the strongest reported aggregate results.
- **Candidate selection preserves several kinds of promise rather than relying on one ranking.** The program combines learned-score, energy, and demand-proxy orderings, applies structural separation, and reserves batch capacity for novelty and random exploration. This is a more explicit balance of exploitation and exploration than a single energy ranking, and is consistent with its strong, relatively even size-level results.
- **Search effort adapts to feedback from evaluated candidates.** The program continues from a parent when a child improves, tracks parent visits and stalls, increases rotation trials for stalled parents, and uses LP prices or loads to mark bottleneck-touching moves. These mechanisms make operator generation responsive to search history and evaluated congestion information.
- **Runtime remains size-sensitive despite strong quality.** The current best averages **35.14 seconds** overall, with runs around **18.5–22.5 seconds at n=13** and **40.6–59.1 seconds at n=16**. Batched routing screening is computationally useful for ranking, but the observed runtime growth shows that screening and search still become more costly as network size increases.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best leads the reported programs on aggregate score and mean gap.** It records **0.99 combined score and 1.50% mean gap**, versus Generation 1’s **0.99 and 1.50%** (also strong, with a 0.99 best-per-Hamiltonian score) and the other summarized results: Generation 0 at **0.98 / 2.32%**, Generation 2 at **0.98 / 2.10%**, Generation 3 at **0.97 / 3.19%**, and Generation 5 at **0.98 / 2.27%**. Its best gap of **−0.12%** is also the strongest stated best gap among these programs.
- **The current program’s strongest average size is n=14, while n=13 remains its relative weak point.** Mean gaps are **1.29% at n=14** and **1.98% at n=13**; n=15 and n=16 are close at **1.33%** and **1.39%**. Across all 16 pool/seed runs, results range from −0.12% to +2.87%, so the aggregate improvement does not eliminate seed and pool variability.
- **Hamiltonian-level results are strong but not uniformly near the reference.** Best-per-Hamiltonian gaps are **+1.46% for r2g181** and **+0.82% for r3g128**. This agrees with the overall strong score while showing a pool-dependent spread; for example, r2g181’s n=13 runs average 2.63% and r3g128’s n=13 runs average 1.71%.
- **Quality gains do not come from fewer LP calls, and runtime varies substantially by size and pool.** Every reported current-program run uses all **2,000 LP calls**. Runtime reaches about **59 seconds at n=16** for r2g181 but is about **41–43 seconds** for the corresponding r3g128 runs, while both use the same budget. This reinforces that search efficiency and instance/pool characteristics affect elapsed time independently of LP-call count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Improve routing-feature accuracy with a small set of alternate feasible routings.** The current screen uses equal splitting over shortest paths, which may mis-rank topologies whose congestion depends on how shortest-path flow is distributed. For each shortlisted topology, add one or two cheap variants—such as deterministic tie-breaking or lightly perturbed path splitting—and rank by a robust aggregate of their peak and upper-tail loads; retain the existing energy and learned signals so this extends rather than replaces the successful hybrid screen.

2. **Make the online predictor uncertainty-aware and use uncertainty for exploration.** Keep the ridge model, but estimate prediction uncertainty (for example, from residuals or ensemble fits) and reserve a small number of LP evaluations for candidates with high uncertainty as well as candidates with low predicted score. The current program already balances learned, energy, proxy, and novelty rankings; uncertainty-directed exploration could discover promising regions the model has not yet learned, especially given the remaining seed-to-seed variability.

3. **Adapt the feature model and selection policy to the observed weak cases.** The n=13 mean gap is 1.98%, and results vary by Hamiltonian even at the same size, so use within-run validation or regularized feature ablation to down-weight routing features that fail to predict LP scores on that pool. Keep QUBO energy and demand proxy as independent selection paths, preventing a poorly calibrated predictor from dominating candidate choice.

4. **Use bottleneck information to bias move generation more selectively.** The program already marks moves touching LP-priced or heavily loaded links, but only biases sampling of the non-shortlisted moves. Increase the share of bottleneck-touching moves in the routing shortlist and generate targeted 2-switches or rotations that remove hot links while preserving degree; compare their cheap routing features against ordinary moves before spending LP calls. This builds on the existing dual/load feedback without relying on dual guidance alone, which performed poorly in earlier results.

5. **Control runtime by allocating screening effort according to instance size and search feedback.** Routing screening grows more expensive at n=16, while the LP budget is still fully used; cap or reduce the shortlist’s routing work on larger instances and redirect saved time toward more diverse parent selection or promising candidates. Conversely, when a parent repeatedly stalls, increase perturbation and relinking diversity rather than only increasing rotation trials, then return to the usual screening budget when improvements resume.
