# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best program, Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search, reinforces that multi-signal screening is more valuable than topology diversity alone.** It combines surrogate energy, a three-variant shortest-path congestion estimate, LP prices/loads, and structurally diverse batch selection. It achieved **0.99 combined score and 1.41% mean gap**, improving on the previous best’s cited **1.50% gap** and beating the other current evaluations: Generation 11 at **0.97 / 3.58%**, Generation 12 at **0.97 / 2.71%**, and Generation 16 at **0.97 / 2.99%**.
- **Mixing complementary degree-preserving operators with targeted candidate ranking remains effective.** The current best uses 2-switches, three-link rotations, random-walk kicks, and path relinking; it selects LP trials across energy, routing, bottleneck exposure, operator type, and novelty. This is consistent with the earlier finding that a screened operator portfolio outperforms the less selectively ranked Generation 9 genetic search (**0.95 / 5.14%**).
- **Preserving exploration while exploiting strong candidates is associated with robust aggregate quality.** The current best starts from energy-ranked but structurally separated repaired candidates, then retains an archive that balances LP score and distance. Its best individual run beat the reference by **0.61%**, while its size-specific mean gaps stayed between **1.01% and 1.65%**. Earlier insights likewise found that separate batch capacity for novelty and random exploration helped the strongest program avoid overcommitting to a single ranking.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Diverse search without sufficiently strong candidate prioritization remains weaker.** Generation 11’s QAOA post-processing search used a diverse archive, topology moves, dual/load pressure, and an uncertainty-aware ridge predictor, but achieved only **0.97 / 3.58%**. Generation 12’s surrogate-screened archive search reached **0.97 / 2.71%**, still behind the current best’s **1.41% gap**. Their results support the earlier observation that diversity and move variety do not compensate for weaker or less effective screening.
- **A full LP budget does not ensure competitive quality.** The current best and Generations 11, 12, and 16 each used **2,000 LP calls**, yet their mean gaps ranged from **1.41% to 3.58%**. The earlier Generation 9 result was weaker still at **5.14%** with the same budget. Evaluation prioritization, rather than call count alone, distinguishes these outcomes.
- **Validation failures can erase the value of an otherwise extensive search design.** Generation 13’s adaptive routing-screened search received a **0.0 score** because its wrapper contained the forbidden `signal` construct. Its proposed predictors and archive search therefore provide no valid performance evidence.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best layers independent candidate signals before expensive LP evaluation.** `select_batch` first ranks fresh proposals by surrogate energy, then evaluates a routing estimate on an energy-limited pool. It also allocates slots to move operators and ranks by dual/load exposure, while reserving room for novelty. This concrete combination accompanies the best reported aggregate result: **0.99 score, 1.41% mean gap**.
- **Its routing screen explicitly accounts for shortest-path tie-breaking uncertainty.** `routing_estimate` computes distances to each destination and simulates three deterministic shortest-path routings, combining the worst peak load with the mean. This adds a congestion signal beyond surrogate energy and is a notable distinction of the current best’s implementation.
- **The search combines targeted moves and escape mechanisms while retaining archive diversity.** `proposals_for` uses hot-link-guided 2-switches, random switches, three-link rotations, short random walks, and target relinking; `archive` preserves structurally distinct high-performing networks before filling remaining places by LP rank. The design avoids relying exclusively on either local bottleneck moves or random exploration.
- **LP information guides proposals as well as selection.** The current best uses positive LP prices (or highly loaded links when prices are absent) to identify hot links, measures hot-link changes and estimated dual gain for proposals, and records parent stalls to bias parent selection. This is consistent with previous findings that dual/load signals work best as part of a broader ranking portfolio, not in isolation.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best is the strongest evaluated program in this comparison.** Its **0.99 combined score / 1.41% mean gap** improves on the previous best’s cited **1.50% gap** and substantially exceeds the current alternatives: Generation 12 (**2.71%**), Generation 16 (**2.99%**), and Generation 11 (**3.58%**). It also exceeds the previous-insight benchmarks for Generation 6 (**1.74%**) and Generations 4 and 7 (**2.93% and 2.80%**).
- **Its quality is comparatively balanced across sizes, with n=14 strongest.** Mean gaps are **1.62% at n=13, 1.01% at n=14, 1.65% at n=15, and 1.37% at n=16**. This is notably tighter than Generation 11’s **2.81% at n=14** and **3.88–3.96% at n=15–16**, and Generation 16’s **3.37% at n=15**.
- **Pool and seed variation remains material despite the leading mean.** The current best’s per-run results range from **−0.61% to +3.57%**. The best-per-Hamiltonian results are **+0.79% for r2g181** and **+0.93% for r3g128**, but individual n=15 runs range from **0.00% to +3.57%**. Thus, its strong aggregate performance does not imply uniformly near-reference outcomes across pools and seeds.
- **The quality advantage is not explained by a lower LP budget, and runtime is not uniformly lower.** The current best uses **2,000 LP calls** and averages **47.86 seconds**, compared with Generation 11’s **40.21 seconds** and Generation 12’s reported **21.9–42.9 seconds**. Its better gap despite the same call count—and longer average runtime than some alternatives—aligns with prior evidence that candidate ranking and allocation, rather than budget consumption alone, drive solution quality.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add uncertainty-aware exploration to batch selection.** Fit a small ridge model to previously LP-evaluated candidates using energy, routing peak, dual/load exposure, and structural features; use its predicted score to rank proposals and reserve one batch slot for a high-uncertainty candidate whose predicted score is still competitive. Keep the existing energy-, routing-, operator-, and novelty-based slots so this supplements rather than replaces the multi-signal screening associated with the best result.

2. **Make routing screening more robust to path-choice variation.** Extend `routing_estimate` with one or two inexpensive alternatives to its three deterministic shortest-path routings, such as demand splitting across tied shortest paths or randomized tie-breaking, and rank by a robust peak or upper-tail load across variants. Compare candidates using this signal alongside energy rather than letting the routing proxy dominate; the current program’s explicit tie-breaking ensemble makes this a targeted test of a successful feature.

3. **Generate degree-preserving moves that deliberately remove LP bottlenecks.** Use positive prices or high loads to construct switches and rotations that remove hot arcs, then score the resulting candidates with the existing energy and routing screens before spending LP calls. Preserve ordinary random switches and kicks in the same proposal portfolio, since the best program benefits from combining dual guidance with broader exploration rather than relying on bottleneck signals alone.

4. **Adapt the search response to stalls and ranking reliability.** When a parent repeatedly fails to improve, increase the share of larger random-walk kicks and target relinking from structurally distant archive members; after an improvement, return to the usual local-move mix. Track how well energy and routing rankings predict LP outcomes, and shift some batch slots toward the more reliable screen while retaining minimum capacity for each signal and novelty.

5. **Improve archive selection and parent choice to address run variability.** The current archive preserves strong, distinct networks, but its fixed distance threshold and rank-first fill may miss useful intermediate regions; test an archive that reserves explicit slots for top LP score, top surrogate/routing scores, and structural novelty. Choose parents across distinct archive regions more often after stalls, while keeping strong LP-ranked parents available, to target the substantial seed and pool variation without imposing a fixed bias toward either Hamiltonian.
