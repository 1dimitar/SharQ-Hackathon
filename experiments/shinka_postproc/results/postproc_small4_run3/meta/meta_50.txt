# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 20 (“Diverse Energy- and Routing-Guided Search”), remains the clear aggregate leader:** combined score **0.99**, mean gap **1.04%**, and best gap **−0.61%**. Its combination of Hamiltonian energy, inexpensive routing features, within-run LP prediction, novelty, and uncertainty-based selection outperforms the new programs, whose mean gaps range from **1.53% to 2.03%** among the competitive full-budget variants.
- **A protected, diversified LP-selection portfolio is the strongest recurring pattern.** Generation 20 reserves batch capacity for energy, routing, learned ranking, novelty, and uncertainty. Generation 45’s related LP-guided diverse archive search achieved **0.98 / 1.54%**, while Generation 51’s diversified surrogate-guided search reached **0.98 / 1.53%** and a **−0.61%** best run. Their negative best gaps show that this portfolio can find benchmark-beating candidates, but their higher means show that consistently selecting strong candidates matters as much as finding an exceptional one.
- **Complementary routing and energy signals are more effective than a single screening signal.** Generation 20 uses demand-weighted distance, peak and upper-tail loads, load RMS, source/target work, direct traffic, and unreachable-pair counts alongside energy. Generation 47’s energy/routing/bottleneck/predictor combination achieved **0.98 / 1.74%**; Generation 46’s surrogate plus shortest-path congestion screening reached **0.98 / 2.03%**. These results reinforce the value of combining signals, while Generation 20’s better mean indicates that signal allocation and selection quality are also important.
- **Degree-preserving switches, rotations, archive diversity, and relinking support effective exploration when paired with strong evaluation.** Generation 20 combines these search moves with diversity filters and LP feedback. Generation 45’s two-/three-edge switches, dual-focused moves, and relinking reached **0.98 / 1.54%**. By contrast, the same broad move families did not guarantee strong results in Generation 48, underscoring that move generation works best as part of a well-calibrated selection portfolio.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **The new full-budget programs did not displace Generation 20.** Generation 45 scored **0.98 / 1.54%**, Generation 47 **0.98 / 1.74%**, Generation 46 **0.98 / 2.03%**, and Generation 51 **0.98 / 1.53%**, compared with Generation 20’s **0.99 / 1.04%**. Since all used **2,000 LP calls**, these differences reflect search and selection quality rather than evaluation budget.
- **Dual/load guidance or adaptive move allocation alone is not enough to ensure good average results.** Generation 45 uses dual-focused moves and achieved **1.54%** mean gap; Generation 47 adds bottleneck signals and achieved **1.74%**. The earlier Generation 43’s dual/load-guided adaptive search was weaker still at **0.97 / 2.70%**. These results support using bottleneck guidance within, rather than instead of, a broader ranking portfolio.
- **Low LP usage can coincide with poor topology quality.** Generation 48’s Adaptive Portfolio Search used an average of just **33 LP calls** and **2.61 seconds**, but scored **0.85** with a **17.21%** mean gap; its best-known gaps were **12.38–26.73%**. The feedback identifies especially weak and variable performance at n=15, indicating that inexpensive evaluation did not compensate for insufficiently effective topology exploration.
- **Exceptional best runs do not establish consistent performance.** Generation 51 attained a **−0.61%** best gap, matching Generation 20’s best, yet its mean gap was **1.53%**. Generation 45 also found **−0.30%** but averaged **1.54%**. Generation 20 paired its strong best result with the best mean (**1.04%**), demonstrating better overall reliability.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 20’s screening architecture preserves capacity for distinct kinds of candidates.** It builds a feature vector from energy plus nine routing measures, fits a regularized within-run predictor from LP results, and selects a guarded batch across energy, routing, prediction, novelty, and uncertainty. This protects exploration and reduces dependence on any one proxy; the resulting **1.04% mean gap** is better than the **1.53–2.03%** means of the newer full-budget searches.
- **Its LP feedback informs both candidate evaluation and move generation without becoming the sole decision rule.** Positive-price or high-load arcs guide switch generation, while candidate selection still includes multiple rankings and diversity. Generation 45’s dual-focused moves produced a good **1.54%** mean but did not match Generation 20, consistent with the value of using LP bottleneck signals as one component of a broader portfolio.
- **Diversity is enforced at several stages in the current best:** repaired starts are filtered for near-duplicates, the archive combines elites with separated candidates, and LP batches avoid near-identical topologies. This is paired with two-switches, three-arc rotations, perturbations, and relinking. The design is consistent with Generation 20’s stronger aggregate result; Generation 45 and Generation 51 also use diverse archives but have weaker means, so diversity alone does not account for the advantage.
- **Cheap routing estimates are used to prioritize, not replace, LP certification.** The current best batches shortest-path and equal-splitting routing calculations, then spends its LP budget on selected candidates. Its runtime of **48.61 seconds** for **2,000 calls** is competitive with Generation 45 (**46.86 seconds**) and Generation 51 (**40.17 seconds**), while delivering a better mean gap. This further points to candidate quality and LP-batch allocation, rather than call count or runtime alone, as the differentiator.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt LP-batch quotas using measured improvement by selection source.** Label each evaluated candidate by whether it came from energy, routing, learned prediction, novelty, or uncertainty selection, and track how often each source improves the incumbent. Adjust later batch quotas modestly toward productive sources while retaining at least one slot for every source; this preserves the protected portfolio that distinguishes the current best without allowing one proxy to dominate.

2. **Prioritize moves that remove LP-identified hot arcs, but keep untargeted exploration.** When generating two-switches and three-arc rotations, explicitly favor valid moves that delete positive-price arcs, falling back to high-load arcs when prices are unavailable. Allocate only part of the move pool to this targeted generation, since bottleneck guidance has helped as a complement to the current energy/routing portfolio but has not been reliable as the sole search strategy.

3. **Add tie-robust routing features for shortlisted candidates.** Evaluate shortest-path loads under a few deterministic tie-breakings, or a mixture of shortest-path splits, and add a high-percentile load statistic to the existing routing feature vector and proxy ranking. Use this as another ranking signal rather than a hard filter, retaining LP certification and the existing energy, learned, novelty, and uncertainty selections.

4. **Make archive and parent selection explicitly preserve feature-group diversity.** In addition to LP elites and structurally separated networks, reserve archive places for low-energy, routing-proxy-leading, and novel candidates; sample parents across those groups, particularly after stalls. The current best’s multi-stage diversity supports broad exploration, while this extension may prevent LP score or any single proxy from narrowing the search prematurely.

5. **Adapt perturbation strength to progress, with extra exploration at n=13 and n=15.** After repeated rounds without improvement, increase multi-switch kicks or steps toward distant archive members; after an improvement, favor local switches and hot-arc rotations. Test a modestly larger exploration allowance at n=13 and n=15, the current best’s weakest sizes, while leaving its effective search mix largely intact for n=14 and n=16.
