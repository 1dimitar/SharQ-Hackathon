# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search, remains the clear benchmark:** it scores **0.99** with a **1.41% mean gap** on 2,000 LP calls. Its combination of an energy-ranked diverse archive, topology-changing operators, and multi-signal screening outperforms the new Generation 14–17 alternatives, whose gaps range from **1.81% to 10.50%**.
- **Screening proposals with complementary signals is stronger than surrogate ranking alone.** The current best screens energy-promising proposals with a three-variant shortest-path congestion estimate, then allocates LP evaluations across operator types, dual/load exposure, and novelty. Generation 14’s dual-bound, surrogate-energy, routing-proxy, and novelty screening is the closest new result at **1.81%**, while Generations 15 and 17, with less comprehensive screening, are at **2.66% and 2.65%**.
- **A diverse archive paired with degree-preserving search moves supports quality across instance sizes.** The current best combines 2-switches, three-link rotations, random-walk kicks, and target relinking; its mean gaps stay between **1.01% and 1.65%** for n=13–16. Generation 14 uses a similar operator family and diverse archive and is also comparatively consistent, though weaker, with a **1.81%** overall gap.
- **Exploration mechanisms help when attached to quality-focused search.** In the current best, archive separation, novelty-based batch selection, and operator-stratified evaluation supplement energy and routing rankings. By contrast, Generation 18’s cross-entropy search over matchings used diverse seeds, donor networks, and uniform exploration but achieved only **0.91 score / 10.50% mean gap**. Exploration alone did not substitute for effective candidate prioritization.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Surrogate/dual screening without the current best’s full screening portfolio produced weaker results.** Generation 15, “Diverse Surrogate-Screened Network Search,” scored **0.97** with a **2.66% mean gap**, and Generation 17, “Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search,” scored **0.97** with a **2.65% mean gap**. Both use diverse archives and topology moves, but their feedback reports substantial seed or pool variation, including gaps above **4%** and a **3.62%** mean at n=15 for Generation 17.
- **Matching-based cross-entropy search was substantially less competitive despite exhausting the LP budget.** Generation 18 used **2,000 LP calls** but had a **10.50% mean gap**, worsening from **8.54% at n=13** to **12.58% at n=16**. Its elite-updated edge-preference model and matching-based sampling did not match the effectiveness of the current best’s direct degree-preserving local search and multi-signal LP screening.
- **A validation failure can nullify an otherwise elaborate search design.** Generation 19’s routing-guided variable-neighborhood search scored **0.0** because `improve` calls `math.isfinite` and other `math` functions without importing `math`. Its proposed residual predictors and routing-guided search therefore provide no valid performance evidence.
- **Using the full LP-call budget is not, by itself, a predictor of solution quality.** The current best and Generations 14–18 all used **2,000 calls**, yet mean gaps ranged from **1.41%** to **10.50%**. The difference is consistent with the importance of which candidates receive LP evaluations, rather than budget consumption alone.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s batch selection deliberately combines independent rankings.** `select_batch` first ranks fresh proposals by surrogate energy, evaluates a bounded energy-ranked pool with `routing_estimate`, reserves places for different move operators, and separately ranks by dual/load exposure before filling remaining capacity by novelty. This is the concrete implementation behind the best observed result: **0.99 score and 1.41% mean gap**.
- **Its routing screen addresses shortest-path tie choices rather than relying on one routing.** `routing_estimate` computes destination distances and simulates three deterministic shortest-path choices, then combines the worst peak load (weight 0.65) with the mean peak (weight 0.35). This supplements the energy surrogate with a congestion signal and distinguishes the current best’s screening from simpler surrogate-ranked approaches.
- **LP prices and loads inform both proposal generation and selection.** `proposals_for` identifies hot links from positive prices, falling back to highly loaded links, and prioritizes proposals by hot-link changes and estimated dual gain. `select_batch` then uses that exposure as a separate ranking signal. This integrates LP detail into search rather than using it only to score completed candidates.
- **Diversity is enforced at multiple points, while moves preserve the topology’s degree constraints.** Repaired starts and the LP-ranked archive favor networks separated by at least four differing arcs; parent selection also accounts for stalls. The move portfolio includes directed 2-switches, three-link rotations, random multi-switch walks, and target relinking. This combines structural breadth with local improvement and escape mechanisms, matching the current best’s comparatively low, size-balanced gaps.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best leads the evaluated programs on both combined score and mean gap.** Its **0.99 / 1.41%** result improves on Generation 14 (**0.98 / 1.81%**), Generations 15 and 17 (both **0.97**, with **2.66%** and **2.65%** gaps), and Generation 18 (**0.91 / 10.50%**). Generation 19 is not comparable on search quality because it failed validation and scored **0.0**.
- **The current best is balanced across sizes, with n=14 strongest.** Its size-specific gaps are **1.62% (n=13), 1.01% (n=14), 1.65% (n=15), and 1.37% (n=16)**. Generation 14 is the closest new result overall but has a wider reported range, from **1.37% at n=13** to **2.25% at n=15**; Generation 17 is notably weaker at n=15, with a **3.62%** mean gap.
- **Pool and seed variation persists even in the leading program.** The current best’s run-level feedback ranges from **−0.61% to +3.57%**: it beat the reference on two n=14 runs and reached **0.00%** on an n=15 run, but some n=13 and n=15 runs exceeded 2%. Its best-per-Hamiltonian gaps are **+0.79% for r2g181** and **+0.93% for r3g128**. Generation 15 also reports substantial seed variation, reaching a **4.18%** gap.
- **The quality advantage is not explained by fewer LP calls or consistently shorter runtime.** The current best uses **2,000 LP calls** and averages **47.86 seconds**. Generation 14 also uses 2,000 calls and is weaker at **1.81%**, while Generation 17 averages **25.96 seconds** but has a **2.65%** gap. Generation 15 averages **45.26 seconds** with a **2.66%** gap. These comparisons reinforce that evaluation allocation and candidate quality—not call count or runtime alone—track solution quality more closely.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add uncertainty-aware ranking to the existing batch selector.** Fit a lightweight ridge model on previously evaluated candidates using surrogate energy, routing peak, dual/load exposure, and structural features, then reserve one LP slot for a candidate with competitive predicted quality but high prediction uncertainty. Keep the current energy-, routing-, operator-, and novelty-based allocations intact so this augments rather than displaces the screening portfolio associated with the best result.

2. **Test a more robust routing screen without letting it dominate selection.** Extend `routing_estimate` with randomized shortest-path tie-breaking or fractional splitting across tied shortest paths, and rank candidates using a robust statistic such as a high percentile of peak loads across variants. Compare this signal alongside the existing energy and dual/load rankings; the current three-variant screen is useful evidence for routing-guided screening, but additional tie handling may better distinguish candidates whose apparent quality depends on one routing choice.

3. **Use LP bottleneck information to construct targeted topology moves.** For hot arcs identified by prices or loads, generate degree-preserving switches or rotations that specifically remove those arcs, and prioritize moves with favorable estimated dual gain before applying the existing energy and routing screens. Retain untargeted switches and random-walk kicks, since the best program’s mixed portfolio is stronger evidence than relying on bottleneck guidance alone.

4. **Adapt move scale and parent choice to measured search progress.** When a parent stalls, increase proposals from longer random-walk kicks and relinking toward structurally distant archive members; after an improvement, favor local switches around the improved network. Track operator-specific LP improvement rates and allocate a small number of additional batch slots to operators that are performing well, while preserving minimum coverage for every operator and novelty.

5. **Make archive diversity explicit across several quality signals.** Reserve archive places separately for the best LP-scored networks, the strongest energy/routing-ranked networks, and structurally novel networks rather than filling primarily by LP rank after the distance-filtered pass. Test a small range of archive separation thresholds and select parents from distinct regions more often after stalls; this targets the observed run-to-run variation while retaining the strong, diverse archive and degree-preserving search that underpin the current result.
