# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 20, “Diverse Energy- and Routing-Guided Search,” remains the strongest evaluated program:** score **0.99**, mean gap **1.04%**, and best individual gap **−0.61%**, using 2,000 LP calls. Its combination of QUBO energy, demand-based routing features, online ridge prediction, and deliberately reserved batch capacity for diversity and uncertainty is the clearest successful pattern.
- **Mixing complementary candidate rankings is more effective than relying on one screening signal.** Generation 20 selects across learned prediction, energy, routing proxy, novelty, and uncertainty. This outperforms narrower surrogate-screened approaches such as Generation 25 (**0.98**, **1.92%** mean gap), Generation 26 (**0.97**, **3.21%**), and Generation 29 (**0.98**, **2.45%**).
- **Degree-preserving local moves plus structural diversity support strong results.** Generation 20’s two-switches, three-arc rotations, and larger perturbations are paired with diverse starts and archive selection. The resulting gaps stay below **1.5% at every size**: **1.34% (n=13), 0.34% (n=14), 1.48% (n=15), and 0.99% (n=16)**.
- **Using LP information to guide both proposals and evaluation allocation is a successful complement to cheap surrogates.** Generation 20 uses LP prices or loads to identify hot arcs, while its batch policy separately protects exploration capacity. This contrasts with Generation 26’s use of dual prices in move ranking, which still achieved a weaker **3.21%** mean gap, indicating that dual guidance is most effective as part of a broader selection portfolio.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Narrower screening and archive search have not matched the current best.** Generation 25’s energy-ranked, topologically diverse archive with surrogate and shortest-path screening scored **0.98 / 1.92%**; Generation 26’s mixed archive and dual-price screening scored **0.97 / 3.21%**. Their results show that diversity and multiple move types alone do not ensure Generation 20’s quality.
- **Path relinking and link-pressure screening did not improve on the stronger multi-signal selection.** Generation 29 added pressure-guided rotations, multi-switch kicks, and relinking toward the incumbent, but scored **0.98** with a **2.45%** mean gap. It was strongest at n=13–14 (about **1.91–1.93%**) and weaker at n=15–16 (**3.11%** and **2.85%**).
- **Destroy-and-repair remains a poor alternative to valid local topology moves.** As noted in prior results, Generation 23’s destroy-and-repair search reached only **0.91** with a **9.78%** mean gap despite using 2,000 LP calls. Reconstruction and randomized refill did not produce candidates competitive with move-based, screened searches.
- **Validation failures nullify otherwise plausible algorithmic changes.** Generation 28’s routing-aware post-processor scored **0.0** because validation rejected the forbidden `signal` construct; its search performance could not be assessed.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 20’s nine-feature routing/energy representation supplies distinct signals to the online predictor.** It combines Hamiltonian energy with demand-weighted distance, largest and upper-tail link loads, load RMS, maximum source/target work, direct traffic, and unreachable pairs. The routing features come from batched APSP and equal splitting across shortest-path choices, providing a congestion-oriented screen rather than relying on energy alone.
- **The predictor is embedded in a deliberately mixed batch-selection policy.** Once it has enough LP-labeled examples, Generation 20 uses a regularized within-run ridge model and uncertainty estimates, while reserving batch choices for energy, routing proxy, novelty, and uncertainty. This avoids allowing one ranking to consume the full evaluation batch and accompanies its **1.04%** mean gap.
- **Diversity is applied at both initialization and evaluation time.** Repaired starts are interleaved between energy and demand-proxy rankings and filtered for near-duplicates; archive selection and batch selection also enforce topology separation. This supports exploration without abandoning high-quality candidates.
- **LP feedback affects where the search moves, not just which candidates are scored.** Generation 20 uses positive LP prices—or high loads as a fallback—to identify hot arcs for move generation. Other programs also use LP detail (Generation 26 uses dual prices; Generation 29 uses link pressure), but their weaker aggregate results underscore the value of combining LP guidance with the broader prediction-and-diversity portfolio.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 20 remains the best overall:** its score is **0.99** and mean gap **1.04%**, versus Generation 25 at **0.98 / 1.92%**, Generation 29 at **0.98 / 2.45%**, and Generation 26 at **0.97 / 3.21%**. All used **2,000 LP calls**, so call count does not explain the quality differences.
- **Generation 20’s advantage is especially pronounced at n=14 and remains consistent across sizes.** Its n=14 mean gap is just **0.34%**, compared with the roughly **2.73–3.45%** size-group gaps reported for Generation 26 and the **3.11%** n=15 gap for Generation 29. The current best’s largest size-group gap is **1.48% at n=15**.
- **Strong aggregate quality coexists with seed variation.** Generation 20 includes a best run at **−0.61%**, but its reported individual gaps range as high as **+2.40%** at n=13; n=15 also ranges from **+0.11% to +2.37%**. Its best per-Hamiltonian results are positive (**+0.40% for r2g181; +0.63% for r3g128**), so the negative best individual gap is not uniform across pools.
- **Runtime is not a reliable proxy for solution quality.** Generation 20 averages **48.61 seconds**, compared with Generation 25’s **115.09 seconds**, Generation 26’s **78.61 seconds**, and Generation 29’s **34.62 seconds**. The faster Generation 29 and slower Generation 25 both trail in mean gap, reinforcing that candidate screening and selection quality matter more than runtime or LP-call total alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt LP-call allocation by the recent success of each screening signal.** Track whether candidates selected by the ridge model, energy, routing proxy, novelty, or uncertainty improve the incumbent, and adjust their next-round quotas modestly while preserving a minimum slot for every signal. This extends the best program’s complementary batch portfolio without letting a temporarily strong ranking eliminate exploration.

2. **Generate degree-preserving moves that explicitly remove hot arcs.** For arcs identified by positive LP prices or high loads, pair each with compatible alternatives and propose valid two-switches or three-arc rotations that remove the flagged arc; retain the existing untargeted moves as well. LP guidance already helps select parents’ moves, and making it directly shape proposals may focus search on bottlenecks without relying on dual information alone.

3. **Make routing-based screening less dependent on shortest-path ties.** Evaluate a small number of deterministic tie-breakings or randomized shortest-path splits for shortlisted candidates, then add a high-percentile link-load feature to the routing representation. Use this as an additional ranking signal—not a hard filter—so candidates with robust congestion performance are favored while the energy, learned-prediction, and diversity signals remain active.

4. **Give the archive explicit coverage quotas for distinct quality signals.** Keep separate groups for LP elites, energy leaders, routing-proxy leaders, and structurally novel candidates, and sample parents across these groups, especially after stalls. The current distance-filtered archive and diverse starts are successful foundations; quotas can prevent the LP-ranked elite or one topology region from gradually dominating them.

5. **Adapt move scale to parent progress and network size.** After repeated non-improving rounds, increase larger perturbations or multi-step moves; after an improvement, prioritize local switches and rotations around the new incumbent. Tune the scale by size, testing extra exploration especially for n=13 and n=15, where the current program’s mean gaps are higher than at n=14 and n=16.
