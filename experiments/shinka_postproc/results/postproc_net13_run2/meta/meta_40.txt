# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, the Generation 49 Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search, remains a strong reference point.** It scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**. Its repair-and-deduplicate initialization, surrogate-ranked diverse starts, and archive search combining two-switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks continue the successful quality-plus-diversity pattern identified in earlier generations.
- **Diversity-aware archive search with several degree-preserving move types is consistently competitive.** The current best uses topology distance thresholds both when selecting up to 24 diverse starts and when maintaining an archive of up to 18 separated networks. Recent related programs scored **0.98**: Generation 35’s archive-novelty search had a **2.57% mean gap**, Generation 36’s UCB parent selection had **2.36%**, and Generation 37’s adaptive operator feedback had **2.30%**. These results reinforce the value of a varied move portfolio and an archive, although they do not exceed the previous Generation 34 result of **0.99** and **2.41% mean gap**.
- **Surrogate screening paired with explicit exploration is a recurring successful pattern.** The current best ranks proposals by surrogate-energy change adjusted for dual-price gain, evaluates up to three topology-separated high-ranked candidates, and sometimes evaluates a tail candidate. Generation 35 likewise combined surrogate quality with novelty screening, while Generation 37 adapted proposal probabilities from observed operator success. Both scored **0.98**, with best gaps of **1.31%**; the current best’s best-per-Hamiltonian score was **0.9909**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Matching-based weight updates did not transfer the archive-search results to a weaker method.** Generation 38’s Elite-Guided Weighted Matching Search used repaired QAOA candidates, candidate-frequency arc weights, and LP-scored elite updates, but scored only **0.92**, with an **11.26% mean gap** and **7.16% best gap**. It used all **2,000 LP calls** per pool, so full budget use alone did not compensate for weaker candidate quality.
- **Dual-guided local search was less robust than the current best’s broader surrogate-screened archive search.** Generation 39 scored **0.96** with a **6.19% mean gap** and gaps reaching **9.17%**, despite using LP bottleneck details, compound perturbations, and path relinking. Its weaker aggregate result suggests that bottleneck-focused guidance and LP detail are not sufficient by themselves; the current best combines price guidance with surrogate ranking, diversity, and exploratory proposals.
- **Full-budget exhaustion remains a poor proxy for convergence or pool quality.** The current best and Generations 35–39 all used **2,000 LP calls**, yet their mean gaps ranged from **2.30%** (Generation 37) to **11.26%** (Generation 38). Even the current best varied from **−0.30% to +5.12%** across pools.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best applies diversity at both initialization and archive maintenance.** It repairs, canonicalizes, and deduplicates candidates; sorts valid starts by surrogate energy; prefers starts at topology distance at least 4; and maintains a similarly separated LP-ranked archive. This is a concrete implementation of quality filtering without allowing the search to concentrate on one narrow region, and accompanies its **0.99 combined score**.
- **Its proposal pipeline balances guidance and exploration rather than relying on one move.** Two-switches and three-link rotations can focus on positive-price or high-load links, while multi-switch kicks provide larger perturbations. Candidate screening combines surrogate-energy change with a dual-price adjustment, selects up to three separated candidates, and sometimes includes a lower-ranked exploratory candidate. This broad portfolio is consistent with the stronger archive-search results relative to Generation 38’s **0.92** matching approach and Generation 39’s **0.96** dual-guided local search.
- **Adaptive operator probabilities retain an exploration floor.** The current best updates smoothed rewards and attempt counts from LP outcomes, then adds a probability floor before normalizing operator selection. This allows successful operators to receive more proposals without permanently suppressing alternatives. Generation 37 also used operator success statistics and scored **0.98**, though the reported results do not establish that adaptation alone improves on the current best or Generation 34.
- **Canonicalization, caching, and validation make the fixed LP budget usable without repeated or invalid evaluations.** The current best caches scores by canonical network, checks validity, skips externally cached networks, and consistently reaches the full **2,000-call** budget. Its mean runtime was **28.95 seconds**. The newer summaries likewise show full-budget use, but their score differences indicate that proposal quality—not budget consumption alone—is the important distinction.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a small, bounded matching-proposal operator to the existing move portfolio.** Generate degree-preserving matching-based candidates for a fixed fraction of proposals, using archive edge frequencies to bias their construction. Pass them through the same validity, cache, surrogate/dual ranking, and diversity filters as the current two-switches, rotations, and kicks; this tests the promising matching pattern without replacing the broader search that outperformed a matching-only approach.

2. **Make proposal selection explicitly aware of archive novelty.** Keep the current surrogate-energy and dual-price ranking, but reserve part of each LP batch for candidates far from the high-quality archive, rather than measuring separation only among candidates in that batch. This extends the program’s successful diversity at initialization and archive maintenance and may help reduce its substantial pool-to-pool variation.

3. **Select parents using both improvement history and exploration needs.** Track each archive member’s evaluation count and improvement magnitude, then favor productive parents while retaining a quota for rarely selected or structurally isolated members. This replaces the fixed 65% preference for the top five with feedback-driven selection, building on the successful archive search while preserving access to less-explored regions.

4. **Improve operator adaptation with outcome-based, per-operator feedback.** Record proposals generated, candidates actually evaluated, and LP improvements—including improvement size—for each move type; update proposal probabilities from these statistics while enforcing a minimum exploration share. The current adaptive scheme already has an exploration floor, and richer feedback could allocate effort more effectively without prematurely suppressing operators.

5. **Try bounded path relinking only for stalled parents.** When a parent repeatedly fails to improve, choose a distant high-quality archive member and generate a few intermediate networks through valid degree-preserving exchanges; screen them with the existing surrogate, dual-price, and diversity criteria before spending LP calls. This adds a directed escape route to the successful move-and-archive framework while avoiding reliance on bottleneck guidance alone.
