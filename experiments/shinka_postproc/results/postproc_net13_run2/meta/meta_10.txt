# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Surrogate screening combined with diverse archive search remains the strongest recurring design.** The current best, *Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search* (network13), scored **0.99** with a **2.64% mean gap** and **0.48% best gap**. It ranks candidate moves by Hamiltonian-energy change, selects a structurally varied LP batch, and searches from an archive of strong, distinct networks. This is substantially better on average than Generation 5’s basin portfolio (**6.60% mean gap**, score **0.95**) and Generation 6’s surrogate-guided search (**2.80%**, score **0.98**).
- **Combining several degree-preserving neighborhoods supports strong solutions across pools.** Network13 includes two-switches, three-link rotations, multi-switch kicks, and path relinking. Its best gaps by Hamiltonian were **0.48%–2.03%**. This is consistent with Generation 7’s two-switches, rotations, multi-switch kicks, and path relinking (**2.64% mean gap**, **0.48% best gap**) and with the earlier finding that multiple neighborhoods outperform reliance on one move type.
- **Diversity at both seeding and archive selection helps retain coverage while exploiting good networks.** Network13 chooses up to 30 surrogate-ranked starts with a topology-distance threshold, then keeps distinct archive representatives before filling with top-ranked entries. Similar diversity-aware strategies performed well in Generation 8 (**2.55% mean gap**, **1.15% best gap**) and Generation 10 (**2.53% mean gap**, **1.32% best gap**), compared with Generation 5’s less consistent basin search.
- **Stall-adaptive exploration is compatible with high solution quality.** In network13, rotation sampling grows from 70 to as many as 350 trials as a parent stalls, while kicks and path relinking provide additional variation. Generation 10 also used a diverse frontier with bottleneck-guided moves and achieved a **2.53% mean gap**. These results support adapting exploration to stalled search rather than using a fixed, narrow neighborhood.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Bottleneck or dual information alone is not a reliable search strategy.** Earlier feedback found Generation 1’s bottleneck-aware approach at **6.77% mean gap** and Generation 4’s dual-guided approach at **7.16%**, both well behind the diverse surrogate-screened designs. Generation 5 likewise prioritized LP-critical links but reached only a **6.60% mean gap** and **0.95 score**. Network13 uses critical-link information as one move-generation signal, but pairs it with random moves, surrogate ranking, and archive diversity.
- **Strong best cases do not imply consistent pool performance.** Network13’s best result was **0.48%** above the known optimum, but its pool gaps ranged from **0.48% to 5.07%**; the latter occurred on r3g028 seed 2. Generation 7 showed a similar spread, from **0.48% to 5.07%**, while Generation 6 reached individual gaps up to **4.65%**. The evaluations therefore show that these search designs can find near-optimal networks but remain sensitive to pool and seed.
- **Exhausting the LP budget does not establish convergence or small gaps.** Network13 used all **2,000 LP calls in every pool**, yet its mean gap was **2.64%** and some runs exceeded 3%. Generation 5 also exhausted the budget with a substantially worse mean gap. Full budget usage is therefore not itself evidence of consistently strong results.

## Implementation Insights
- **Network13 makes the surrogate an explicit filter before costly LP evaluation.** `screen_batch` excludes locally seen and tool-cached topologies, ranks fresh moves by energy change from the parent, selects up to six with topology separation, and fills a batch of up to nine. This combines efficient rejection of duplicates with both quality-oriented and diverse candidate selection. Its observed mean was **2.64% gap** at 2,000 calls per pool.
- **The archive balances elite quality with exploration.** `archive()` first retains up to 24 networks separated by a topology-distance threshold, then fills to 32 with the best remaining networks; `select_parent()` samples from the top six nearly half the time and from the full archive otherwise. This combines exploitation and less-greedy parent selection, matching the strong best-per-Hamiltonian results of **0.48%, 2.03%, 0.97%, and 1.86%**.
- **Move generation combines focused and unfocused exploration.** LP prices—or high loads when prices are unavailable—identify links for focused two-switches and rotations. The program also samples random two-switches, escalates rotation trials with parent stalls, generates multi-switch kicks, and relinks toward elite networks. This is broader than the critical-link-focused approaches that performed poorly on their own.
- **The implementation avoids repeated evaluations while maintaining a path to progress.** `evaluate()` records local LP scores, while screening checks both `seen` and `tools.is_cached`. If a parent has no fresh screened moves, the search tries another archive member and then a random kick, avoiding a repeated search of an exhausted neighborhood. Despite these safeguards, all runs reached the LP-call limit, so avoiding duplicate calls did not reduce total budget use in these evaluations.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best retains a top-tier combined score but is not the best on every reported gap metric.** Network13 scored **0.99**, with **2.64% mean gap**, **0.48% best gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**. It matches Generation 7’s reported combined score and best gap, but Generation 7’s mean gap was also **2.64%**; against the earlier global best figures, network13’s mean gap is higher than the previously reported **2.19%** and its best gap is slightly higher than **0.40%**. Thus it is a strong result, not a demonstrated improvement over that earlier best.
- **Generation 6 and Generations 8 and 10 had better mean gaps than network13, but weaker best gaps.** Generation 6 reported **2.80% mean / 1.46% best**, Generation 8 **2.55% / 1.15%**, and Generation 10 **2.53% / 1.32%**, versus network13’s **2.64% / 0.48%**. Network13’s standout is its best observed pool result, while the listed later generations were somewhat better on average.
- **Hamiltonian-level best results are strong, but seed-level spread remains material.** Network13’s best-per-Hamiltonian gaps range from **0.48%** on r2g181 to **2.03%** on r3g028, while individual pools span **0.48%–5.07%**. The r3g028 seed-2 result is notably worse than its seed-3 best, illustrating that pool-level variability can persist even when a Hamiltonian has a good best-of-pool result.
- **Runtime is moderate relative to earlier top-performing runs.** Network13 averaged **34.41 seconds** for 2,000 LP calls. This is faster than the previous global-best program’s reported **45.95 seconds** and Generation 10’s **38.23 seconds**, though slower than some earlier programs with weaker gaps. The evaluation supports effective search quality at this runtime, but all programs discussed still consumed the full LP budget.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt LP batch size to surrogate confidence and stagnation.** Keep the current small, diverse batches when candidate energy changes are clearly separated, but evaluate more candidates when top-ranked moves are nearly tied or the parent has stalled repeatedly. This extends the successful surrogate filter while giving uncertain neighborhoods a better chance to produce improvements.

2. **Make batch diversity archive-aware.** When selecting LP candidates, combine parent-relative energy change with absolute surrogate energy and distance from strong archived networks; increase the separation requirement when several candidates are similarly ranked. The current distance filter reduces near-duplicate evaluations, and archive-relative ranking may avoid spending calls on locally attractive moves that lead back into already explored regions.

3. **Adapt parent selection to search progress.** Retain the elite-plus-diverse archive, but reduce the probability of choosing from the top six after repeated non-improving batches and favor less-visited archive members instead. This targets the substantial seed-level spread—including the 5.07% outlier—without discarding the exploitation that supports the program’s strong best cases.

4. **Track neighborhood productivity and adjust move-generation effort.** Record whether two-switches, rotations, kicks, and path relinking yield improvements for each parent or search phase, then shift sampling toward productive move types while preserving a minimum exploration quota for each. The current combination of neighborhoods is a strength; adapting its mix could use the fixed LP budget more effectively than applying the same sampling effort regardless of results.

5. **Improve stalled-search escapes with guided path relinking and kick strength.** When a parent stalls, increase the chance of relinking toward a distant elite network and vary the number of degree-preserving switches in kicks, rather than relying primarily on more sampled rotations. These moves build on the program’s existing multi-neighborhood design and may help cross barriers that persist despite its adaptive rotation trials.
