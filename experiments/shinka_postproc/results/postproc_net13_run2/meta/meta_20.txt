# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 19’s “Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search,” confirms that surrogate screening combined with a varied archive is the strongest pattern in these evaluations.** It achieved a **0.99 combined score**, **2.52% mean gap**, **0.79% best gap**, and **0.98 mean pool score**. It selects diverse low-energy starts, searches with degree-preserving swaps, rotations, and multi-switch kicks, and prioritizes LP evaluations using surrogate scores, critical-link information, neighborhood diversity, and adaptive operator/stall statistics.
- **Multiple degree-preserving neighborhoods plus adaptive search consistently produce strong results.** Generation 18’s adaptive parent-productivity search scored **0.99** with a **0.56% best gap**; Generation 17’s adaptive relinking search scored **0.98** with a **1.37% best gap**; and Generation 19 retained a strong **0.79% best gap** across its tested Hamiltonians. Swaps, rotations, and kicks provide more coverage than a single move type.
- **Diversity is effective when paired with quality ranking.** Generation 19’s starts and archive balance low surrogate energy with topology separation. This fits the earlier Generation 14 result (**0.99 combined score**, **−0.30% best gap**) and the Generation 18 result: both show that strong individual solutions can emerge from searches that preserve distinct network regions rather than keeping only the current elites.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Matching-model sampling was substantially weaker than archive-based local search.** Generation 16’s Adaptive Weighted-Matching Search scored **0.90** with a **13.07% mean gap** and **8.15% best gap**, versus Generation 19’s **0.99**, **2.52%**, and **0.79%**. Its smoothed edge-frequency model and randomized perfect matchings did not find competitive topologies, despite using the full LP budget.
- **Exhausting all LP calls did not ensure consistent convergence.** Generations 15–19 all used **2,000 LP calls per pool**, yet Generation 19’s pool gaps ranged from **0.79% to 4.12%**, and Generation 17’s best gap was **1.37%** while its weakest Hamiltonian reached **2.69%**. Budget use alone therefore does not explain solution quality.
- **Strong headline and best-case metrics conceal pool sensitivity.** Generation 19’s best-per-Hamiltonian gaps were **0.79–1.46%**, but individual pools reached **4.12%**; Generation 18 also had a pool gap as high as **4.53%**. The archive-based approach is reliably strong at the Hamiltonian-best level, but outcomes still vary by pool and seed.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 19’s search uses a concrete quality-and-diversity pipeline:** repair and deduplicate candidate networks, rank starts by `tools.energy`, enforce topology distance when selecting up to 30 starts, and retain up to 24 separated archive members before filling the archive to 32 with top-scoring networks. This preserves distinct search regions while keeping high-quality candidates.
- **LP calls are selected through surrogate-ranked, diversity-aware batches rather than evaluating every generated move.** `screen_batch` filters locally seen and cached networks, ranks fresh moves by energy change from the parent, chooses separated high-ranked moves, and then fills the batch. Batches grow from a nominal 9 to as many as 24 when surrogate rankings are ambiguous or parents stall. This accompanies Generation 19’s approximately **21.39-second** average runtime and strong score.
- **The neighborhood portfolio combines guided and unguided exploration.** `make_moves` exhaustively tests two-switches involving critical links, adds random two-switches, samples rotations, and generates multi-switch kicks. Operator success rates adjust sampling effort, while stall counts increase rotation trials and LP batch sizes. This embodies the earlier finding that critical-link information is more effective as one signal among broader search mechanisms than as the sole focus.
- **Adaptive parent selection balances exploitation with exploration.** The current program selects from the top six archive members **48%** of the time and otherwise samples from the full archive; unsuccessful parents accrue stalls and receive broader exploration. This complements Generation 18’s productivity-based parent selection and Generation 17’s adaptive neighborhood escalation, both of which also achieved **0.98–0.99** combined scores.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 19 is the current strongest overall evaluated program, but not the best on every individual metric.** Its **0.99 combined score**, **2.52% mean gap**, **0.79% best gap**, **0.98 mean pool score**, and **21.39-second** runtime are strong. Generation 18 also scored **0.99** and had a better best gap (**0.56%**), while Generation 17 had a lower mean gap (**2.23%**) and a **1.37% best gap**, but took **29.64 seconds**. Thus, Generation 19’s result combines a near-best best case with a relatively fast runtime, rather than leading every gap metric.
- **The current program substantially outperforms the matching-based alternative.** Generation 16’s **0.90** score and **13.07% mean gap** are far worse than Generation 19’s **0.99** and **2.52%**, despite both consuming the full **2,000-call** budget. This supports the advantage of surrogate-screened archive search over relying on a learned matching-sampling model in these evaluations.
- **Across the surrogate-search generations, strong aggregate scores coexist with meaningful variation.** Generation 15 scored **0.98** with a **2.37% mean gap**; Generation 17 scored **0.98** with a **2.23% mean gap**; Generation 18 and Generation 19 scored **0.99** with mean gaps of **2.50%** and **2.52%**, respectively. Generation 19’s observed pool range (**0.79–4.12%**) demonstrates that a strong combined score and good best-per-Hamiltonian results do not eliminate seed-level variability.
- **Generation 19’s best-per-Hamiltonian results are consistently close to reference values, though none beat them.** Its best gaps were **1.46%** on r2g181, **1.37%** on r3g028, **0.79%** on r3g109, and **1.06%** on r3g128. This is a robust Hamiltonian-level profile, contrasting with earlier Generation 14’s exceptional **−0.30%** best gap but wider overall pool range (down to **+4.83%**).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make LP batch selection aware of the whole archive.** Keep the current parent-relative surrogate ranking, but add a novelty score based on each candidate’s distance from the best archived networks. Select a mix of top-ranked moves and candidates that explore underrepresented regions, so LP calls do not repeatedly confirm nearby variants.

2. **Select parents using productivity and exposure, not only archive rank.** Track each parent’s visit count, LP evaluations, and improvements; reduce selection probability for repeatedly stalled parents while reserving chances for underexplored and recently productive members. This extends the current elite-versus-full-archive sampling and targets the seed-to-seed variability that remains despite strong aggregate scores.

3. **Allocate LP calls and move generation by operator performance.** Track the exact LP improvement rate and improvement magnitude for swaps, rotations, and kicks, then adapt each operator’s sampling and batch share while keeping a minimum exploration quota. The current portfolio is a strength, and this makes its adaptive feedback more informative than the existing coarse win/trial rates.

4. **Add path relinking as a targeted escape for stalled parents.** When a parent stalls, choose a distant high-quality archive member and progressively apply valid degree-preserving switches that move the parent toward that member; surrogate-screen the resulting intermediates before spending LP calls. This complements random kicks with directed exploration between promising regions.

5. **Calibrate surrogate ranking against exact LP outcomes.** Record surrogate energy and exact LP score for evaluated candidates, and use the observed relationship to adjust screening by parent or search phase. When the surrogate poorly separates candidates, deliberately reserve part of each batch for candidates from lower-ranked energy bands rather than relying mainly on the current top-ranked moves.
