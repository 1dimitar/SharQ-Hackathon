- **The current best, “Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search,” remains the strongest reference point.** It scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, a **−0.30% best gap**, and a **0.97 mean pool score**. - **Archive search with several degree-preserving move types consistently performs well, but does not ensure robust pool-level results.** Generation 32’s guided archive-relinking search scored **0.99**, with a **2.63% mean gap** and **−0.30% best gap**. Generation 34’s novelty-screened archive search also scored **0.99**, with a **2.41% mean gap** and a **−0.30% best pool**, using two-switches, rotations, LP guidance, and random kicks. Both support the current best’s broad move portfolio and diverse archive as effective components.
- **Matching proposals can improve mean gap while retaining the top combined score.** Generation 29’s Prior-Guided Exchange Search with Matching Proposals scored **0.99**, with a **2.19% mean gap**, **0.98 mean pool score**, and **−0.30% best gap**. It outperformed the current best on mean gap and mean pool score, while both tied on combined score and best gap.
- **Surrogate-screened diverse search remains a reliable, if slightly lower-scoring, pattern.** Generations 30 and 31 scored **0.98**, with mean gaps of **2.26%** and **2.21%**, respectively, using diverse starts, archive search, and surrogate-screened LP batches. Generation 34 reached **0.99** with a **2.41% mean gap**, suggesting that archive novelty and its combination with LP-guided proposals can accompany a stronger aggregate score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Full LP-budget use does not guarantee high or consistent pool quality.** The current best, Generations 30–34, and Generation 29 all used approximately **2,000 LP calls**. Yet the current best ranged from **−0.30% to +5.12%** across pools, and Generation 34 ranged from **−0.30% to +3.80%**. Budget exhaustion is therefore not evidence that a search has converged to uniformly strong networks.
- **Bottleneck-focused guidance alone has not matched the best aggregate performance.** Generation 33’s Adaptive Bottleneck-Focused Topology Search scored **0.98**, despite using congestion details to prioritize links and an adaptive archive; its mean gap was **3.01%**, with best gaps varying from **1.06% to 3.02%**. Generation 34’s broader novelty-aware search scored **0.99**, illustrating that bottleneck targeting by itself was not sufficient in these results.
- **Best-case gaps can be misleading without pool-level metrics.** Generation 33 achieved a **1.06% best gap** but only **0.98 combined** and a **3.01% mean gap**. The current best also has a reference-beating result, but its **5.12% worst pool** and **0.97 mean pool score** show that an exceptional result does not remove sensitivity to pool and seed.
- **Surrogate screening and adaptation do not automatically close the remaining gap.** Generations 30 and 31 scored **0.98** with mean gaps near **2.2%**, while the current best’s adaptive operator feedback and surrogate/dual-price screening coexist with a **2.79% mean gap**. These mechanisms are useful parts of search, but the evaluations do not show that they alone consistently improve pool-level outcomes.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best applies diversity at both initialization and archive maintenance.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, selects up to 24 surrogate-energy-ranked starts separated by topology distance at least 4, and fills to 30. Its archive likewise keeps up to 18 structurally separated networks before filling by LP score. This combines quality filtering with protection against a narrow search, and accompanies its **0.99 combined score**.
- **Its proposal and screening pipeline combines guided search with controlled exploration.** The program uses LP prices—or high-load links when prices are unavailable—to focus two-switch and rotation proposals; it ranks candidates using surrogate-energy change adjusted by dual-price gain, evaluates up to three diverse top candidates, and sometimes evaluates a tail candidate. This is a concrete implementation of targeted-but-not-exclusive screening, though its pool gaps show the screening is imperfect.
- **The current best adapts operator selection from observed LP outcomes, but keeps exploration available.** Smoothed rewards and attempt counts update probabilities for two-switches, rotations, and kicks, with a probability floor added before normalization. The floor prevents the observed reward history from entirely eliminating an operator; Generation 34’s novelty-aware parent selection and random kicks reflect a related emphasis on escaping narrow neighborhoods.
- **Cached evaluation and validity checks support budget use without sacrificing topology constraints.** The current best canonicalizes networks, caches LP results in `seen`, checks validity before evaluation, and skips already-seen or externally cached proposals. It used the full **2,000-call** budget on every reported pool, with a mean runtime of **28.95 seconds**. Full use is efficient budget management, but the variable results indicate that proposal quality and starting conditions remain important.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best is still tied at the top combined score, but not the best on every metric.** It scored **0.99**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**. Generation 29 also scored **0.99** and matched its best gap, while improving mean gap to **2.19%** and mean pool score to **0.98**. Generation 34 also scored **0.99**, with a **2.41% mean gap** and **−0.30% best gap**.
- **Recent surrogate-screened programs mostly score 0.98, with a small number reaching 0.99.** Generations 30 and 31 scored **0.98** with mean gaps of **2.26%** and **2.21%**; Generation 32 scored **0.99** with a **2.63% mean gap**; and Generation 34 scored **0.99** with a **2.41% mean gap**. This suggests that the broad archive-and-screening family is competitive, while particular guidance and diversity choices affect aggregate results.
- **The current best’s strongest result is not representative of all its pools.** Its best-per-Hamiltonian gaps are **−0.30%** on r2g181, **+1.37%** on r3g028, **+0.97%** on r3g109, and **+1.65%** on r3g128, yielding a **0.9909 best-per-Hamiltonian score**. Individual pools nevertheless include gaps up to **+5.12%**, consistent with the seed and pool sensitivity reported for other archive searches.
- **Runtime varies substantially across approaches, but lower runtime does not establish better quality.** The current best averaged **28.95 seconds**; Generations 30 and 31 averaged **46.52** and **49.09 seconds**, while Generations 32–34 averaged **17.33**, **15.39**, and **23.29 seconds**. These faster programs ranged from **0.98 to 0.99** combined score, so runtime and score do not move uniformly together.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a bounded matching-proposal operator to the existing move portfolio.** Generate edge-frequency-weighted perfect-matching proposals for a small fixed share of candidates, then apply the current validity checks and surrogate/dual-price ranking before LP evaluation. Generation 29’s matching proposals improved mean gap to 2.19% and mean pool score to 0.98 while retaining a 0.99 combined score, making this a promising complement to two-switches, rotations, and kicks.

2. **Make candidate screening explicitly archive-aware.** Keep the current energy and dual-price ranking, but add a bonus for candidates that are structurally distant from the high-quality archive; use each LP batch for a mix of top-scoring proposals and proposals from underrepresented regions. This extends the program’s successful diversity at initialization and archive maintenance, while addressing its large variation across pools and seeds.

3. **Use archive novelty and parent progress together when selecting parents.** Track each parent’s LP evaluations, improvement magnitude, and selection count; favor productive parents, but reserve a controlled share of selections for rarely explored or structurally distant archive members. This makes the current parent-selection policy more responsive than its fixed 65% preference for the top five and directly targets the persistent pool-level variability.

4. **Calibrate operator adaptation using LP outcomes and preserve exploration quotas.** For each move type, separately track proposals generated, proposals actually evaluated, and improvements over the parent, weighting successful moves by improvement size; update proposal shares from these statistics while enforcing a minimum share for every operator. The current reward scheme only records whether an evaluated candidate beats its parent, so richer feedback may improve operator allocation without losing the exploration protection that has supported strong archive searches.

5. **Add safe path relinking for stalled parents.** When a parent repeatedly fails to improve, select a distant elite archive member and construct several degree-preserving intermediate networks through valid exchanges; screen the intermediates with the existing surrogate and dual-price score before spending LP calls. This adds a directed escape mechanism to the current move portfolio and archive, complementing the successful multi-move search without relying on bottleneck guidance alone.
