# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 78 (*consensus_switches*), confirms that a broad, diverse move portfolio paired with surrogate screening can deliver both a strong aggregate score and a benchmark-beating result.** It achieved **0.99 combined**, **1.83% mean gap**, and **−0.30% best gap**. Its search combines degree-preserving switches, rotations, multi-switch kicks, and archive relinking, rather than relying on a single neighborhood.
- **Maintaining diversity at both initialization and archive selection remains a strong pattern.** Generation 78 chooses low-energy, structurally separated starts and retains diverse LP-ranked archive members. This builds on Generation 49’s similar approach, which scored **0.99** with a **−0.30% best gap**, and Generation 77’s diversity-aware archive search, which scored **0.98** with a **2.76% mean gap**.
- **Using LP-derived information to focus proposals can complement broad exploration.** Generation 79 prioritizes swaps and rotations touching LP-identified bottlenecks and achieved **0.98** with a **1.37% best gap**. The current best also uses `lp_detail` prices or loads to focus moves, but supplements these with unguided swaps, kicks, and relinking, consistent with earlier findings that guidance works best alongside general search.
- **A benchmark-beating pool is possible, but does not imply consistently near-best runs.** Generation 78’s r2g181 seed 1 reached **299,715**, or **−0.30%** against the known 300,620. Across its 16 pools, however, gaps ranged from **−0.30% to +3.92%**. Generation 49 and Generation 73 also found **−0.30%** results, reinforcing that this search family can beat the reference while remaining seed-sensitive.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Guide-directed relinking alone did not match the current best’s aggregate quality.** Generation 76, *relink_destroy_rebuild*, used guide-directed edge replacements, directed 2-switches, and multi-switch jumps, but scored **0.97** with a **4.39% mean gap** and **1.86% best gap**. Its weaker r2g181 best gap (**3.53%**) contrasts with Generation 78’s **−0.30%** on that Hamiltonian.
- **Adaptive parent selection and bottleneck-focused local search were competitive but did not consistently improve the best result.** Generation 79 scored **0.98**, with a **2.49% mean gap** and **1.37% best gap**; Generation 77 scored **0.98** with a **2.76% mean gap** and **1.08% best gap**. Both used the full LP budget, so their lower scores relative to Generation 78 were not explained by fewer evaluations.
- **Surrogate guidance and archive diversity by themselves have not guaranteed lower gaps.** Generation 74’s LP-rewarded operators had a **2.34% mean gap** and **0.63% best gap**; Generation 77’s novelty-aware parent selection had a **2.76% mean gap**. Both are useful search components, but their results trail Generation 78’s **1.83% mean gap** when considered as complete configurations.
- **Full LP-budget use is not sufficient for strong outcomes.** Generations 74, 76, 77, 78, and 79 all used **2,000 LP calls per pool**, yet their combined scores ranged from **0.97 to 0.99** and their mean gaps from **1.83% to 4.39%**. Evaluation quality therefore depends on the proposals and archive behavior, not just call count.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 78 separates proposal generation, screening, and expensive LP evaluation.** It generates focused swaps from LP price/load information, archive-consensus swaps, random swaps, stall-escalated rotations, multi-switch kicks, and relinking steps. `screen_batch` then ranks fresh moves by surrogate-energy change, selects a diverse quality subset, and reserves one slot for archive novelty before LP evaluation. This is the concrete implementation behind its **0.99 score** and **1.83% mean gap**.
- **The current best balances elite exploitation with access to broader regions.** Its parent selector chooses among the top six archive entries **48%** of the time and samples from the full archive otherwise. The archive first retains up to **24 structurally separated** LP-ranked networks, then fills to **32**. This continues the diversity principles identified in Generation 49, while its broader move portfolio and novelty-reserved batch distinguish the implementation.
- **Stall-responsive neighborhoods provide an adaptive way to spend search effort.** Generation 78 increases rotation trials from **70** up to **350** as a parent’s neighborhood stalls, and switches to another archive parent or a random degree-preserving kick when screening yields no fresh batch. These mechanisms help avoid repeatedly spending effort on an exhausted neighborhood while still using the full LP budget.
- **Canonicalization and caching keep evaluations disciplined, though they do not reduce observed call counts.** Generation 78 sorts arcs into canonical tuples, stores LP scores in `seen`, checks validity, and excludes locally seen or tool-cached proposals. As in Generation 49, these safeguards prevent duplicate work, but every pool still used **2,000 LP calls**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt the move portfolio using LP-confirmed improvement rates.** Track, for each operator, how often its evaluated candidates improve on their parent and update smoothed operator-selection probabilities accordingly, while keeping a nonzero exploration floor for every operator. This extends the current best’s broad mix of focused swaps, consensus swaps, rotations, kicks, and relinking with feedback from actual LP outcomes rather than proposal volume.

2. **Spend the novelty slot on candidates farthest from the archive, not just farthest from its nearest member.** The current screen ranks novelty by minimum distance to scored archive entries; try selecting the candidate with the greatest aggregate distance from several top archive networks, using surrogate energy to break ties. This preserves the existing quality-ranked batch while testing whether exploration away from the archive as a whole can reduce seed-to-seed variability.

3. **Use bounded relinking between strong, structurally distant archive members as a deliberate search phase.** Occasionally choose a high-quality parent and a distant target, generate several valid switches that increase target overlap, and screen the intermediate networks with the existing surrogate-and-novelty batch logic. This builds on the current best’s successful relinking component while making its use more intentional and giving promising bridges a chance to receive LP evaluations.

4. **Make parent selection account for underexplored regions as well as archive quality.** Retain the current preference for elite parents, but use some of the remaining probability to favor structurally distinct archive members that have been selected infrequently or whose regions have not recently produced improvements. This is a targeted way to explore the substantial run-to-run variation without replacing the quality-guided search with novelty-only selection.

5. **Refine stall escalation using operator-specific outcomes.** Instead of increasing rotation trials solely from a parent’s overall stall count, track whether rotations, swaps, and kicks from that parent have yielded LP improvements, and increase sampling only for underperforming or underexplored neighborhoods. The current stall-responsive search already helps escape exhausted regions; a more selective escalation could spend its full LP budget on fresh opportunities rather than simply generating more moves.
