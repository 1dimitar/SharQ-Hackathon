# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search (Generation 20), confirms the value of surrogate-screened search over a diverse archive.** It achieved a **0.99 combined score**, **2.64% mean gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**, evaluating **2,000 LP calls per pool** in **48.12 seconds**. Repairing and deduplicating candidates, preserving structurally diverse low-energy starts, and screening batches before LP evaluation produced a strong overall result.
- **Adaptive archive search can improve mean gap and best-case quality, though not the combined score.** Generation 21’s Diverse Surrogate-Guided Network Search also scored **0.99**, with **2.05% mean gap** and a **0.13% best gap**—better on those gap metrics than Generation 20. It used adaptive parent selection alongside two-switches, rotations, multi-switch kicks, and path relinking. Its **3.34% worst gap** shows that the improvement in averages and best case did not eliminate pool variation.
- **Guided relinking and dual-informed moves can produce exceptional individual solutions.** Generation 23’s Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search found a **−0.30% best gap** (better than the reference) with a **2.40% mean gap** and **0.99 combined score**. Generation 22’s Adaptive Dual-Guided Network Search also reached a **−0.30% best gap**. These results support combining surrogate ranking and LP-derived signals with broad topology moves, rather than relying on one guidance signal alone.
- **A varied degree-preserving move portfolio remains a consistent strength.** Across the current program and Generations 20–23, two-switches combined with rotations, three- or multi-switch moves, kicks, or relinking produced strong overall scores. The current program uses two-switches, three-switches, and kicks, while Generation 20’s rotations and multi-switch kicks also accompanied a **0.99** score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Full LP-budget use is not sufficient for consistent quality.** The current program used all **2,000 calls in every pool**, yet gaps ranged from **−0.30% to +5.12%**. Generation 22 likewise used the full budget but had a **2.79% mean gap** and a **5.12% worst gap**. This extends the earlier observation from Generations 15–19: consuming the budget does not guarantee convergence to strong solutions.
- **Search quality remains sensitive to pool and seed, even with strong aggregate scores.** In the current program, `r2g181` ranged from **−0.30% to +4.65%**, while `r3g109` ranged from **+0.97% to +5.12%**. Generation 20’s pools ranged from **0.48% to 5.07%**, and Generation 21 reached **3.34%** at its weakest. Archive diversity and adaptive search improve overall performance but do not remove this variability.
- **The evaluated failure in Generation 24 was implementation-related, not evidence against its search design.** Its score was **0.0** because `improve` called `math.exp` without importing `math`, causing validation to fail. As with other invalid evaluations, no algorithmic performance comparison can be drawn from that result.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s pipeline combines candidate repair, surrogate-ranked starts, and archive-based exploration.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, orders valid networks by `tools.energy`, selects up to 24 starts separated by a topology distance of at least 4, then fills to 30. Its archive similarly preserves up to 18 separated networks before filling to 30 by LP score. This concretely implements the quality-plus-diversity pattern identified in earlier generations.
- **LP-derived prices steer proposals and their ranking, while surrogate energy screens a compact batch.** The current program extracts positive dual prices (or falls back to high-load links), uses those links to focus two- and three-switch proposals, and ranks moves by surrogate energy change adjusted by dual-price gain. It then evaluates up to three diverse high-ranked moves plus an occasional exploratory tail move. This balances targeted search with exploration and accompanies the current program’s **0.99 score** and **28.95-second** mean runtime.
- **Adaptive operator selection is present, but its feedback is local to improvements over the selected parent.** The current program adjusts probabilities for two-switches, three-switches, and kicks using smoothed reward-to-attempt rates; it also tracks parent stalls. This is consistent with the strong results of adaptive search variants, including Generation 21’s adaptive parent-productivity search, although the current evaluation’s pool gaps show that adaptation does not guarantee robust results across seeds.
- **The current implementation is faster than the earlier current-best baseline described in the previous insights, while retaining a comparable combined score.** It averages **28.95 seconds** at **0.99**, compared with the earlier Generation 19 result of **21.39 seconds** at **0.99** and **2.52% mean gap**. The current program’s **2.79% mean gap** is slightly worse, and its run time is higher; therefore, its strong best-known result does not represent an across-the-board improvement over Generation 19.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make surrogate-screened batch selection archive-aware.** Keep the current energy-plus-dual ranking, but add a novelty term measuring each proposal’s distance from the best archived networks. Use each LP batch for a mix of top-ranked moves and candidates from underrepresented regions; the current diverse archive is a strength, but parent-relative ranking can still spend calls on near-duplicates.

2. **Choose parents using measured productivity and exploration exposure.** Track each archive member’s selection count, LP evaluations, and improvement magnitude, then favor recently productive or underexplored parents while gradually reducing the weight of repeatedly stalled ones. This extends the adaptive parent selection that achieved strong mean gaps in Generation 21 and targets the pool-to-pool variability that remains in the current program.

3. **Improve operator adaptation using exact LP outcomes.** Record proposal counts, LP-evaluated counts, and both improvement frequency and magnitude separately for two-switches, three-switches, and kicks. Adjust their proposal probabilities and batch representation from these measurements, with a minimum quota for every operator so an initially unlucky move type is not eliminated.

4. **Use guided path relinking to escape stalls.** When a parent has stalled, select a distant, high-quality archive member and generate valid degree-preserving intermediate topologies that progressively move the parent toward it. Screen those intermediates with the existing surrogate before LP evaluation; recent strong results from relinking and dual-informed search suggest this can complement random kicks with directed exploration.

5. **Calibrate surrogate ranking against LP results and preserve a screening fallback.** For evaluated moves, track how energy and dual-adjusted rank relate to exact LP improvement, and use that history to tune the ranking weight or identify phases where the surrogate is unreliable. In those phases, reserve part of the batch for diverse mid- or lower-ranked proposals, rather than relying mainly on the current top-ranked moves.
