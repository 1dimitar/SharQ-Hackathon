# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 49 Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search, remains a strong reference point:** it scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.973 mean pool score**. Its repaired, deduplicated, energy-ranked starts and quality-and-diversity archive helped it achieve a **0.9909 best-per-Hamiltonian score**, including a benchmark-beating result on r2g181.
- **Combining varied degree-preserving moves with surrogate screening is consistently effective.** Generation 47 Adaptive Diverse Frontier Network Search scored **0.99**, with a stronger **2.13% mean gap** and **−0.30% best gap**; it used price/load-focused two-switches, rotations, archive-biased permutations, and random kicks. Generation 44’s related search also scored **0.99**, with a **2.34% mean gap** and **0.56% best gap**.
- **Structural diversity at initialization and during search remains a useful feature.** Generation 49 separates initial and archive members by topology distance, then LP-ranks the remaining slots. Generation 47 likewise combines a diverse frontier with novelty-aware parent selection. These approaches outperformed Generation 46’s recombination-centered search, which scored **0.98** with a **2.99% mean gap**.
- **Guided moves can yield exceptional pool-specific results without eliminating variability.** Generation 49 found **299,715** on r2g181 seed 1, **0.30% below** the reported known best. Generation 47 also reported a **−0.30% best gap** on r2g181, while its other Hamiltonians’ best gaps were **+1.31% to +1.41%**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Recombination and adaptive operator weighting did not beat the strongest diverse frontier searches.** Generation 46 Adaptive Recombination and Network Neighborhood Search used repaired crossover, edge switches, and adaptive parent/operator weights, but scored **0.98** with a **2.99% mean gap** and a **1.53% best gap**. This is weaker than Generation 49’s **0.99 / 2.79% / −0.30%** and Generation 47’s **0.99 / 2.13% / −0.30%**.
- **Path relinking is not consistently beneficial by itself.** Generation 48’s bounded path-relinking variant scored **0.98** with a **2.29% mean gap** and **1.15% best gap**; Generation 44’s stalled-path-relinking variant scored **0.99**, but still had a **2.34% mean gap** and results sensitive to pool and Hamiltonian. These results do not show a consistent advantage over the stronger archive/frontier approaches.
- **A full LP budget does not ensure uniformly good outcomes.** Generation 49 used all **2,000 calls** on every pool but ranged from **−0.30% to +5.12%** gap. Generation 44 also exhausted its budget and had a weakest-Hamiltonian best result of **306,715**, illustrating that call utilization alone does not prevent weak search outcomes.
- **Pool and seed sensitivity persists even for the top-scoring program.** Generation 49’s r2g181 results ranged from **−0.30% to +4.65%**, and r3g109 ranged from **+0.97% to +5.12%**. Its strong best-per-Hamiltonian results coexist with substantially weaker individual pools.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 49’s strongest implementation feature is staged filtering before expensive LP evaluation.** It repairs and validates candidate networks, deduplicates canonical arc sets, ranks starts by `tools.energy`, and selects structurally separated initial networks. Search proposals are then screened by surrogate energy adjusted with dual-price gain before up to three diverse candidates—and sometimes a random tail candidate—are LP-evaluated.
- **It maintains diversity without abandoning quality selection.** The archive selects up to 18 members separated by topology distance before filling to 30 by LP rank; parent selection favors the top five 65% of the time but still samples the full archive. This balances elite exploitation with access to less-used regions, consistent with the program’s strong **0.99** score and **0.9909** best-per-Hamiltonian score.
- **The move portfolio is broad, and adaptation retains an exploration floor.** Generation 49 mixes two-switches, three-arc rotations, and multi-switch kicks. Smoothed reward/attempt rates determine operator probabilities, with a constant **0.25** added before normalization so an operator’s probability cannot collapse solely due to poor recent rewards. Generation 47 used a related mix of bottleneck-focused switches, rotations, archive-biased proposals, and random kicks, achieving the best reported mean gap (**2.13%**) among these summaries.
- **LP information acts as a proposal guide rather than the sole objective.** Generation 49 targets positive-price arcs, falls back to heavily loaded arcs, and ranks moves using `energy(net) - base_e - 0.02 * dual_gain`. It also checks validity and cache status and avoids repeat LP evaluation through canonical-network caching. These filters help make the fixed budget productive, though the program still averaged **28.95 seconds** per pool.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reserve LP evaluations for structurally novel candidates.** Keep the current energy/dual-gain ranking for most of each batch, but reserve one LP slot for the valid, uncached candidate with the greatest symmetric-difference distance from the quality-and-diversity archive, breaking ties by surrogate rank. The current program already benefits from diverse starts and archive members; extending that diversity pressure to batch selection may help its substantial pool-to-pool variability.

2. **Use progress- and exploration-aware parent selection.** Track each archive member’s selection count and LP improvement contribution, then choose parents using a mixture of high-performing members, under-selected members, and structurally isolated members. This is a targeted alternative to the fixed 65% preference for the top five and preserves elite exploitation while giving less-explored regions a chance to produce improvements.

3. **Update operator rewards from LP outcomes, including evaluation opportunity.** For each move type, track proposals generated, candidates selected for LP evaluation, and the size of resulting improvements; use a smoothed improvement-per-evaluation rate to adjust operator probabilities while retaining a nonzero exploration floor. This builds on the successful switch/rotation/kick portfolio and avoids over-rewarding operators merely because they generate many screened proposals.

4. **Add a bounded escape move for stalled parents.** After several unproductive rounds, pair a stalled parent with a distant, high-quality archive member and generate a few valid degree-preserving exchanges that move the parent toward the partner’s arc set. Screen these candidates with the existing validity, cache, surrogate/dual, and novelty checks, keeping the move occasional because prior path-relinking results were mixed rather than consistently superior.

5. **Test archive-guided edge proposals as a small, separately measured operator.** For a limited share of proposals, bias a degree-preserving switch toward edges recurring among strong, structurally diverse archive members, then apply the same screening and LP-evaluation pipeline as for existing moves. Keep it additive and bounded: recombination and matching-style guidance have not reliably beaten the frontier search on their own, but may contribute useful proposals when guided by the current archive.
