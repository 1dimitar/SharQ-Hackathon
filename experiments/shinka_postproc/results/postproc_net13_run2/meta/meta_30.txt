# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 19’s Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search, remains a strong reference point:** it scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**. Its repaired, deduplicated starts are ranked by surrogate energy while preserving topology diversity; subsequent archive search combines two-switches, rotations, and kicks, with LP-derived prices helping guide proposals. The best result beat the reference on `r2g181`, though individual pools still ranged up to a **5.12% gap**.
- **The new matching-proposal variant also reached a 0.99 combined score and matched the current best’s −0.30% best gap.** Generation 29’s Prior-Guided Exchange Search with Matching Proposals had a **2.19% mean gap**, better than the current best’s 2.79%, and a **0.98 mean pool score**. Its congestion-informed exchanges, elite-directed moves, and occasional edge-frequency-weighted perfect-matching proposals accompanied these strong aggregate results; most pools nevertheless remained **1.31–3.79%** above reference.
- **Surrogate screening combined with topology diversity continues to produce competitive results.** Generation 27’s Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search scored **0.98**, with a **2.30% mean gap** and a **0.91% best gap**, using an archive, two-switches, rotations, kicks, and bottleneck-informed screening. Generation 25’s similar calibrated-screening approach also scored **0.98**, with a **2.50% mean gap**. This supports the earlier finding that a diverse, quality-ranked archive and varied degree-preserving moves are reliable components, though they do not alone ensure a 0.99 score.
- **Adaptive or targeted exchange search can improve particular gap metrics without uniformly improving the aggregate score.** Generation 26’s Adaptive Degree-Preserving Network Search scored **0.98**, with a **3.00% mean gap** and **1.84% best gap**; Generation 29’s matching hybrid achieved a stronger mean gap. Across these variants, two-switches and rotations or three-arc exchanges remain useful, while the results show that the move portfolio and guidance mechanism matter alongside adaptation.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Spending all 2,000 LP calls did not guarantee consistently strong pool results.** The current best and Generations 25–27 and 29 all used their full budgets. Yet the current best ranged from **−0.30% to +5.12%**, Generation 26 from **+1.84% to +3.88%**, and Generation 25 from **+1.79% to +3.91%**. Full budget use is therefore not itself evidence that search has converged.
- **Surrogate-screened search variants did not consistently match the current best’s combined score.** Generations 25 and 27 both scored **0.98**, despite using diversity-aware screening and broad degree-preserving moves; Generation 27’s best gap was **0.91%**, but its **2.30% mean gap** and combined score remained below the current best’s **0.99**. Strong single-pool outcomes do not necessarily translate into better overall results.
- **The Generation 28 archive-path-relink variant produced no valid evaluation.** Its `improve` function raised `AttributeError: 'list' object has no attribute 'add'` and scored **0.0**. This is an implementation failure, not evidence that archive search or path relinking is ineffective.
- **Adaptive search alone did not remove residual gaps or pool variation.** Generation 26 used adaptive parent selection, bottleneck-based prioritization, and fallback exploration, but scored **0.98** with a **3.00% mean gap** and no pool below **1.84%**. The current best’s own adaptive operator feedback likewise coexists with a **5.12% worst pool**, so adaptation does not ensure robustness.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best implements quality-plus-diversity at both initialization and archive maintenance.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, selects up to 24 surrogate-energy-ranked starts separated by topology distance at least 4, then fills to 30. Its archive preserves up to 18 similarly separated networks before filling to 30 by LP score. This combines strong starts with structural breadth and accompanies a **0.99 combined score**.
- **The current best uses LP information to target moves, then screens a compact batch using both surrogate energy and dual prices.** It focuses proposals on positive-price links, or on heavily loaded links when prices are unavailable; candidate moves are ranked by energy change adjusted for dual-price gain. It evaluates up to three diverse high-ranked candidates plus an occasional exploratory tail candidate. This targeted-but-not-exclusive screening is consistent with its strong best-per-Hamiltonian score of **0.9909**, while pool gaps indicate the screening does not eliminate difficult seeds.
- **A varied degree-preserving move set is repeatedly present in successful programs.** The current best uses two-switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks; Generations 25–27 also combine two-switches or exchanges with rotations and kicks, while Generation 29 adds elite-directed exchanges and matching proposals. The evidence supports the portfolio approach, but does not isolate any one operator as the cause of higher scores.
- **Adaptive probabilities and cached LP evaluations are useful implementation features, but their impact is not sufficient on its own.** The current best updates operator rewards after LP results and caches scores by canonicalized topology. Generation 26 also uses adaptive parent selection and a fixed evaluation budget. Both consume their full budgets, but Generation 26’s score is **0.98**, showing that adaptation and budget management must be considered alongside proposal quality and diversity.

- **The current best remains tied at the top combined score with Generation 29, but the variants trade off average and pool quality.** Both scored **0.99**. # META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make proposal screening archive-aware.** Keep the current surrogate-energy and dual-price ranking, but add a novelty bonus based on each candidate’s minimum topology distance from the high-quality archive. Use each LP batch for a mix of top-ranked proposals and candidates from underrepresented regions, extending the current program’s successful combination of quality-ranked starts and topology-diverse archive maintenance.

2. **Add matching proposals as a bounded exploration operator.** Generation 29’s congestion-informed exchange search with occasional edge-frequency-weighted perfect-matching proposals matched the current best’s combined score and best gap while improving mean gap from 2.79% to 2.19%. Integrate matching proposals as a small, fixed share of candidate generation, then screen them with the existing surrogate and dual-price ranking so they complement rather than replace the current two-switch, rotation, and kick portfolio.

3. **Allocate parent selections using both progress and underexploration.** Track each archived network’s selection count, LP evaluations, and improvement magnitude; favor productive parents while giving a controlled chance to rarely selected ones. This targets the substantial pool-to-pool variation that persists despite the current program’s diverse archive and adaptive operator probabilities.

4. **Update operator probabilities from exact LP outcomes with explicit exploration floors.** For each move type, record how often proposals are generated, LP-evaluated, and improve the parent, along with improvement magnitude; use those measurements to adjust proposal shares while reserving a minimum quota for every operator. The current program adapts operator weights, but its coarse reward signal can miss differences between proposal generation and actual LP success.

5. **Use stalled-parent path relinking toward a distant elite, with safe intermediate validation.** When a parent stalls, choose a structurally distant high-quality archive member and generate degree-preserving intermediate networks through valid exchanges; screen these candidates with the existing surrogate before spending LP calls. This adds directed escape moves to the current varied move portfolio, while ensuring each intermediate is validated before it enters the evaluation batch.
