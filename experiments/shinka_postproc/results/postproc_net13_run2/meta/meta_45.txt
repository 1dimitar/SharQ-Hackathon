# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 49 Surrogate-Guided Diverse Network Search, remains a strong reference point:** it scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.973 mean pool score**. Its repair-and-deduplicate initialization, surrogate-ranked starts, and diverse archive combine solution quality with coverage of different network structures.
- **A portfolio of degree-preserving moves paired with surrogate screening continues to perform well.** The current best uses two-switches, three-link rotations, multi-switch kicks, and diversity-aware selection of LP evaluations. The new Generations 40–43 also used variants of this pattern and scored **0.98–0.99**: Generation 40 scored **0.99 / 2.40% mean gap**, Generation 42 **0.98 / 2.27%**, and Generation 43 **0.99 / 2.59%**.
- **Diversity is useful at both initialization and archive maintenance.** In the current best, starts are separated by topology distance at least 4, as are archive entries; among up to 30 archive members, up to 18 are selected for structural separation before remaining positions are filled by LP rank. Generation 42’s diverse archive search achieved the strongest reported mean gap among the new programs, **2.27%**, though its combined score was **0.98**.
- **Guided search can produce substantial wins on favorable pools, but results remain pool-dependent.** The current best found a **−0.30%** gap on r2g181 seed 1. Generations 40 and 41 also beat the known benchmark by **0.30%** on r2g181, using adaptive parent selection and dual-relief/exchange guidance, respectively.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Full LP-budget use does not guarantee strong performance.** The current best, Generations 40–43, and Generation 41 all used **2,000 calls per pool**, yet their mean gaps ranged from **2.27% to 2.95%** (excluding Generation 45’s invalid result). Earlier Generation 38 also used the full budget but scored only **0.92**, with an **11.26% mean gap**.
- **LP-guided matching and exchange mechanisms were less consistent than the strongest diverse archive searches.** Generation 41’s LP-Guided Exchange Search with Matching Sampling scored **0.98** with a **2.95% mean gap**, despite using congestion prices, loads, elite arc probabilities, and weighted matchings. Its other Hamiltonian best gaps reached **+3.25%**, while the current best scored **0.99** overall.
- **Dual guidance alone does not eliminate weak pools.** The current best incorporates positive-price/load targeting and a dual-price adjustment in candidate ranking, yet its pool gaps still range from **−0.30% to +5.12%**. The related Generation 41 also varied from a benchmark-beating result to best gaps of **+1.66% to +3.25%**, indicating that such guidance complements rather than replaces surrogate quality and structural diversity.
- **Validation failures erase any potential algorithmic value.** Generation 45’s elite edge-frequency/crossover approach received a **0.0 combined score** because `model_sample` used forbidden `__import__`; its search performance was not credited.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best combines multiple filters before spending LP calls.** It repairs and canonicalizes candidates, deduplicates them, ranks starts by `tools.energy`, and spreads initial and archived networks by symmetric-difference distance. During search, it generates a compact proposal batch, ranks proposals by surrogate-energy change adjusted for dual-price gain, and LP-evaluates up to three separated candidates plus an occasional exploratory tail candidate.
- **Operator adaptation retains exploration rather than committing to one move type.** The current best updates smoothed reward and attempt counts for two-switches, rotations, and kicks, then adds a probability floor before normalizing operator probabilities. This is a concrete way to adapt the move mix without suppressing alternatives permanently; the related Generation 40 likewise used adaptive parent selection and achieved **0.99 combined**.
- **LP details are used as guidance, not as the only selection criterion.** The current best targets positive-price arcs, falls back to highly loaded arcs when prices are unavailable, and combines price changes with surrogate energy when ranking moves. Generation 41 also used LP prices and loads, but its **2.95% mean gap** shows that LP guidance is most effective as part of a broader quality-and-diversity search.
- **Caching, validity checks, and bounded batches help make the fixed budget productive.** The current best caches results by canonical network, skips invalid or externally cached proposals, and uses up to 36 generated candidates to select a small LP batch. It used all **2,000 calls** in every pool, averaging **28.95 seconds**; Generation 43’s related search averaged **16.37 seconds** while also using the full budget, but achieved a slightly worse **2.59% mean gap**.

However, Generation 42’s **2.27% mean gap** and Generation 40’s **2.40%** are lower than the current best’s **2.79%**; combined score and mean gap therefore do not rank these programs identically.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reserve part of each LP batch for candidates novel relative to the archive.** Keep the existing surrogate/dual ranking for most evaluations, but select one candidate per batch by maximizing its symmetric-difference distance from the high-quality archive, breaking ties by energy. This extends the program’s proven structural diversity beyond initialization and archive maintenance and may help with its large pool-to-pool variation.

2. **Add bounded path relinking for stalled parents.** When a parent has accumulated several unsuccessful rounds, pair it with a distant, high-quality archive member and generate a small number of intermediate networks using valid degree-preserving exchanges that move the parent toward the partner’s arc set. Screen these candidates through the existing validity, cache, energy, dual-price, and diversity filters before using LP calls; this offers a directed escape route without replacing the successful move portfolio.

3. **Make parent selection reward both progress and underexploration.** Track each archive member’s selection count and the magnitude of LP improvements it has produced; favor productive parents, while reserving a fixed share of selections for rarely chosen or structurally isolated members. This replaces the current fixed 65% preference for the top five with feedback-driven exploration while retaining access to promising elites.

4. **Update operator probabilities using evaluation outcomes, not just proposal wins.** For each move type, track proposals generated, proposals selected for LP evaluation, and the size of any resulting improvement; update its smoothed reward rate from those statistics while preserving a minimum probability for every operator. The current adaptive mix already works alongside two-switches, rotations, and kicks, so richer feedback is a targeted way to allocate effort without prematurely suppressing a useful move.

5. **Test a small archive-guided matching operator as an addition to the existing portfolio.** For a limited fraction of proposals, construct degree-preserving matching-based candidates biased toward edges common among strong, diverse archive members, then pass them through the same validation, cache, surrogate/dual, and novelty filters as other moves. Matching/exchange guidance was less consistent on its own, so keep it bounded and evaluate whether it contributes useful candidates rather than replacing the proven operators.
