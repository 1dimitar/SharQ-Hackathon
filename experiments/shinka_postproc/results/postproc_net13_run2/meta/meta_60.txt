# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Surrogate screening plus diverse, degree-preserving search remains the strongest overall pattern.** The current best, **Generation 49 Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search**, scored **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.973 mean pool score**. It repairs and deduplicates candidate topologies, ranks starts by surrogate energy, and screens diverse proposals before spending LP calls.
- **A broader move portfolio supports strong discoveries.** Generation 49 combines two-switches, three-arc rotations, and multi-switch kicks; its proposals are ranked by surrogate energy adjusted by LP dual-price gain. This produced strong best-per-Hamiltonian gaps: **−0.30%** on r2g181, **+1.37%** on r3g028, **+0.97%** on r3g109, and **+1.65%** on r3g128.
- **Recent results confirm that archive-based, surrogate-guided search is effective, while differing in consistency.** Generation 56 scored **0.99** with a **2.26% mean gap** and **1.15% best gap**; Generation 57 also scored **0.99**, with a **2.41% mean gap** and **0.59% best gap**. Both use diverse archives and screened degree-preserving moves, and both exhausted the 2,000-call budget.
- **Adaptive selection and novelty reserves can yield exceptional pool-level results.** Generation 58 reached a **0.40% best gap** and Generation 61 reached **−0.30%**, both using 2,000 LP calls per pool. These results show the value of adaptive parent selection and topology diversity for best-case discovery, though their aggregate outcomes were not stronger than the top combined scores.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **The LP-guided topology EDA was markedly weaker than archive-based search.** Generation 55 scored **0.94**, with an **8.44% mean gap** and **5.04% best gap**, versus Generation 49’s **0.99** and **2.79% mean gap**. Its rank-weighted link frequencies, demand prior, and LP edge penalties produced strong candidates but did not consistently approach the best topologies.
- **A very strong best pool does not ensure robust aggregate performance.** Generation 61 found a **−0.30% best gap**, but its other Hamiltonians had best gaps of **+1.99% to +2.53%**; its overall mean gap was **2.40%** and combined score **0.98**. Similarly, Generation 57’s **0.59% best gap** coexisted with a **5.05%** pool gap.
- **More search effort or faster execution alone does not account for stronger results.** Generation 55 averaged **15–20 seconds** but scored **0.94**; Generation 49 averaged **28.95 seconds** and scored **0.99**. The evaluated programs generally used the full **2,000 LP calls**, so runtime and budget use alone do not explain their performance differences.
- **Search quality remains sensitive to candidate pools and seeds despite valid implementations.** Generation 49’s pool gaps ranged from **−0.30% to +5.12%**. Generation 58 and Generation 61 also showed variation across Hamiltonians, so strong best-case results did not eliminate weaker individual runs.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 49’s staged filtering is a key successful implementation pattern.** It repairs and validates candidates, canonicalizes arc sets for deduplication, ranks starts with `tools.energy`, and selects structurally separated initial networks. This avoids spending LP calls on invalid or redundant topologies while retaining a varied starting set.
- **Its archive balances quality and novelty.** The program keeps up to **18** LP-ranked, mutually separated networks, then fills the archive to **30**. Parent selection favors the top five **65%** of the time but can sample the full archive, combining exploitation with access to less-elite topologies.
- **The operator portfolio combines focused exploration with an explicit exploration floor.** Two-switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks are selected using smoothed success rates, with an additive **0.25** probability floor. Positive LP-price arcs guide proposals, and highly loaded arcs are a fallback; screened proposals combine surrogate energy and dual gain using `energy(net) - base_e - 0.02 * dual_gain`.
- **Canonicalization, validity checks, and caching make the search robust under the LP budget.** Generation 49 canonicalizes networks before caching and LP evaluation, checks validity before accepting proposals, and uses cached scores to avoid repeated evaluations. Its successful full-budget runs contrast with earlier failed implementations: Generation 52 stopped on a missing `math` import, and Generation 54 failed on an unhashable NumPy array.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 49 remains the strongest reference on aggregate metrics among the reported programs.** It scored **0.99 combined**, with a **0.973 mean pool score** and **0.9909 mean best-per-Hamiltonian score**. Generation 56 and Generation 57 also scored **0.99**, but their mean gaps were **2.26%** and **2.41%**, respectively; the supplied results do not report equivalent pool-score figures for a direct comparison beyond combined score.
- **The newest results reinforce the strength of diverse surrogate-guided archives without displacing the current best.** Generation 56’s **2.26% mean gap** improves on Generation 49’s **2.79%**, while Generation 57’s **0.59% best gap** is better than Generation 49’s **−0.30%** only if comparing incorrectly by magnitude: **−0.30% is the lower, better gap**. Their reported aggregate combined scores are tied at **0.99**, with Generation 49 retaining the documented best-per-Hamiltonian score and pool-score evidence.
- **Generation 58 and Generation 61 show that best-gap results and mean performance can diverge.** Generation 58 had a **0.40% best gap** but a **2.40% mean gap** and **0.98 combined score**; Generation 61 had a **−0.30% best gap**, also a **2.40% mean gap** and **0.98 combined score**. Generation 49 likewise shows this distinction, with a single **−0.30%** pool but gaps up to **+5.12%**.
- **The weaker EDA result establishes a substantial gap between link-frequency construction and iterative archive search in these evaluations.** Generation 55’s **0.94 score** and **8.44% mean gap** trail the diverse-search results, while using all **2,000 LP calls**. The advantage in these results is associated with iterative surrogate-screened topology moves and archive selection, rather than simply exhausting the LP budget.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reserve one LP evaluation per search batch for a maximally novel candidate.** Keep the existing surrogate/dual-ranked batch, but also evaluate the valid, uncached proposal with the greatest symmetric difference from the archive, breaking ties by surrogate rank. This extends the current program’s effective diversity filtering and directly tests a way to reduce its substantial variation across seeds and pools.

2. **Choose parents using archive quality, novelty, and exploration history.** Retain a strong preference for elite archive members, but occasionally prioritize members that are structurally isolated or have been selected infrequently; track whether their descendants improve on the parent. This builds on the successful quality-and-diversity archive while allowing useful regions outside the top-five-biased selection to contribute more consistently.

3. **Update operator rewards using improvement per LP evaluation.** Track, for two-switches, rotations, and kicks, how many proposals are screened, sent to LP, and yield improvement, then update smoothed selection rates from those outcomes while preserving a nonzero probability floor. This refines the existing adaptive portfolio so operators are rewarded for useful discoveries rather than proposal volume alone.

4. **Use stalled-parent escape moves sparingly.** When a parent has repeatedly failed to improve, occasionally combine it with a distant, high-quality archive member and apply a small number of degree-preserving exchanges that move toward the partner’s arc set. Validate, deduplicate, and screen these proposals through the current surrogate/dual and novelty pipeline; the bounded use keeps this exploratory strategy from displacing the proven local search.

5. **Add archive-consensus edge guidance as a limited proposal bias.** Identify arcs that recur among high-quality but structurally diverse archive members, then bias a small fraction of switches toward preserving or introducing those arcs. Evaluate these candidates through the existing screening and LP pipeline, retain the current operators’ exploration floor, and compare improvement per LP call to avoid overcommitting to consensus that may reflect only one promising region.
