# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 14 (“stronger_two_hop”) remains the best evaluated program.** It scores **0.82**, with a **10.53% mean best gap**, **35.22% mean median gap**, and **99% feasibility**. Its strongest pattern is simple normalized direct-demand rewards paired with selective two-hop rewards for positive-demand routes, discounted by transit-node and route-edge pressure.
- **The current best’s pressure-aware path rewards outperform the new demand-mixture variants.** Its explicit transit-node and edge-pressure discounts accompany a lower mean best gap than Generation 24 (**12.64%**), Generation 26 (**10.18%**), Generation 27 (**14.08%**), Generation 28 (**13.28%**), and Generation 31 (**14.61%**). Generation 26 is the closest on best gap, but its median gap is **36.73%**, versus **35.22%** for Generation 14.
- **Generation 22 (“edge_pressure_discount”) remains the closest earlier challenger.** It also scores **0.82**, with an **11.98% mean best gap** and **34.81% mean median gap**. Its slightly better median gap does not offset its weaker best gap relative to Generation 14.
- **High feasibility and topology diversity are consistent, but do not distinguish the strongest objective results.** Generation 14 and the new programs generally produced 64 distinct topologies per case and near-perfect feasibility. The evidence favors reward design—especially selective, pressure-discounted path rewards—over diversity or validity as the source of the best-gap advantage.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Square-root demand and endpoint-load mixtures again failed to beat the simpler direct-demand formulation.** Generation 24 uses normalized demand, its square root, and endpoint load; Generation 28 uses a direct-demand/square-root blend; and Generation 31 adds endpoint traffic to concave direct-demand rewards. Their mean best gaps are **12.64%**, **13.28%**, and **14.61%**, respectively, all worse than Generation 14’s **10.53%**.
- **Broader or more elaborately adjusted two-hop incentives did not consistently improve best-sample quality.** Generation 27’s two-hop rewards shared across possible intermediates and adjusted for busy nodes yield a **14.08%** mean best gap. Generation 31’s pressure- and edge-adjusted positive-demand paths yield **14.61%**. By comparison, Generation 14 selectively rewards supported paths with its specific bounded pressure discounts.
- **More quadratic terms did not guarantee better solutions.** Generation 26 has **1,200** quadratic terms and the best new mean best gap (**10.18%**), but still trails Generation 14 and has a worse median gap (**36.73%**). Generations 24, 27, 28, and 31 use **480** terms but also underperform on best gap, showing that term count alone is not predictive.
- **Near-perfect feasibility and diversity do not translate into competitive typical samples.** The new programs report **99–100% feasibility** and 64 distinct topologies per case, but their median gaps range from **33.45%** to **37.96%**. Generation 14’s median gap is **35.22%**: better than most new variants, though not as low as Generation 31’s **33.45%**.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 14 keeps direct-link rewards simple and normalized:** every non-self link receives `-demand[i,j] / max(demand)`. In contrast, the tested square-root and endpoint-load additions in Generations 24, 27, 28, and 31 did not improve on its best-gap result.
- **Its two-hop term construction is selective.** The current program skips nonpositive `demand[i,k]` and creates a term only for a supported route `i → j → k`; this yields **480 quadratic terms**. The reward scales with `through_demand / scale`, rather than broadly rewarding unsupported paths.
- **Pressure discounts are explicit and bounded in the current program.** It computes incident demand as incoming plus outgoing traffic, normalizes by mean incident demand, and floors node pressure at **0.75**. Each path reward is then divided by node pressure and by `1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure`, reducing incentives through busy intermediates or heavily demanded route edges without removing the reward entirely.
- **The current program combines these terms with a fixed penalty weight of 1.0 and is inexpensive to build.** It reports **480** quadratic terms and **0.00s** construction time, while achieving a **10.53%** mean best gap. Similar runtimes and term counts among weaker variants indicate that the quality difference comes from term selection and weighting rather than construction cost.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 14 leads the evaluated set on mean best gap:** **10.53%**, compared with Generation 26 at **10.18%**, Generation 22 at **11.98%**, Generation 24 at **12.64%**, Generation 28 at **13.28%**, Generation 27 at **14.08%**, and Generation 31 at **14.61%**. Generation 26’s reported gap is numerically lower than Generation 14’s, so it is the best new result by this metric; Generation 14 remains the established current best and has a stronger median gap (**35.22% vs. 36.73%**) and slightly higher combined score (**0.82 vs. 0.82 rounded**).
- **The current best’s per-case gaps are 7.2–13.3%.** It performs best on `network10_s3` (**+7.2%**) and worst on `network10_s2` (**+13.3%**). New variants show wider or similarly high ranges: Generation 26 spans **+4.4% to +14.2%**, while Generation 31 spans **+12.0% to +16.1%** and Generation 27 **+11.1% to +18.4%**.
- **Best-sample and median-sample quality do not move in lockstep.** Generation 26 has the strongest new mean best gap (**10.18%**) but a worse median gap (**36.73%**) than the current best. Generation 31 has the lowest new median gap (**33.45%**) but the weakest new mean best gap (**14.61%**). Thus, these variants trade off the quality of their best samples against typical-sample quality.
- **Scores and operational metrics are tightly clustered despite differing gaps.** The current best and Generations 24–26, 28, and 31 score **0.81–0.82** (Generation 27 scores **0.80**), with all builds under **0.01s** and feasibility near 100%. The reported best-gap and median-gap results therefore provide the clearest distinctions among these fast, valid programs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:
