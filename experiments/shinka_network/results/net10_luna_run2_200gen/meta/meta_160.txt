# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Linear Topology Surrogate**
- **Implementation**: Assigns each directed link a linear weight equal to negative demand normalized by the matrix maximum, with no quadratic terms and a degree-penalty weight of 1.0. The evaluator receives the terms and runtime through a fixed entry point.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.80; mean best gap was 12.76%, median gap 40.37%, feasibility 100%, and the model used zero quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: All cases produced 64 distinct topologies; three had 100% valid samples before repair, while network10_s3 had 98%. Best gaps ranged from 10.7% to 15.9%, with network10_s3 worst, indicating the simple demand-only surrogate yields valid but suboptimal topologies.

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Two-Hop Surrogate QUBO**
- **Implementation**: Assigns negative linear weights to directed links in proportion to demand, then adds negative quadratic rewards for two-hop paths, discounted by transit-node pressure and direct edge demand. Returns a degree-penalty weight of 1.0; construction is efficient for the tested 10-node networks.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.81, with a 12.30% mean best gap, 36.41% mean median gap, 0.99 feasible rate, and 480 quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: The method produced topologies close to the reference best, but median gaps were substantially larger; the largest best gap was 15.6% on network10_s1. It generated 64 distinct topologies per case and built in under 0.01 seconds.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 135 remains the best overall**, scoring **0.85** with an **8.09% mean best gap**. Its strongest pattern is a linear-dominant direct-link reward, `-(0.85 × demand + 0.15 × sqrt(demand))`, combined with selective, pressure- and edge-discounted two-hop rewards. The current best’s case-level best gaps range from **6.4% to 10.3%**.
- **Pressure- and edge-aware path support remains competitive.** The current best uses node pressure with a floor of `0.75` and discounts each path by `1 + 0.5 × edge_pressure`. Generation 154, a related *Pressure-Discounted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate*, scored **0.84** with a **10.67% best gap** and **28.88% median gap**. These results reinforce the value of congestion-aware path rewards, though Generation 135 retains the better best-sample quality.
- **Compact, selective two-hop modeling performs better than expanded route modeling.** Generation 135 has **480 quadratic terms** and an **8.09% best gap**. Generation 158 uses **1,200 terms** and achieved a **10.92% best gap**; its 100% feasibility did not offset its weaker best-sample quality.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Increasing or otherwise changing two-hop support has not beaten Generation 135’s calibration.** The current best’s code sets `path_strength = 0.56`, but its measured result is still **8.09% best gap**—the reported baseline, not evidence of an improvement over it. Generation 154 scored **0.84** with a **10.67% best gap**, and Generation 155 scored **0.82** with an **11.50% best gap**.
- **Capping path rewards and raising the degree penalty improved feasibility but weakened solution quality.** Generation 157 achieved **100% feasibility**, yet scored **0.80**, with **15.09% best** and **37.24% median** gaps. Its weakest case, `network10_s1`, reached a **20.7% best gap**.
- **More elaborate route rewards and redundancy terms did not reliably improve typical solutions.** Generation 158 scored **0.82** with a **35.33% median gap** and 1,200 quadratic terms. Generation 159 scored **0.83**, but had a **30.28% median gap** and only **0.75 feasibility**. These results continue the earlier pattern that extra terms or high feasibility alone do not ensure better objective quality.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 135’s demand preprocessing supports stable reward construction.** It clips demand to nonnegative values, clears the diagonal, normalizes by maximum traffic, and falls back to a scale of `1.0` for zero demand. Its direct rewards therefore exclude self-links while retaining a well-defined zero-demand behavior.
- **The direct-link reward is linear-dominant but not purely linear.** The current best blends normalized demand with its square root at an **85:15** ratio. This preserves demand sensitivity while giving moderate-demand links some influence; newer variants with different direct rewards have not surpassed its **8.09%** mean best gap.
- **Two-hop rewards are selective and congestion-aware.** The current best skips zero-demand paths, bounds relative path quality to `[0.9, 1.25]`, and discounts support by intermediate-node pressure and component-edge load. It produces **480 terms** with negligible build time. Generation 155 also used pressure-discounted support and 480 terms, but its **11.50% best gap** and **33.73% median gap** show that matching the broad structure does not guarantee matching Generation 135’s calibration.
- **Feasibility and topology diversity are not sufficient quality measures.** Generation 157 produced valid samples and 64 distinct topologies per instance, but still had substantially larger gaps. Likewise, Generation 135’s feasibility is **0.81**, below some newer alternatives, while its best-solution quality remains stronger.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best leads the reported programs on mean best gap and score:** Generation 135 scores **0.85** at **8.09%** mean best gap. The closest new result is Generation 154 at **0.84** and **10.67%**; Generation 159 scores **0.83** and **11.40%**.
- **The new evaluations do not displace the prior leader.** Generation 155 scores **0.82** with an **11.50%** best gap; Generation 157 scores **0.80** with **15.09%**; and Generation 158 scores **0.82** with **10.92%**. Their median gaps are also higher than the current best’s **29.52%**, except Generation 154, whose **28.88%** median gap is slightly lower.
- **Generation 135’s case results reveal a best-versus-typical-sample tradeoff.** Its best gap is lowest on `network10_s2` (**6.4%**), but that case has the worst median gap (**33.1%**). `network10_s3` is weakest on best gap (**10.3%**) and has a **30.5%** median gap. Thus, its strong best samples do not imply equally strong typical samples.
- **Construction cost and topology count do not explain the score differences.** Generation 135 and several alternatives build in about **0.00s** and produce 64 distinct topologies per instance. The largest differences are in reward quality and resulting gaps, rather than build time or reported diversity.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep two-hop path strength narrowly around `0.56`.** Test `0.50` and `0.62`, keeping the direct reward, path-quality bounds, and pressure discounts fixed. This isolates whether the best program’s selective path support is slightly too strong or weak without expanding the route model.

2. **Tune edge-pressure discounting independently.** Compare the current denominator factor `1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure` against `1 + 0.85 * edge_pressure` and `(1 + edge_pressure) ** 1.5`, while leaving node pressure unchanged. Congestion-aware discounts are part of the strongest pattern, but stronger discounts should be tested carefully because related variants have not displaced the leader.

3. **Adjust the relay-pressure floor in small steps.** Try `0.65` and `0.85` instead of `0.75`, changing no other path calculation. This tests whether paths through low-pressure relays are being over- or under-supported while preserving the current compact, pressure-aware design.

4. **Test a modest cap on aggregate two-hop support per OD pair.** After calculating an OD pair’s path weights, proportionally rescale them only when their total magnitude exceeds `0.75` of that pair’s direct-link reward. This preserves the relative ranking of paths and is a restrained alternative to the more aggressive capped route model, which hurt solution quality.

5. **Slightly increase the direct reward’s concavity without changing its linear dominance.** Compare `0.75 * value + 0.25 * sqrt(value)` with the current `0.85 * value + 0.15 * sqrt(value)`, keeping all path terms fixed. The existing blend is a key strength, and this narrow test checks whether giving moderate-demand links somewhat more influence can improve typical or weakest-case results.
