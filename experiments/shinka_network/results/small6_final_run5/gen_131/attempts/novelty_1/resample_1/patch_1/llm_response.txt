<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the uniform reciprocal-link charge with a demand-weighted two-node cut estimate. Traffic leaving or entering a node pair must cross that pair’s boundary; reciprocal links reduce the available boundary links. A second-order expansion of inverse squared boundary capacity therefore gives a pairwise penalty that emphasizes cuts carrying more traffic. Centering the associated linear terms by endpoint effects preserves their ranking contribution across valid degree-two topologies, while reusing the existing reciprocal couplings keeps the QUBO compact.
</DESCRIPTION>

    # Approximate two-node cut congestion. Reciprocal links reduce the
    # boundary-link count, so the same external traffic is carried by fewer
    # links. The quadratic coefficients are the second-order expansion of
    # inverse squared boundary capacity around four available links.
    row_sum = normalized.sum(axis=1)
    col_sum = normalized.sum(axis=0)
    incident_scale = max(
        float(np.max(np.maximum(row_sum, col_sum)), 1e-12)
    )
    cut_linear = np.zeros((n, n), dtype=float)
    cut_strength = 0.80 / 144.0
    expansion_scale = float(np.clip((n / 12.0) ** 0.15, 0.90, 1.11))

    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i + 1, n):
            mutual_support = float(
                np.sqrt(normalized[i, j] * normalized[j, i])

            external = max(
                0.0,
                max(
                    float(row_sum[i] + row_sum[j]),
