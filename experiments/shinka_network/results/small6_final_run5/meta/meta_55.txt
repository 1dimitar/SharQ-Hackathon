# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: Pressure-Discounted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate**
- **Implementation**: Normalizes nonnegative, non-self demand and assigns negative linear weights using a demand/square-root blend. For each positive-demand pair, it selects a small set of two-hop routes by pressure-adjusted affinity, adds a low-pressure alternative, and returns a fixed penalty weight of 1.25.
- **Performance**: Combined score is 0.89 (raw 0.83, post-processed 0.96); mean best and median gaps are 11.66% and 32.42%, with 91% feasibility and 383 quadratic terms on average.
- **Feedback**: The surrogate builds quickly and produces consistently strong post-processed results, reducing the mean gap to 4.38%. Before repair, median gaps remain around 29–37%, and the largest best-sample gap is 16.8% for network14_s0, so its strongest contribution is as a head start for the fixed post-processor.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 8 remains the strongest established balance, and the current best result.** Its combined score is **0.9003** (raw **0.8398**, post-processor **0.9607**), with **96% feasibility**, **467 quadratic terms**, and a **4.12% mean repaired gap**. Its sparse, demand-supported two-hop routes, pressure-adjusted intermediate selection, and reciprocal-link penalty deliver a strong balance of sample quality and repair-ready solutions.
- **Sparse pressure-aware routing repeatedly produces competitive head starts.** Generation 50 (Pressure-Aware Sparse Network QUBO) and Generation 51 (Demand-Aware Sparse Routing QUBO) both score **0.90**, with repaired gaps of **3.65%** and **4.14%**, respectively. Generation 50 pairs **98% feasibility** with just **463 terms**; Generation 51 uses **823 terms** and has **89% feasibility**. These results reinforce that pressure-aware route selection can yield good repaired candidates without requiring a dense route model.
- **The current best’s targeted coverage and demand blend remain effective relative to more expansive variants.** It uses \(0.75\) normalized demand plus \(0.25\) square-root demand for direct rewards, and selects two or four affinity-ranked intermediates plus one pressure-discounted alternative. This produces **467 terms** and a **4.12%** repaired gap. By comparison, the related Generation 51 adds limited three-hop alternatives, raising the mean to **823 terms** without improving the rounded **0.90** score or repaired gap.
- **Repair-ready candidates are a consistent strength, but Generation 8 retains an especially strong overall balance.** Its mean gap falls from **10.83%** before repair to **4.12%** afterward. New programs also show substantial repair gains: Generation 50 reports **3.65%** and Generation 54 **4.22%** repaired gaps. Those lower or similar repaired gaps do not, by themselves, establish a better combined result than Generation 8.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **More elaborate route modeling has not established an overall improvement over Generation 8.** Generation 52’s soft routing and congestion-pressure terms score **0.87**, with **14.02%** mean best gap and **5.45%** repaired gap, despite **100% feasibility**. Generation 53’s adaptive route expansion, interaction adjustments, and diversity penalties score **0.895**, below Generation 8’s **0.9003**.
- **Adding route alternatives can increase model size without improving the score.** Generation 51’s limited three-hop additions accompany **823 terms**, versus Generation 8’s **467**, while both round to **0.90** and Generation 51 has lower feasibility (**89%** versus **96%**). Earlier Generation 48 likewise expanded coverage to about **519.5 terms** without surpassing Generation 8’s overall result.
- **High feasibility or a low repaired gap alone does not imply stronger raw sample quality.** Generation 52 reaches **100% feasibility** but has a **14.02%** mean best gap and scores **0.87**. Generation 50’s **3.65%** repaired gap is better than Generation 8’s **4.12%**, but its feedback still reports pre-repair best gaps of **7.5–16.6%** and weaker quality on larger networks. The metrics favor balanced performance rather than optimizing only feasibility or repair outcomes.
- **Raw ranking remains a recurring weakness across variants.** Generation 8’s median gap is **28.72%**, while the new summaries report median gaps around **26–40%** for Generations 50–53. Their post-processors greatly reduce those gaps, but the typical sampled topology remains substantially weaker than the best repaired candidate.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 8’s demand preparation is compact and directly tied to its reward construction.** It clips negative traffic, zeros the diagonal, normalizes by the largest positive value, and blends normalized and square-root demand for direct-link rewards. This avoids self-demand and negative entries affecting the model while moderating the dominance of large traffic values.
- **Its route interactions are sparse and demand-supported.** For each positive-demand OD pair, it ranks intermediates by edge affinity discounted by node pressure to the \(0.75\) power, keeps two or four candidates according to a demand quantile, and adds one alternative selected by pressure discount. The resulting **467 terms** are materially fewer than Generation 51’s **823**, while retaining a comparable overall score.
- **The reciprocal-link penalty is a focused structural correction in the current best.** Generation 8 penalizes paired \(i\to j\) and \(j\to i\) links, scales the penalty with network depth, and reduces it when both directions have substantial demand. Its complete implementation has the best established combined score; the evaluation does not isolate the penalty’s causal effect, but newer interaction and route-expansion adjustments have not shown a better overall balance.
- **Pressure-aware alternatives demonstrate several ways to control model growth, with different tradeoffs.** Generation 50 selects sparse two-hop routes using cached route evidence and transit-pressure estimates and averages **463 terms**; Generation 54 uses damped softmax routing and keeps the best two or three intermediates, averaging about **404 terms**. Both score **0.90**, but Generation 8 achieves a more precisely reported **0.9003** with **467 terms**, showing that the current best’s moderate coverage is competitive with these smaller constructions.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 8 remains the current best on combined score:** **0.9003**, comprising raw **0.8398** and post-processor **0.9607**. Its **96% feasibility**, **10.83% mean best gap**, and **4.12% mean repaired gap** provide a balanced reference for the new evaluations.
- **The new Generation 50 and 51 results match the rounded score but do not displace Generation 8.** Both score **0.90**; Generation 50 has a better reported repaired gap (**3.65%**) and similar model size (**463** versus **467** terms), while Generation 51 has **4.14%** repaired gap, **89% feasibility**, and **823 terms**. The reported rounded scores and differing metric tradeoffs do not demonstrate a superior overall result.
- **Other recent variants trail on combined performance despite strengths in individual metrics.** Generation 52 scores **0.87** with **100% feasibility** and **5.45%** repaired gap; Generation 53 scores **0.895** with **99% feasibility**, **602 terms**, and **4.49%** repaired gap; Generation 54 scores **0.90** with **95% feasibility** and **4.22%** repaired gap. These results are competitive, but none provides a reported combined score above Generation 8’s **0.9003**.
- **Generation 8’s instance results show consistent repair gains but expose size- and case-specific raw weaknesses.** Its largest raw gap is **14.5% on network15_s0**, and its largest repaired gap is **7.35% on network13_s1**; the per-size sample-quality score is lowest at **n=13 (0.833)**, while head-start is lowest there as well (**0.942**). This aligns with the broader pattern: post-processing often yields strong outcomes, but raw sample ranking and occasional larger-network or instance outliers remain weaker.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add one ambiguity-triggered two-hop candidate per OD pair.** Preserve the current two-or-four affinity-ranked routes and pressure-discounted alternative, but add at most one more route when the next-best affinity score is close to the last selected score. This tests whether limited flexibility helps uncertain pairs without the large term increase seen with broader route expansion.

2. **Require demand support on both legs of selected routes.** Keep the current OD-demand reward and pressure discount, then multiply each selected two-hop reward by a bounded factor based on normalized demand for \(i\to j\) and \(j\to k\). This favors routes whose component links have independent traffic support while retaining the sparse, demand-aware construction that performs well.

3. **Moderately discount overused intermediates in a second pass.** After route selection, accumulate demand-weighted usage for each intermediate and slightly reduce rewards for routes through the most-used nodes. Keep the adjustment local and mild: pressure-aware routing has been competitive, while more elaborate global interaction modeling has not consistently improved the combined score.

4. **Try one supported three-hop path only for difficult OD pairs.** Add a path with two distinct intermediates only when the best two-hop options are weak or closely ranked, require positive normalized demand on all three legs, and discount by pressure at both intermediates. Cap this at one extra path per OD pair to test limited flexibility without repeating the term growth of broader three-hop variants.

5. **Make the reciprocal-link penalty selectively route-aware.** Retain the current size-scaled baseline and its mutual-demand weakening, but modestly increase the penalty for directed links that frequently appear in selected route rewards. This extends a focused structural correction in the best program while avoiding a global penalty increase whose benefit is not established.
