# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 8, remains a strong quality–size balance.** Its combined score is **0.90** (sample quality **0.8398**, head-start **0.9607**), with **96% feasibility**, a **4.12% mean post-processed gap**, and about **467 quadratic terms**. Its normalized direct-demand rewards, sparse two-hop routes, and reciprocal-link penalty together support good repair-ready candidates without a dense model.
- **Sparse, demand-supported two-hop routes with pressure-aware discounts are consistently effective.** Generation 20, *Pressure-Discounted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate*, also scores **0.90**, with a **3.82% post gap** and **86% feasibility**. Generations 21, 23, 24, and 25 score **0.89–0.90**, with post gaps of **3.83–5.04%**. These results reinforce the value of using demand evidence to select routes while discounting congested transit nodes.
- **Repair-ready candidates matter more than raw sample quality alone.** Generation 8’s **10.83% mean best gap** falls to **4.12%** after repair. Generation 20 likewise reports **11.42%** raw best gap and **3.82%** post gap; Generation 23 reports **13.19%** and **3.83%**. High head-start performance persists despite imperfect raw topology quality.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Adding route complexity has not reliably improved results.** Generation 24 adds limited three-hop rewards yet scores **0.90** with **660 mean terms** and a **4.04% post gap**—a solid result, but no clear improvement over Generation 8’s **4.12%** gap at roughly **467 terms**. Earlier, Generation 16’s selected three-hop terms also failed to improve on the sparse best, with a **4.53%** post gap and weak raw median quality.
- **Denser interaction models do not guarantee better samples or scores.** Generation 23 produces **906–3,881** quadratic terms and has a **33.46% median gap**, while scoring **0.89**. This contrasts with Generation 8’s roughly **467 terms** and **0.90** score. Earlier dense models, including Generations 18 and 19, likewise underperformed the sparse best.
- **Pressure-aware selection alone does not eliminate feasibility or difficult-instance weaknesses.** Generation 20 has only **86% mean feasibility**, despite its strong post gap, and Generation 21’s largest raw best gap is **15.5%**. In Generation 8, raw best gaps reach **14.5%** on network15_s0, while post gaps reach **7.35%** on network13_s1. Repair remains important, and aggregate results hide instance-level variation.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 8’s direct rewards are carefully scaled.** It clips negative demand, clears the diagonal, normalizes by maximum demand, and combines normalized demand with its square root using **0.75/0.25** weights. This preserves reward for moderate-demand links while keeping terms comparable; the program achieves **0.90** with about **467 quadratic terms**.
- **Its two-hop selection balances route affinity and congestion.** For each positive-demand pair, it ranks intermediates using edge affinity divided by node pressure to the power **0.75**, retains two or four candidates based on demand, and adds one alternative selected by pressure discount. This creates a sparse, demand-supported route set; the resulting repaired mean gap is **4.12%**.
- **The reciprocal-link penalty is a targeted structural correction.** Generation 8 penalizes \(i \to j\) paired with \(j \to i\), which can spend scarce expansion on backtracking, while reducing the penalty for substantial mutual demand. Its modest depth scaling accompanies **96% feasibility** and a **0.9607 head-start score**.
- **The newer programs vary route scoring and penalties, but do not establish a consistent improvement over Generation 8.** Generation 20 uses affinity, node pressure, rank gaps, and a pressure-favored alternative with a fixed **1.25** penalty; it matches the **0.90** combined score and improves the post gap to **3.82%**, but has lower feasibility. Generation 25 uses top-two-or-three route couplings, redundancy and pressure adjustments, and a fixed **1.15** penalty; it scores **0.89** with a **5.04%** post gap.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 8 remains among the best overall results and retains the strongest compact-model balance in the comparisons.** Its **0.90** score matches Generations 17, 20, and 24. Generation 17 has **98% feasibility** and a **4.04%** post gap; Generation 20 has a **3.82%** post gap but **86% feasibility**; Generation 24 has a **4.04%** post gap and **94% feasibility**, at **660 terms**. Generation 8 combines **96% feasibility**, a **4.12%** post gap, and about **467 terms**.
- **The current best’s repaired results are strong but instance-dependent.** Its post gaps range from **0.94%** on network12_s1 to **7.35%** on network13_s1; its worst raw best gap is **14.5%** on network15_s0. Across sizes, sample-quality scores range from **0.828 to 0.850**, while head-start scores range from **0.942 to 0.979**.
- **Generations 21, 23, and 25 have competitive repair performance but lower combined scores.** Generation 21 scores **0.89** with **95% feasibility** and a **4.17%** post gap; Generation 23 scores **0.89** with **100% feasibility** and a **3.83%** post gap; Generation 25 scores **0.89** with **96% feasibility** and a **5.04%** post gap. Their results reinforce strong post-processing performance, but do not surpass Generation 8’s combined score.
- **Quadratic-term count is not a reliable proxy for quality.** Generation 8 achieves **0.90** with about **467 terms**, while Generation 24 also reaches **0.90** with **660 terms**. Generation 23 uses far more terms yet scores **0.89**, and previous dense generations scored below the best. The evaluations favor selective route interactions over density alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Retain more two-hop candidates only when their scores are genuinely ambiguous.** For each endpoint pair, compare the top affinity-to-pressure scores and keep a small additional candidate when the leading scores are close; keep fewer when the top route clearly dominates. This tests a targeted way to improve route coverage while preserving Generation 8’s sparse model.

2. **Refine route rewards using support from both legs.** Multiply each endpoint-demand-supported two-hop reward by a bounded factor based on the normalized demands on \(i\to j\) and \(j\to k\), while retaining the existing node-pressure discount. This could avoid rewarding routes with a weak leg without abandoning the sparse, demand-supported routing pattern that performs well.

3. **Estimate congestion from the selected routes, then apply a modest second-pass discount.** Accumulate each selected intermediate node’s weighted transit demand and slightly reduce rewards for routes through the most heavily loaded nodes. Keep the adjustment bounded so it refines the existing pressure-aware ranking rather than overwhelming direct-demand and route-affinity rewards.

4. **Test a single three-hop alternative only for difficult endpoint pairs.** Add one three-hop route when endpoint demand is high but the best two-hop candidates are weak or closely tied, scoring it by support on all legs and pressure at its transit nodes. Earlier broad three-hop models did not clearly beat the compact best, so keep this addition tightly capped.

5. **Increase constraint penalties selectively for highly coupled links.** Count how often each link appears in selected route-reward terms and modestly strengthen its constraint penalty only for the most frequently coupled links, leaving the baseline near Generation 8’s size-scaled setting. This targets feasibility weaknesses such as the 91% rate on network15_s2 without uniformly suppressing useful rewards.
