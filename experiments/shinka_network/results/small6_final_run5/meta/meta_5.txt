# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Demand-aware sparse two-hop structure is the strongest overall pattern.** The current best, **Demand-Aware Sparse Two-Hop QUBO** (Generation 4), scores **0.90** overall (**0.83 raw, 0.96 post-processed**), narrowly exceeding the earlier pressure-discounted programs at **0.89**. It combines demand-weighted direct-link rewards with sparse route couplings, endpoint affinity, and transit-pressure ranking.
- **Balancing direct-link rewards with indirect-route incentives works better than relying on route selection alone.** Generation 4 uses demand-dependent support for two-hop routes and reduces their reward when direct demand is already strong. Its weak positive interactions between a direct link and the route’s legs model redundancy. It achieves a **4.04% mean post-processor gap**, compared with **6.32%** for Generation 3’s Sparse Congestion-Aware Two-Hop QUBO.
- **Pressure discounting and alternative routes consistently provide a strong post-processing head start.** Generations 0–2 scored **0.89** and had post-processor gaps around **4.20–4.47%**. The current best retains pressure-aware route ranking while adding demand-dependent support and redundancy interactions; its post-processor gap is **4.04%**.
- **Sparse route couplings can preserve good results while keeping construction cheap.** The current best builds in roughly **0.00–0.01s** for nearly all cases (one reported 0.18s), and produces **64 distinct topologies per case**. Its average of **660 quadratic terms** is higher than Generation 3’s 252.5, but it remains far below exhaustive pairwise route enumeration.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **A fixed, small set of route couplings can leave the raw sample quality weak.** Even the current best has an **11.19% mean best-sample gap** and a **30.39% mean median gap**. Its per-instance best gaps range from **7.1% to 15.5%**, with the largest at **network15_s1**. The sampled topologies are therefore much less effective before repair than after it.
- **Adding congestion awareness alone did not improve the overall score.** Generation 3’s congestion-aware selection, demand-dependent support, and degree penalty produced **0.86** combined score and a **6.32% post-processing gap**, below Generation 4’s **0.90** and **4.04%**. Its lower coupling count (252.5 versus 660) coincided with weaker post-processed results.
- **A fixed penalty and otherwise strong surrogate terms do not eliminate feasibility variation.** The pressure-discounted programs used a fixed penalty of **1.25** and reported feasibility as low as **91%** in some summaries. Generation 4’s fixed **1.15** penalty reached **94% mean feasibility**, but still ranged from **88%** on network13_s1 to 100% on several cases; penalty choice alone does not guarantee uniformly valid samples.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 4’s input sanitization makes the scoring logic robust:** it replaces NaN and infinite values, clips negative demand, and zeros the diagonal before computing normalized traffic. It then uses a max-demand scale, row/column shares, and incident pressure to derive stable affinity and congestion features.
- **Route ranking combines endpoint affinity with pressure discounts.** For each positive-demand pair, Generation 4 scores candidate intermediates using row-share and column-share affinities plus normalized edge evidence, discounted by node pressure. It keeps two routes for smaller demands and three for demands above the positive-demand 68th percentile.
- **Direct and indirect terms are explicitly balanced.** The direct score blends normalized demand, its square root, and endpoint-share affinity. Two-hop support is demand-weighted but reduced for strong direct demand; route couplings are negative rewards, while weak positive direct-to-leg interactions encode redundancy. This richer structure is associated with the best post-processing gap among the evaluated programs.
- **The post-processor is a major contributor to final quality across programs.** For Generation 4, mean gap falls from **11.19% best-sample** to **4.04% post-processed**; individual examples include network10_s2 improving from **8.4% to 1.83%** and network13_s0 from **10.1% to 2.85%**. This mirrors earlier pressure-discounted results, where post-processing reduced gaps to roughly 2.16–7.38%.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 4 is the best evaluated program by combined score:** **0.90**, versus **0.89** for Generations 0–2 and **0.86** for Generation 3. Its raw score (**0.83**) matches the strongest reported raw score, while its post-processed score (**0.96**) matches the top reported value.
- **Its clearest advantage is post-processed quality, not pre-repair sample quality.** Generation 4’s **4.04%** mean post-processing gap improves on the pressure-discounted programs’ approximately **4.20–4.47%** and Generation 3’s **6.32%**. Its mean best gap (**11.19%**) is also strong relative to prior pressure-discounted summaries around **11.4–12.0%**, but its median remains poor at **30.39%**.
- **Performance weakens somewhat at larger network sizes.** The sample-quality component is strongest at n=10 (**0.855**) and lower at n=16 (**0.821**); mean best gaps are especially high for network15_s1 (**15.5%**) and network16_s1 (**15.4%**). Despite this, post-processing remains comparatively stable, with individual gaps from **1.83% to 5.69%**.
- **More couplings correlate with better repair results here, but not automatically with better raw sampling.** Generation 4 averages **660** quadratic terms and outperforms Generation 3’s **252.5** terms on combined score and post-processing gap. Yet its median gap remains **30.39%**, confirming that coupling count and post-repair performance do not translate directly into uniformly strong sampled topologies.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adaptively vary the number of candidate routes per demand pair.** The current program keeps two or three intermediates using a global demand percentile, and its sparse two-hop structure is associated with the best post-processed gap. Try selecting route count from both demand strength and the score margin between ranked candidates—for example, retain extra routes when the top candidates are close, but keep fewer when one route clearly dominates.

2. **Improve transit-pressure estimates with route-specific load.** Preserve the successful pressure discount, but estimate how much traffic a node would receive as an intermediate from the selected routes, then discount candidates that are already heavily loaded by those routes. This could make route rankings more informative than pressure based only on total generated or received demand, while keeping the coupling graph sparse.

3. **Tune direct-versus-indirect balance by endpoint affinity.** The current demand-aware support and redundancy interactions improve on route-only and congestion-only approaches. Test making the indirect reward and direct-to-leg redundancy terms depend on how strongly the endpoints already share traffic with the candidate’s two legs, so routes with strong evidence receive more support while weakly evidenced alternatives contribute less.

4. **Add a small, targeted set of three-hop alternatives for difficult pairs.** Two-hop routes are the proven foundation, so retain them and add three-hop route couplings only for pairs with weak top-ranked two-hop options or very high demand. Rank these alternatives using the same endpoint-affinity and pressure-discount logic, and cap their number to avoid the cost and dilution of exhaustive route enumeration.

5. **Address the raw-sample weakness with targeted feasibility-aware penalties.** Best-sample gaps remain substantial and feasibility varies by instance, despite strong post-processing results. Try scaling the constraint penalty modestly with network size or estimated variable degree, or applying a larger penalty only to variables involved in many selected route couplings; compare feasibility and raw quality to avoid overwhelming the useful demand and route rewards.
