# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 8, remains the strongest quality–size balance.** Its combined score is **0.90** (sample quality **0.8398**, head-start **0.9607**), with **96% feasibility**, a **4.12% mean post-processed gap**, and about **467 quadratic terms**. Its normalized direct rewards, sparse demand-supported two-hop routes, and reciprocal-link penalty yield a compact model and strong repair-ready candidates.
- **Sparse, demand-informed routing is consistently competitive, but newer variants do not surpass Generation 8 overall.** Generation 28’s *Adaptive Sparse Surrogate QUBO* also scores **0.90**, with **0.84 raw** and **0.96 post** scores, **96% feasibility**, and a **4.55% post gap** using **447 terms**. Its demand/affinity/pressure route selection and size-scaled degree penalty are close to the best’s balance, though its post gap is higher.
- **Pressure-aware route selection plus the fixed post-processor produces strong repaired results.** Generation 29’s *Pressure-Aware Sparse QUBO Surrogate* has a **4.51% post gap** and **98% feasibility**; Generation 27’s *Pressure-Aware Surrogate QUBO* has a **5.02% post gap** with **100% feasibility**. These reinforce the usefulness of sparse, pressure-discounted two-hop routes, even when raw samples are imperfect.
- **Repair-ready quality is a recurring strength across the leading programs.** Generation 8 improves from a **10.83% mean best-sample gap** to **4.12%** after repair. Generation 28 similarly has an **11.99%** raw best gap and **4.55%** post gap. The evaluated post-processor substantially improves the practical outcome across these approaches.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Adding more route complexity has not delivered a clear improvement.** Generation 22’s *Multi-Hop Coverage and Sparse Route QUBO* uses Sinkhorn-balanced routing, discounted walks, centered curvature, and sparse route-completion rewards, but scores **0.88** with a **4.78% post gap** and **2,469 terms**. Generation 26’s targeted three-hop variant scores **0.89**, but still has a **33.75% median raw gap** and **393 terms**; its post gap is **4.67%**. Neither outperforms Generation 8’s **0.90** score and **4.12%** post gap.
- **Three-hop alternatives can improve repaired results without fixing raw sample quality.** Generation 26’s largest best-sample gap is **18.7%** on network13_s2, despite its targeted high-demand three-hop option. Its **13.33%** mean best gap and **33.75%** median gap remain weak relative to the repaired score.
- **More quadratic interactions do not reliably improve quality.** Generation 22’s **2,469 mean terms** accompany a lower **0.88** score than Generation 8’s roughly **467 terms** and **0.90**. Generation 29 produces only **272–630 terms** and gets a **0.89** score, while Generation 27 uses **846–3,702 terms** for **0.88**. Across these results, term count alone does not predict a better score.
- **Pressure-aware scoring alone does not eliminate weak raw samples or repair dependence.** Generations 26–29 report median raw gaps around **28–34%** and best-sample gaps around **12–14%** on average. Their post-processor reduces gaps substantially, but Generation 8 also has this pattern and retains the best overall score among the listed programs.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 8’s direct rewards are carefully scaled.** It clips negative demand, clears the diagonal, normalizes by maximum traffic, and combines normalized demand with its square root using **0.75/0.25** weights. This gives moderate-demand links a meaningful reward without allowing demand magnitude to dominate; the result is **0.90** combined score with about **467 terms**.
- **Its two-hop selection combines affinity and congestion rather than using either alone.** For each positive-demand pair, it ranks intermediates by edge affinity divided by node pressure to the **0.75** power, keeps two or four candidates depending on demand, and adds one pressure-discounted alternative. This sparse route set accompanies a **4.12%** post gap and **0.9607** head-start score.
- **The reciprocal-link penalty is a targeted structural correction.** Generation 8 penalizes paired \(i \to j\) and \(j \to i\) links, which can waste expansion on backtracking, but reduces that penalty when both directions have substantial demand. Its modest depth scaling supports **96% feasibility** without a dense interaction model.
- **The newer variants explore useful components but show no consistent replacement for this combination.** Generation 28 uses demand/affinity/pressure-based route coupling, a low-pressure fallback, backtrack penalties, and a network-size-scaled degree penalty; it reaches **0.90** with **447 terms**, but its post gap is **4.55%**. Generations 26–27 use fixed **1.25** penalties and pressure-aware routes, achieving post gaps of **4.67%** and **5.02%**, respectively.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 8 remains the top overall reference among the evaluated programs.** Its **0.90** score and **4.12%** mean post gap compare favorably with Generations 22 and 27, both at **0.88**, and Generations 26 and 29, both at **0.89**. Generation 28 matches its **0.90** score but has a slightly higher **4.55%** post gap.
- **The best program’s performance is robust but varies by instance.** Its raw best gaps range from **7.4%** (network10_s2) to **14.5%** (network15_s0); post-processing brings the gaps to **0.94–7.35%**. Sample-quality scores by size range from **0.828 to 0.850**, and head-start scores from **0.942 to 0.979**.
- **Post-processing is a major contributor to effective solution quality across programs.** Generation 8’s mean gap falls from **10.83%** to **4.12%**. Generation 26 falls from a **13.33% best-sample gap** to a **4.67% post gap**, and Generation 28 from **11.99%** to **4.55%**. The high post scores therefore coexist with noticeably weaker raw-sample quality.
- **Compactness is compatible with strong performance, while density is not a reliable advantage.** Generation 8 uses about **467 terms** for a **0.90** score; Generation 28 uses **447 terms** and also scores **0.90**. In contrast, Generation 22’s much denser model scores **0.88**, and Generation 27’s broad term-count range does not prevent the same **0.88** score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add route candidates adaptively when the top affinity-to-pressure scores are close.** Keep the current two-or-four-candidate rule as the baseline, but retain one additional intermediate when the leading scores are within a small relative margin. This tests whether a little extra coverage can improve the repaired gaps without the term-count growth seen in dense routing models.

2. **Discount two-hop rewards when either leg has weak demand support.** Multiply the existing endpoint-demand support by a bounded factor based on the normalized demands on \(i\to j\) and \(j\to k\), while preserving the current node-pressure discount and sparse candidate selection. This targets poorly supported shortcuts without replacing the direct-reward scaling that contributes to the best program’s strong results.

3. **Apply a modest second-pass congestion adjustment to selected routes.** After selecting routes, accumulate their weighted use of each intermediate node and slightly discount routes through the most heavily used nodes. Keep the adjustment bounded so it refines, rather than overrides, the existing affinity and pressure ranking.

4. **Try one three-hop alternative only for endpoint pairs with weak or ambiguous two-hop options.** Require positive endpoint demand and support on all three legs, and discount the reward by pressure at both intermediate nodes. Earlier three-hop approaches did not improve raw quality consistently, so cap this to one alternative per qualifying pair to preserve the compact model.

5. **Strengthen constraint penalties selectively for heavily coupled links.** Count how often each link appears in route-reward interactions and apply a small additional penalty only to the most frequently coupled links, retaining the current size-scaled baseline elsewhere. This tests a targeted way to improve feasibility on weaker cases such as network15_s2 without uniformly suppressing useful rewards.
