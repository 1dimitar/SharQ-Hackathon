# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 28 (“Pressure-Aware Sparse Two-Hop QUBO”), remains the leader:** score **0.85**, mean best gap **10.86%**, and median gap **25.17%**. Its strongest pattern is the combination of demand-calibrated direct-link rewards with targeted, pressure-aware relay rewards—not simply maximizing the number of relay terms.
- **A concave blend of linear and square-root demand rewards appears to preserve useful signal across demand levels.** Generation 28 uses `-(0.75*d + 0.25*sqrt(d))` for direct links and a matching blend for OD support. Its best gaps were **+9.5%** on `network12_s0` and **+12.3%** on `network12_s1`. The newly evaluated Programs 79–84 did not surpass those results; their best gaps ranged from **11.46% to 18.19%**.
- **Selective relay allocation with pressure-aware ranking is the strongest observed relay strategy.** Generation 28 ranks relays by leg affinity discounted by node pressure and adds up to two relays for high-demand OD pairs above the 80th-percentile cutoff. This achieved **304 quadratic terms** and a **25.17% median gap**, better than the new variants’ **30.75–40.20%** median gaps.
- **Moderate term counts and topology exploration can coexist with better quality.** Generation 28 produced 80–86 distinct topologies and led on best and median quality. This reinforces that useful relay selection matters more than either aggressive sparsification or increasing interactions without better selection.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **The newly tested relay variants all underperformed Generation 28.** Generation 84 (“Pressure-Aware Sparse Two-Hop QUBO”) was closest, scoring **0.84** with an **11.46%** best gap; Programs 79–83 scored **0.78–0.82**, with best gaps from **14.34% to 18.19%**. None improved the best program’s score or best gap.
- **Changing relay budgets or adding candidates did not reliably improve outcomes.** Program 82 (“Demand-Weighted Direct and Relay QUBO Surrogate”) used extra candidates for high-demand OD pairs but had only **4%** mean pre-repair feasibility and a **30.75%** median gap. Program 80 used **352 quadratic terms**, yet its **34.96%** median gap and **15%** feasibility were worse than Generation 28.
- **Fixed penalties and delegated degree penalties did not resolve feasibility problems.** Program 79 used a fixed penalty of **1.25** and achieved **12%** feasibility; Program 84 used **1.5** and achieved **24%**, but still had the largest per-instance feasibility disparity (**45% versus 4%**). Generation 28 also has low feasibility (**17%**), so feasibility remains a broader weakness rather than a problem solved by any evaluated penalty setup.
- **Pressure-adjusted relay selection alone is insufficient for consistent quality.** Program 83 had a **15.38%** mean best gap and only **7%** and **26%** pre-repair validity on the two instances. Program 81’s pressure-adjusted sparse choices yielded a **14.34%** best gap and **34.96%** median gap, despite using more terms than Generation 28.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 28’s direct-link construction is concrete and balanced:** it clips negative demand, removes self-traffic, normalizes by the maximum, then assigns each off-diagonal link the reward `-(0.75*d + 0.25*sqrt(d))`. This supports both large and moderate demands and accompanies the best reported score and gaps.
- **Its relay ranking combines leg affinity and pressure:** the ranking uses `sqrt((0.1+a)*(0.1+b)) / (0.75 + pressure[j])`, followed by a mild rank factor and an additional pressure discount in the quadratic weight. The measured result—**0.85** with **304 terms**—outperforms the newly evaluated pressure-aware variants, indicating that the full reward and selection construction matters, not pressure adjustment by itself.
- **The demand-selective budget is a distinguishing feature.** Generation 28 starts with a base relay count and adds up to two relays for OD pairs at or above the positive-demand 80th percentile. By comparison, fixed or otherwise sparse choices in Programs 79–84 did not match its quality; even Program 84’s **264 terms** produced an **11.46%** best gap and **26.71%** median gap.
- **Feasibility and objective quality are separate concerns in these results.** Generation 28’s **17%** mean validity is low, but its best and median gaps remain better than the new programs. The latter also show that neither term count nor feasibility alone predicts solution quality: Program 82 had the worst mean feasibility (**4%**) and a **14.50%** best gap, while Program 84 reached **24%** feasibility but still did not match Generation 28’s gap metrics.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 28 remains the overall best reported result:** **0.85** score, **10.86%** mean best gap, **25.17%** median gap, and **17%** mean feasibility. The closest new result, Program 84, scored **0.84** with an **11.46%** best gap and **26.71%** median gap.
- **The newer programs’ best gaps were consistently worse than the current best.** Program 79 had an **18.19%** gap; Programs 80, 82, and 83 had **14.34–15.38%** gaps; Program 84 had **11.46%**. Their median gaps (**30.75–40.20%**) also all exceeded Generation 28’s **25.17%**.
- **Generation 28’s case-level results are comparatively balanced:** best gaps were **+9.5%** on `network12_s0` and **+12.3%** on `network12_s1`, with median gaps of **26.5%** and **23.9%**. Several new variants showed larger gaps or severe feasibility variation: Program 84’s validity ranged from **45% to 4%**, while Program 79’s `network12_s1` produced no valid solutions before repair.
- **Quadratic-term count does not explain the ranking.** Generation 28’s **304 terms** accompany the best score; Program 80 used **352** and had worse best and median gaps, while Program 83 used **264** and also underperformed. The observed advantage is associated with Generation 28’s particular combination of demand calibration and selective, pressure-aware relay allocation, not simply with more or fewer interactions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reduce the baseline relay budget while preserving demand-selective extras.** Change `relay_count` to `min(n - 2, max(1, n // 5))`, leaving the 80th-percentile cutoff, two-relay extra cap, candidate ranking, and rewards unchanged. This isolates whether fewer low-priority relay interactions can retain the best program’s quality while reducing its 304 quadratic terms.

2. **Focus extra relay support on the most demanding OD pairs.** Raise `high_demand_cutoff` from the 80th to the 90th percentile, keeping the extra-relay cap and all reward formulas fixed. The current demand-selective allocation is part of the leading design; this tests whether concentrating it further can improve the weaker `network12_s1` result.

3. **Test an affinity that rewards strength on both legs.** Replace the geometric-mean affinity with `min(first_leg, second_leg) + 0.25 * max(first_leg, second_leg)`, while retaining pressure discounts, ranking, budgets, and weights. This checks whether emphasizing balanced two-hop paths improves on the current relay selection without changing its successful demand-calibrated structure.

4. **Tune pressure sensitivity with controlled variants.** Separately raise and lower the exponent in the relay-ranking denominator, using `affinity / (0.75 + pressure[j])**p` for `p = 1.25` and `p = 0.75`; keep the additional pressure discount in the weight unchanged. This isolates whether stronger or gentler pressure avoidance improves relay quality, since pressure-aware selection helps but has not been reliably successful on its own.

5. **Make relay reward strength more selective by OD demand.** Keep the direct-link reward and relay candidate selection unchanged, but multiply each relay weight by `1.1` when its OD demand is at or above the existing 80th-percentile cutoff and by `0.9` otherwise. This tests whether the best program’s calibrated demand signal can be extended to relay interactions without adding more candidates or quadratic terms.
