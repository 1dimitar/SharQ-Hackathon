# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 28 `targeted_high_demand_relays`, remains the strongest evaluated design:** score **0.85**, mean best gap **10.86%**, and mean median gap **25.17%**. Its compact relay support—ordinary relay coverage plus up to two extra relays for high-demand OD pairs—outperformed the newer alternatives, whose scores ranged from **0.75 to 0.84**.
- **Calibrated direct-link rewards paired with pressure-aware, targeted relay ranking are the most consistently successful pattern.** Generation 28 blends normalized demand and its square root as `0.75*value + 0.25*sqrt(value)`, then ranks relays by balanced leg affinity discounted by node pressure. Its **304 quadratic terms** and **0.85** score compare favorably with Generation 51’s **264 terms / 0.84** and Generation 45’s **640 terms / 0.81**.
- **Selective support for high-demand routes appears more effective than uniform relay expansion.** Generation 28 gives high-demand OD pairs up to two additional relays and achieved a **10.86%** mean best gap. Generation 42 and Generation 51 also used sparse, pressure-aware relay selection and scored **0.84**, but neither surpassed the best result; their best gaps were **11.80%** and **11.06%**, respectively.
- **Compactness is useful when interactions are targeted, not as a goal by itself.** Generation 28 scored **0.85** with 304 terms, while Generation 47 scored **0.83** with only 18 terms and Generation 46 scored **0.82** with 880. The results support selective interaction design over either extremely sparse or broad quadratic support.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Fractional congestion estimates and sparse congestion penalties did not improve outcomes.** Generation 41’s fractional two-hop loads and shared-link penalties produced the lowest score, **0.75**, a **22.48%** mean best gap, and **22%** mean feasibility despite 848 terms. Its network12_s0 feasibility was only **13%**.
- **Increasing or changing relay pressure and penalty handling alone did not beat the best design.** Generation 45’s pressure-discounted surrogate with penalty weight **6.0** scored **0.81** and had only **4%** mean pre-repair feasibility. Generation 42 and Generation 51 used penalty weight **1.5** and scored **0.84**, still below Generation 28’s **0.85**.
- **Broader or less selective two-hop rewards remained unreliable.** Generation 44 scored **0.83** with a **13.11%** mean best gap and **12%** feasibility; Generation 45 had a **9.72%** best gap but **41.90%** median gap and only **4%** feasibility. These results show that a good best sample can coexist with poor typical quality and weak raw validity.
- **Improved exploration did not translate into consistently good or feasible solutions.** Generation 42 produced **79–94** distinct topologies and Generation 51 produced **90–99**, but their mean median gaps were **28.85%** and **27.50%**, with mean feasibility of **8%** and **23%**, respectively. Diversity alone was not enough to match the best program’s overall performance.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 28’s direct-link score is supported by the strongest valid evaluation.** It clips negative demand, clears self-traffic, normalizes by maximum demand, and applies `0.75*value + 0.25*sqrt(value)`. The blend emphasizes heavy links while retaining reward for moderate-demand links; the result was a **0.85** score and **10.86%** mean best gap.
- **Its relay ranking combines route affinity with congestion awareness without expanding support indiscriminately.** The ranking uses `sqrt((0.1+a)*(0.1+b)) / (0.75 + node_pressure[j])`; high-demand OD pairs receive up to two extra relays. The resulting **304 quadratic terms** are substantially fewer than Generation 41’s **848** and Generation 45’s **640**, while achieving better score and best-gap results.
- **Canonicalizing quadratic keys and aggregating shared interactions keeps the QUBO consistent.** Generation 28 orders the two directed-edge keys before storing each interaction and sums weights when paths produce the same key. The evaluation does not isolate the effect of this implementation detail, but it supports compact aggregation in the winning design.
- **Feasibility remains a weakness even for the best program.** Generation 28 had **20%** raw validity on network12_s0 and **14%** on network12_s1, with median gaps of **26.5%** and **23.9%**. Its advantage is strongest in best-solution quality, not in consistently feasible sampling.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 28 remains ahead of all newly summarized programs:** its **0.85** score compares with **0.84** for Generations 42 and 51, **0.83** for Generation 44, **0.81** for Generation 45, and **0.75** for Generation 41. Its **10.86%** mean best gap is also lower than the newer programs’ gaps, which range from **9.72%** to **22.48%**; Generation 45’s lower gap comes with notably worse median quality and feasibility.
- **Generation 51 is the closest new comparison, but it does not displace the best.** Its pressure-aware sparse two-hop model scored **0.84**, with an **11.06%** mean best gap, **27.50%** median gap, and **23%** feasibility. Generation 28 scored **0.85**, with **10.86%** best gap and **25.17%** median gap, though its mean feasibility was lower at **17%**.
- **The best program’s case-level results are uneven but strong relative to the new evaluations.** Generation 28’s gaps were **9.5%** on network12_s0 and **12.3%** on network12_s1. Generation 42 reported **9.6–14.1%**, Generation 44 **11.8% and 14.4%**, and Generation 41 reached **27.0%** on network12_s0. The current best still has its larger gap on network12_s1.
- **Quadratic-term count is not a reliable standalone predictor of quality.** Generation 28’s 304 terms outperformed Generation 46’s 880-term design, while Generation 47’s 18-term model scored **0.83** but had a **14.01%** mean best gap. The newer Generation 51 achieved **0.84** with 264 terms, reinforcing that targeting and reward calibration matter more than simply minimizing or maximizing interaction count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep the ordinary relay budget while keeping the ranking, rewards, and high-demand rule fixed.** Compare the current `min(n-2, max(2, n//4))` against `min(n-2, max(1, n//5))` and `min(n-2, n//3)`. Generation 28’s compact targeted support is a strength, so test whether a modestly smaller or larger base budget improves the weaker network12_s1 gap without broadly expanding interactions.

2. **Concentrate extra relay support on more exceptional OD pairs.** Raise the high-demand cutoff from the 80th to the 90th percentile, retaining the cap of two extra relays and all other settings. This tests whether focusing quadratic support more narrowly improves best-solution quality while preserving the selectivity associated with Generation 28’s 304 terms.

3. **Test a bottleneck-sensitive relay ranking.** Replace the geometric-mean affinity with `min(first_leg, second_leg) + 0.25 * max(first_leg, second_leg)`, while retaining pressure discounting and the existing relay budgets. This favors relays with two useful legs rather than a single strong leg, extending the current program’s successful targeted, pressure-aware selection.

4. **Tune pressure sensitivity while preserving relay selection.** Keep the existing ranking unchanged, but replace the reward denominator `(0.75 + node_pressure[j])` with `(0.75 + node_pressure[j])**alpha`, testing `alpha = 0.75` and `1.25` separately in otherwise identical variants. This isolates whether gentler or stronger congestion discounting improves on the current pressure-aware design without changing its support structure.

5. **Calibrate direct-link rewards independently of relay terms.** Test `0.85*value + 0.15*sqrt(value)` and `0.65*value + 0.35*sqrt(value)`, changing only the direct-link blend. Generation 28’s `0.75/0.25` blend is part of the strongest evaluated design; these controlled variants test whether emphasizing heavy demands or preserving more reward for moderate demands can reduce the remaining gap.
