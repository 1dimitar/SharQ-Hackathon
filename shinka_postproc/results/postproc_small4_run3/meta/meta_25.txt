# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 20 “Diverse Energy- and Routing-Guided Search,” is the strongest evaluated program:** it scores **0.99** with a **1.04% mean gap**, improving on the previous best’s **1.41%** mean gap while retaining the same 2,000 LP calls per run. Its **−0.61% best gap** also shows it can beat the best-known reference on an individual run.
- **Combining distinct cheap signals with LP-trained prediction remains effective.** Generation 20’s energy and demand-based routing features are complemented by an online ridge predictor and explicit batch capacity for diverse and uncertain candidates. This multi-signal selection pattern is consistent with the earlier leading program’s advantage over approaches relying on narrower surrogate screening, such as Generation 21 (**0.97 score, 2.67% mean gap**) and Generation 24 (**0.97, 2.81%**).
- **Diverse topology search with degree-preserving moves supports strong results across sizes.** Generation 20 uses two-arc switches, three-arc rotations, and larger perturbations; its mean gaps are **0.34% at n=14** and **0.99% at n=16**, as well as **1.34% at n=13** and **1.48% at n=15**. This reinforces previous evidence that local topology changes plus structural diversity outperform broader exploration without equally effective candidate screening.
- **Explicit exploration capacity appears compatible with solution quality.** Generation 20 reserves batch capacity for diverse and uncertain candidates rather than relying only on exploitation. Its low overall gap and best-known-beating run contrast with Generation 23’s destroy-and-repair search (**0.91 score, 9.78% mean gap**), where randomized refill and archive restarts did not produce similarly consistent quality.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Destroy-and-repair underperformed the move-based, multi-signal searches.** Generation 23’s bottleneck-focused arc removal, recursive refill, annealing, and archive restarts used the full 2,000 LP calls but reached only **0.91 score and 9.78% mean gap**. Gaps were roughly **10–11% for n=14–16**, indicating that archive-guided reconstruction was not enough to prioritize high-quality candidates.
- **Surrogate screening without as strong an integrated selection portfolio remained weaker.** Generation 21 scored **0.97 / 2.67% mean gap**, and Generation 24 scored **0.97 / 2.81%**, compared with Generation 20’s **0.99 / 1.04%**. Both weaker programs use diverse archives and topology operators, but their reported variation and, for Generation 24, high runtime suggest that those components alone do not match the current best’s combined energy, routing, and online-prediction screening.
- **Uncertainty-guided search does not guarantee uniform performance across sizes.** Generation 20 is markedly strongest at **n=14 (+0.34%)**, but averages **+1.34% at n=13** and **+1.48% at n=15**. Its good aggregate therefore coexists with a clear size-dependent gap, rather than indicating equally strong outcomes on every instance class.
- **Exhausting the LP budget does not explain the quality differences.** Generations 20–24 all used **2,000 LP calls**, yet their mean gaps ranged from **1.04%** to **9.78%**. As the earlier comparisons also showed, how candidates are screened and selected is more closely associated with quality than call count alone.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 20’s screening combines useful but different information sources.** It computes nine routing/energy features, including energy, demand-weighted distance, high-end link loads, work concentration by source and target, direct traffic, and unreachable pairs. After a minimum training set, its ridge model predicts LP scores and uncertainty; batch selection also draws from energy, routing-proxy, novelty, and uncertainty rankings. This concrete combination accompanies the best observed result: **0.99 score and 1.04% mean gap**.
- **Its routing features approximate congestion through equal splitting over shortest-path choices.** The code computes batched all-pairs shortest paths, propagates demand backward by distance level, and derives link-load summaries. This adds a demand-driven signal to QUBO energy instead of asking the surrogate alone to rank candidates—a pattern also supported by previous results favoring comprehensive screening over narrower surrogate/dual approaches.
- **Diversity is enforced in both initialization and LP batch selection.** Repaired candidates are interleaved by energy and an independent demand-based proxy, with near-identical starts suppressed; later, archive separation and diverse batch selection limit repeated evaluations of structurally similar networks. The search supplements this with valid two-switches, three-edge rotations, multi-switch perturbations, and partial walks toward archived alternatives.
- **The implementation uses LP information both to guide moves and to select evaluations.** `generate` identifies hot arcs from positive prices, falling back to highly loaded links, and marks moves that affect them. Batch selection separately reserves opportunities for uncertainty and novelty. This builds on the previous best’s finding that LP loads/prices are more useful when incorporated into proposal generation and evaluation allocation, not merely used to score completed candidates.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 20 improves the leading mean gap from the previous best’s 1.41% to 1.04%, with the combined score unchanged at 0.99.** It uses the same **2,000 LP calls** and averages **48.61 seconds**, so the measured gain is in solution quality rather than budget reduction.
- **The current best is strongest at n=14 and stays below a 1.5% mean gap at every reported size.** Its gaps are **1.34% (n=13), 0.34% (n=14), 1.48% (n=15), and 0.99% (n=16)**. It beats Generation 21’s overall **2.67%** and Generation 24’s **2.81%** mean gaps, and is far ahead of Generation 23’s **9.78%**.
- **Seed variation remains visible, even in the strongest program.** For example, Generation 20’s n=13 pool/run feedback includes individual gaps from **+0.40% to +2.40%**, while n=15 includes results from **+0.11% to +2.37%**. The best individual result is **−0.61%**, but the best reported per-Hamiltonian results are still positive: **+0.40% for r2g181** and **+0.63% for r3g128**.
- **Runtime and LP-call totals do not track quality consistently across programs.** Generation 20 averages **48.61 seconds**; Generation 21 averages **44.02 seconds** with a **2.67%** gap, Generation 23 averages **34.07 seconds** with a **9.78%** gap, and Generation 24 averages **101.32 seconds** with a **2.81%** gap. Together with their identical LP budgets, these results reinforce that screening and candidate quality—not simply time spent or evaluations consumed—distinguish the leading search.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Allocate LP batch slots adaptively using recent screening performance.** Track whether energy, routing proxy, ridge prediction, uncertainty, and novelty selections actually produce improvements, then modestly increase slots for signals that have been productive while retaining a minimum slot for each. This extends the current best’s complementary screening portfolio without letting one signal crowd out the others.

2. **Add targeted hot-arc switches to the existing move generator.** For arcs flagged by positive LP prices or high loads, explicitly pair each hot arc with compatible alternatives and generate valid degree-preserving switches that remove it; keep the current untargeted switches, rotations, and perturbations as well. This makes bottleneck information directly shape proposals, complementing its existing use in candidate ranking and evaluation.

3. **Improve routing-feature robustness across tied shortest paths.** Compute routing summaries under several deterministic tie-breakings or randomized shortest-path splits, then add a high-percentile peak-load feature to the existing fractional-routing features. Use it as one additional ranking signal rather than a hard filter, preserving the energy- and predictor-guided diversity that accompanies the best results.

4. **Strengthen archive coverage across quality signals.** Maintain separate quotas for LP elites, energy/routing-proxy leaders, and structurally novel networks, and choose parents across these groups—especially after repeated stalls. This builds on the current distance-filtered archive and diverse initialization while reducing the risk that a single ranking or search region dominates exploration.

5. **Adapt perturbation scale to parent progress and instance size.** After a parent stalls, increase its long-walk or archive-relinking proposals; after an improvement, emphasize local switches around the new incumbent, while retaining the current rotation and multi-switch options. Test this against the observed size-dependent gaps, particularly at n=13 and n=15, without replacing the effective mixed move portfolio.
