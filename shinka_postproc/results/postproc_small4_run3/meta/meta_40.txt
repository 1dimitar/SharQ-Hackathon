# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 20 (“Diverse Energy- and Routing-Guided Search”), remains the strongest evaluated approach.** It achieves a **0.99 combined score**, the lowest mean gap at **1.04%**, and a best gap of **−0.61%**. Its balanced use of QUBO energy, routing features, an online ridge predictor, novelty, and uncertainty selection is stronger in aggregate than the newer variants.
- **Protected, multi-signal evaluation is more reliable than a narrow ranking signal.** Generation 20 selects LP candidates from learned predictions, energy, routing proxy, novelty, and uncertainty, rather than letting one signal consume the batch. Recent related programs also scored well—Generation 35 at **0.99 / 1.45%**, Generation 38 at **0.99 / 1.51%**, and Generation 36 at **0.98 / 1.57%**—but none matched Generation 20’s mean gap.
- **Diverse archives and degree-preserving moves support strong results across sizes.** Generation 20 combines two-switches, three-arc rotations, and larger perturbations, while maintaining diversity in starts and archive selections. Its mean gaps remain below 1.5% at every size, especially **0.34% at n=14**; the current evaluation also has strong n=14 results (**0.34%**) and n=16 results (**0.99%**).

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Energy/novelty-heavy screening without equally effective LP selection is weaker.** Generation 37 (“Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search”) scored **0.96** with a **3.70% mean gap**. Despite its adaptive signal allocation, archive, and diverse perturbations, all runs used the full LP budget, so the weaker outcome reflects search or candidate-selection quality rather than fewer evaluations.
- **Dual/load guidance and archive relinking alone do not match the best’s broader portfolio.** Generation 39 scored **0.98 / 2.39%**, while Generation 35’s dual-guided hot-arc search scored **0.99 / 1.45%**. Both use LP-derived bottleneck signals, but their higher mean gaps than Generation 20 suggest these signals work best as part of a broader screening and exploration strategy.
- **Strong individual solutions do not ensure the best aggregate performance.** Generation 36 reached a **0.14% best gap** and Generation 39 a **0.61% best gap**, but their mean gaps were **1.57%** and **2.39%** respectively. By comparison, Generation 20’s **−0.61% best gap** accompanies the best mean gap, indicating stronger aggregate reliability as well as a strong best run.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 20’s routing features complement, rather than replace, the surrogate energy.** Its nine-feature representation includes demand-weighted distance, peak and upper-tail link loads, load RMS, source/target work, direct traffic, and unreachable pairs. Batched APSP with equal splitting across shortest paths gives a cheap routing proxy; the current evaluation’s **1.04%** mean gap is consistent with this combination being more effective than a narrower screen.
- **The online ridge model is effective when embedded in a protected selection portfolio.** Generation 20 calibrates predictions within each run and uses uncertainty, while reserving LP evaluations for energy-ranked, routing-ranked, and novel candidates. Generation 35 and Generation 38 also use online prediction, but their **1.45%** and **1.51%** mean gaps show that having a predictor alone does not reproduce Generation 20’s result.
- **Diversity is enforced at several stages in the current best.** It filters near-duplicate repaired starts and preserves topology separation in both the archive and LP batch. This complements an elite archive and broad move set, reducing the risk that a batch or search population collapses onto similar networks.
- **LP feedback is useful for proposing moves, but not sufficient by itself.** Generation 20 uses positive LP prices—or high loads as a fallback—to target hot arcs, then combines those moves with rotations, archive walks, and broader selection. Generation 35’s hot-arc search and Generation 39’s pressure-guided relinking both use dual/load signals, but achieved higher mean gaps, reinforcing the value of combining LP feedback with independent rankings and exploration.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt candidate-batch quotas using observed improvement rates.** Track whether each selection source—learned prediction, energy, routing proxy, novelty, and uncertainty—produces an incumbent improvement, then modestly increase the next-round allocation for effective sources while preserving at least one slot for every source. This extends the protected multi-signal portfolio that helped Generation 20 outperform narrower ranking strategies.

2. **Generate explicit hot-arc removal moves.** For arcs with positive LP prices, or the highest-load arcs when prices are unavailable, prioritize compatible two-switches and three-arc rotations that remove the targeted arc; retain untargeted moves as well. This makes the current program’s useful LP bottleneck guidance more direct without relying on dual/load signals alone.

3. **Add a tie-robust routing feature for candidate ranking.** For the shortlisted candidates, recompute routing loads using a small set of deterministic shortest-path tie-breakings or splits, then rank using a high-percentile or worst-case load summary alongside the existing equal-split proxy. Keep it as an additional selection signal—not a filter—to preserve the energy, predictor, novelty, and uncertainty diversity associated with the best results.

4. **Preserve archive coverage across candidate qualities.** Reserve archive slots for LP elites, low-energy candidates, routing-proxy leaders, and structurally novel topologies, and sample parents across those groups, especially after stalls. This builds on the existing distance-filtered archive while reducing the chance that LP score or one topology region dominates the search.

5. **Adapt perturbation scale to progress and instance size.** After repeated non-improving rounds, increase multi-switch kicks and steps toward distant archive members; after an improvement, favor local switches and rotations, including hot-arc moves. Test a modestly higher exploration allowance at n=13 and n=15, where mean gaps are weakest, while retaining the current move mix elsewhere.
