# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 49 remains the strongest evaluated program.** Its surrogate-screened, diversity-aware QAOA search achieved a **0.99 combined score**, **1.00% mean gap**, and **32.32 seconds** using 2,000 LP calls. The current-best implementation explicitly shows how its repaired, energy-ranked diverse starts, archive, and screened LP batches work together to deliver this result.
- **Generation 51 is the closest new result.** Its diverse archive, LP-scored switches and rotations, path relinking, and adaptive kicks achieved a **0.98 score**, **1.61% gap**, and **32.91 seconds**. This supports the effectiveness of combining archive diversity with LP-guided local search, though it did not match Generation 49’s gap.
- **QAOA starts substantially improved aggregate outcomes in the current best.** Generation 49’s mean best-known/best ratio was **0.9901** for QAOA starts versus **0.9730** for random starts. QAOA also beat random starts by wide margins on seeds 1 and 2: **1.67% vs. 3.45%** and **0.40% vs. 4.10%** gaps.
- **Balanced guidance and diverse proposal screening outperform dual-focused search alone.** Generation 49’s surrogate-and-distance batch screening attained a **1.00% gap**, while Generation 52’s price/load-guided two-switch search reached **3.78%** in 52.17 seconds. The broader evidence favors using dual signals as one proposal bias alongside surrogate quality and diversity, not as the dominant search criterion.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Adding move types and exploration mechanisms does not by itself improve quality or runtime.** Generation 54 used two-link switches, targeted rotations, random kicks, and adaptive search, but reached a **2.09% gap** in **57.52 seconds**—worse and slower than Generation 49’s 1.00% gap in 32.32 seconds.
- **More complex price-guided neighborhood search can underperform efficient screening.** Generation 52’s dual price/load-guided search had a **3.78% gap** and **52.17-second** runtime, despite using all 2,000 LP calls; Generation 55’s related price-guided, path-relinking approach achieved a **2.77% gap** in 42.5 seconds. Full budget use therefore does not imply effective allocation.
- **QAOA initialization is not a per-seed guarantee.** In Generation 49, random starts narrowly beat QAOA on seed 0 (**0.84% vs. 0.94% gap**). Generation 51 also reported a seed where random starts slightly beat QAOA, and Generation 55 showed mixed results with random starts slightly better on average (**2.23% vs. 2.68% gap**). The benefit is contingent on candidate quality and diversity.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 49 avoids spending LP calls on an undifferentiated initial pool.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, sorts them by surrogate energy, and chooses up to 30 starts while enforcing a minimum topology distance of four where possible. This combines cheap ranking with coverage before costly evaluation.
- **Its archive and batch selection balance exploitation with coverage.** The archive first retains up to 24 high-quality networks separated by topology distance, then fills to 32 with remaining top-ranked networks. For each parent, `screen_batch` ranks unseen, uncached moves by surrogate-energy change, selects diverse proposals first, and fills the batch with high-ranked alternatives. This is a concrete distinction from approaches that rely more heavily on generating additional move types.
- **LP prices guide rather than constrain the current-best search.** `make_moves` exhaustively considers switches involving positive-price links (or heavily loaded links when prices are unavailable), but also adds random switches, sampled rotations, and multi-switch kicks. This preserves broader exploration while using LP detail to focus part of the neighborhood.
- **Caching and bounded adaptive exploration support the current best’s runtime.** `seen` prevents repeated LP scoring, `tools.is_cached` filters already evaluated proposals, and parent stall counts increase rotation sampling up to 350 trials. The program uses the full 2,000-call budget in about 32 seconds, substantially faster than the more extensive Generation 54 and Generation 52 searches.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 49 is still the overall leader:** **0.99 score**, **1.00% mean gap**, and **32.32 seconds**. The best new result, Generation 51, scored **0.98** with a **1.61% gap** in 32.91 seconds. It is close in runtime, but does not match the leader’s solution quality.
- **The new results reinforce large quality differences at the same LP budget.** Generation 51’s 1.61% gap compares favorably with Generation 54’s 2.09%, Generation 55’s 2.77%, and Generation 52’s 3.78%; all used about 2,000 LP calls. Proposal selection and search organization are more informative than call count alone.
- **Runtime varies substantially without a corresponding improvement in solution quality.** Generation 49 takes 32.32 seconds for a 1.00% gap, versus 42.5 seconds for Generation 55’s 2.77%, 52.17 seconds for Generation 52’s 3.78%, and 57.52 seconds for Generation 54’s 2.09%. Generation 51 is the exception among the newer variants, retaining near-leader runtime while achieving a moderate quality result.
- **Generation 49’s aggregate QAOA advantage coexists with seed sensitivity.** Its QAOA gaps were **0.94%, 1.67%, and 0.40%**, versus random-start gaps of **0.84%, 3.45%, and 4.10%**. Thus, the aggregate improvement is driven by strong gains on two seeds, while the seed-0 reversal shows that initialization quality is variable rather than uniformly beneficial.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt batch diversity to the proposal pool.** Keep `screen_batch`’s surrogate ranking and distance-aware selection, but vary how many diverse candidates it accepts based on the pool’s structure: favor stronger separation when top-ranked proposals cluster together, and favor energy-ranked filling when few promising candidates are available. This extends a selection mechanism associated with the best result while testing whether fixed distance and batch thresholds waste LP calls.

2. **Allocate searches by parent productivity, with a diversity floor.** Track LP evaluations and incumbent improvements per archive parent, then bias parent selection toward regions that have recently improved the best score. Reserve a fixed share of iterations for less-tested or structurally distant parents so exploitation does not collapse the diverse archive that helped distinguish the strongest programs from weaker searches.

3. **Adapt move generation based on measured move-type returns.** Track whether switches, rotations, and kicks produce improving candidates, and shift proposal-generation effort toward the move types that are productive for the current search while retaining a small exploration share for the others. This builds on the best program’s combination of focused and broad moves without assuming that adding more move types or trials will improve results.

4. **Use a small, controlled random-start quota alongside QAOA starts.** Keep most initial LP evaluations for repaired, energy-ranked, diverse QAOA candidates, but reserve a small fraction for repaired random topologies and compare a few allocation ratios. QAOA has the stronger aggregate result, especially on seeds 1 and 2, but its seed-0 reversal shows that a limited random hedge may help without replacing the effective QAOA initialization.

5. **Make stagnation kicks targeted and archive-preserving.** When progress stalls, kick a non-elite archive member using a few degree-preserving switches biased toward high-price or heavily loaded links, then screen the kicked topology and its nearby moves with the existing surrogate-and-distance procedure. Cap the attempts and leave elite networks untouched; this uses dual information as a proposal bias rather than letting it dominate the search.
