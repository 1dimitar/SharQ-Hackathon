# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 49 remains the strongest overall result:** 0.99 combined score and **1.00% mean gap** in 32.32 seconds using 2,000 LP calls. Its repaired, deduplicated, energy-ranked starts, topology-diverse archive, and surrogate-screened LP batches continue to be the best-supported combination.
- **The current-best program’s QAOA-seeded, diverse archive search is effective, especially across difficult seeds.** QAOA starts averaged a **0.9901 best-known/best ratio**, versus **0.9730** for random starts. They were substantially better on seeds 1 and 2, though random starts narrowly won on seed 0. This is consistent with Generation 63, where QAOA initialization also beat random starts on all three seeds, reaching a **1.96% mean gap**.
- **Surrogate ranking combined with diversity is a stronger pattern than dual guidance alone.** The current best screens moves by energy change and then selects distinct topologies for LP evaluation. Generation 63 combined this kind of archive search with dual prices and achieved a 1.96% gap; Generation 62’s diverse restarts reached 3.11%, and Generation 59’s edge-frequency sampling reached 4.19%. These remain behind Generation 49’s 1.00% gap.
- **Dual information appears useful as a targeted proposal signal, not as the main selection rule.** In the current best, positive-price links guide exhaustive switches and rotations, while random switches and kicks preserve exploration. This balanced use accords with Generation 56’s 1.21% gap and outperforms the weaker dual-heavy results documented previously, such as Generation 52’s 3.78% gap.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Unbounded or costly proposal generation can invalidate an otherwise promising search.** Generation 53 scored **0.0** after exceeding the 150-second limit; its feedback cited large proposal volumes, validity checks, LP work, and a loop that could continue when no new proposals were available. The current best includes fallback moves and a 10,000-round stop, helping avoid that failure mode.
- **QAOA initialization is not uniformly beneficial.** In the current best, random starts slightly won on seed 0 (0.84% gap versus 0.94% for QAOA). Generation 62 also saw random starts outperform QAOA on two of three seeds (0.9729 versus 0.9700 mean ratio), and Generation 64’s random-start ratio (0.9709) exceeded its QAOA ratio (0.9670). Initialization benefits therefore vary by program and seed.
- **Using the full LP budget or adding adaptive search machinery does not guarantee better solutions.** Generation 62 used all 2,000 calls but had a 3.11% mean gap; Generation 64 also used 2,000 calls and reached 3.42%. Earlier adaptive searches likewise fell short of Generation 49, showing that evaluation count and added move types do not substitute for effective candidate ranking and screening.
- **More elaborate sampling can be worse than focused, well-screened search.** Generation 59’s randomized backtracking and edge-frequency model achieved a 4.19% gap in 41.13 seconds, while Generation 63’s refinement search achieved 1.96% in 16.6 seconds. The results favor effective initialization and selective LP evaluation over proposal machinery alone.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best makes expensive LP calls selective.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, ranks starts by `tools.energy`, and chooses an initially diverse subset. It then screens fresh moves by surrogate-energy change, selects up to six mutually diverse moves first, and evaluates batches of up to nine. This design achieved a 1.00% mean gap with 2,000 LP calls in 32.32 seconds.
- **Its archive balances elite quality with coverage.** `archive()` first retains up to 24 networks separated by topology distance, then fills to 32 with the best remaining networks. Parent selection mixes the top six with the broader archive. This provides both exploitation and access to different regions, rather than repeatedly searching only the single incumbent.
- **Dual prices are integrated without closing off exploration.** `make_moves()` exhaustively checks switches involving positive-price links, but also samples 70 unrestricted switches, up to 350 rotations for stalled parents, and multi-switch kicks. That complements the energy-and-distance screening and avoids making dual prices the sole search criterion.
- **Caching and explicit progress handling support runtime control.** The local `seen` map prevents repeated LP scoring; `tools.is_cached()` filters already-evaluated moves; and when a neighborhood is exhausted, the search tries other archive members and random kicks before stopping after prolonged lack of progress. This contrasts with Generation 53’s timeout and supports the current best’s 32-second runtime.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 49 is still the current leader:** **0.99 score, 1.00% mean gap, 2,000 LP calls, 32.32 seconds**. The new successful programs do not surpass it: Generation 63 reached 1.96% in 16.6 seconds, Generation 62 reached 3.11% in 36.15 seconds, Generation 64 reached 3.42% in about 16.4 seconds, and Generation 59 reached 4.19% in 41.13 seconds.
- **Generation 49’s quality is strong across its reported QAOA seeds but varies by initialization.** Its QAOA gaps were **0.94%, 1.67%, and 0.40%**; random-start gaps were **0.84%, 3.45%, and 4.10%**. The QAOA advantage on seeds 1 and 2 drives the large mean-ratio difference, despite losing narrowly on seed 0.
- **The newer results reinforce that score quality is not determined by runtime or LP-call count alone.** Generation 63 was roughly twice as fast as Generation 49 but had nearly twice its gap. Generations 62 and 64 consumed all 2,000 LP calls yet also had materially larger gaps. Candidate quality, diversity, and LP-batch selection remain the clearest differentiators in the results.
- **The failed Generation 53 marks a separate correctness-of-evaluation boundary.** Its 0.0 score after a 150-second timeout is not comparable as solution quality, but it demonstrates that proposal volume and failure to make progress can nullify the value of an otherwise sophisticated search. The current best avoids that outcome while maintaining full budget use and near-optimal results.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt the diversity quota in `screen_batch` to the actual clustering of promising moves.** Measure topology distances among the best surrogate-ranked proposals; when they are tightly clustered, select more mutually distant candidates before filling the batch by energy rank, and when they are already diverse, favor the top-ranked moves. This refines the current best’s successful combination of surrogate screening and topology diversity without increasing LP calls.

2. **Allocate parent selection using recent LP-call productivity while preserving exploration.** Track how often searches from each archive parent produce an improvement, then modestly favor productive parents while reserving a fixed share of selections for less-tested or distant archive members. This builds on the diverse archive and elite sampling that work well, while reducing wasted batches without collapsing coverage.

3. **Tune proposal effort by move type based on observed returns.** Record whether focused switches, unrestricted switches, rotations, and kicks yield improvements, and adjust their proposal counts over time; keep a minimum quota for every type and cap total proposals. This tests whether the current blend can direct screening effort toward useful moves while avoiding the costly, unbounded neighborhood expansion that caused a prior timeout.

4. **Keep QAOA-led initialization, but reserve a small, explicit quota for random starts.** Repair and deduplicate both pools, then choose most initial candidates by energy-ranked diversity from QAOA samples and use the remainder for random topologies. QAOA’s aggregate results are substantially stronger, especially on seeds 1 and 2, but the seed-0 reversal—and other evaluations where random starts won—supports a limited hedge rather than relying exclusively on either source.

5. **Make stagnation kicks targeted and bounded, and apply them to non-elite archive members.** After repeated stalls, bias a small number of degree-preserving switches toward positive-price or high-load links, then pass the resulting networks through the existing surrogate-and-distance batch screen; cap kick attempts and leave elite members unchanged. This uses dual information as a proposal signal, consistent with successful balanced use of prices, without making dual guidance the main selection rule.
