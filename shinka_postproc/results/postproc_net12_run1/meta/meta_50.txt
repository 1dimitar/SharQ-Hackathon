# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: QAOA-Guided Network Repair and Local Search**
- **Implementation**: Repairs each QAOA candidate into a valid network, scores unique networks with the routing LP, then applies randomized 2-exchange descent to the top five. It repeatedly kicks the current best network and descends until the LP budget is exhausted.
- **Performance**: Achieved a combined score of 0.97, mean gap of 3.42%, and 2,000 LP calls in about 12.41 seconds.
- **Feedback**: QAOA starts averaged 0.9671 best-known/best versus 0.9393 for random starts, showing a consistent benefit from QAOA initialization. Across three seeds, gaps ranged from 1.64% to 5.23%; random-start gaps were worse in every case.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 49 — Surrogate-Guided Diverse QAOA Network Search, combines strong quality with moderate runtime:** it scored **0.99** with a **1.00% mean gap** in **32.32 seconds**, using 2,000 LP calls. Its repaired, energy-ranked, diverse QAOA starts and surrogate-screened batches produced gaps of **0.94%, 1.67%, and 0.40%**.
- **Diverse archives and LP-guided moves are repeatedly effective, but proposal selection matters.** Generation 48 — Diverse Dual-Guided Network Search reached a **1.37% gap** in **27.08 seconds** with a quality-and-diversity archive, dual-guided swaps and rotations, and adaptive kicks. Generation 45’s diverse LP-guided search also scored **0.99**, with a **1.32% gap**, though it took **38.69 seconds**.
- **QAOA initialization is usually advantageous, especially for Generation 49.** Its mean best-known/best ratio was **0.9901** from QAOA starts versus **0.9730** from random starts; QAOA improved on random for seeds 1 and 2. The results also show this is not universal: on seed 0, random starts were slightly better (**0.84% gap** versus **0.94%**).
- **Using surrogate screening to allocate a fixed LP budget can outperform broader but less selective search.** Generation 49 screens moves by energy and topology distance before evaluating small LP batches, reaching a lower mean gap than Generations 46 and 47, which also used 2,000 calls but had gaps of **2.06%** and **5.87%**.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **More neighborhood mechanisms do not guarantee better results.** Generation 46’s elite archive, switches, rotations, and random kicks scored **0.98** with a **2.06% gap** in **53.34 seconds**, while Generation 49’s screened, diverse search achieved a **1.00% gap** in **32.32 seconds**. The contrast points to search organization and LP-batch selection—not move variety alone—as important differentiators.
- **Bottleneck guidance can underperform when the overall search or move ranking is weaker.** Generation 47 used LP prices and loads to prioritize local moves but had a **5.87% gap**, despite consuming its full 2,000-call budget. This reinforces the previous finding that price-focused search (including Generation 43 at **3.18%** and Generation 44 at **4.62%**) was less effective than balanced guidance plus broader screened exploration.
- **Full LP-budget use is not evidence of efficient allocation.** Generations 45–49 all used their full 2,000-call budget, yet mean gaps ranged from **1.00%** (Generation 49) to **5.87%** (Generation 47). Evaluated-call count alone does not explain solution quality.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 49’s initialization avoids spending LP calls on an undifferentiated seed set.** It repairs and deduplicates candidates, orders them by surrogate energy, and enforces edge-distance diversity when choosing up to 30 starts. It then builds an archive from evaluated networks, retaining distinct regions before filling remaining slots with top-ranked candidates.
- **Its batch screening balances quality, diversity, and exploration.** `screen_batch` ranks fresh moves by change in surrogate energy, first selects moves separated by a minimum topology distance, then fills the batch with the best remaining candidates. This spends LP calls on a varied but energy-favored sample rather than evaluating every generated neighbor.
- **The program uses LP details as a bias, not a hard constraint.** Positive-price links—or, when prices are unavailable, heavily loaded links—focus exhaustive two-switches and sampled rotations; additional random switches and multi-switch kicks retain broader exploration. This matches the earlier finding that dual-price guidance works best when paired with alternatives rather than restricting the search to hot links.
- **Stagnation handling and caching help keep the bounded search productive.** Generation 49 increases rotation sampling for stalled parents, tracks per-parent stalls, tries other archive members when a batch is exhausted, and filters seen or cached networks before LP evaluation. These mechanisms fit its **32.32-second** runtime while it uses all 2,000 calls, though the program still includes a repeated-search fallback capped at 10,000 rounds.

- **Generation 49 is the strongest result in the current group and improves on the previous global leader, Generation 42.** It achieved a **1.00% mean gap** and **0.99 combined score**, versus Generation 42’s **1.17% gap** and **0.99 score**. It was also faster (**32.32 seconds** versus **47.68 seconds**) at the same 2,000-call budget.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adapt batch diversity to proposal quality.** In `screen_batch`, retain the energy-ranked, topology-diverse selection, but vary the minimum distance and the number of diversity-first slots based on the candidate pool: enforce stronger separation when many proposals cluster near the same topology, and fill more slots by energy rank when promising proposals are scarce. This extends the current best’s effective screening while testing whether its fixed thresholds of distance 2 and six diverse picks leave LP calls underused.

2. **Allocate LP calls according to parent productivity while preserving exploration.** Track each archive parent’s LP evaluations and incumbent improvements, then select parents using a mix of improvement rate and structural distance from the best-performing regions. Reserve a fixed fraction of searches for under-tested or distant parents so productive basins get more attention without losing the diverse exploration that has helped distinguish the best program from weaker dual-guided searches.

3. **Escalate move types based on measured stagnation, not only parent identity.** The current rotation schedule increases trials after repeated stalls for a parent; extend this by tracking whether switches, rotations, or kicks actually produce improvements, and shift proposal effort toward effective move types while retaining a small exploration share for the others. This could reduce time spent generating unproductive neighborhoods while building on the program’s demonstrated benefit from combining focused moves with broader alternatives.

4. **Use controlled QAOA-versus-random seed diversification.** QAOA starts win on average, especially on seeds 1 and 2, but random starts slightly outperform them on seed 0. Reserve a small portion of the initial LP evaluations for repaired random topologies, then select the remaining starts from the energy-ranked, diversity-aware QAOA pool; compare several small allocation ratios to test whether this hedges seed sensitivity without sacrificing the QAOA advantage.

5. **Make kicks targeted and archive-preserving.** When global progress stalls, kick a non-elite archive member using a few degree-preserving switches biased toward high-price or heavily loaded links, then screen the resulting topology and its nearby moves as a new search start. Keep existing elites untouched and cap kick attempts, extending the current kick mechanism and dual-price bias while avoiding the failure mode of restricting the whole search to bottleneck-focused proposals.
