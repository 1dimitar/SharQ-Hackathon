# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 100 remains the aggregate accuracy leader, and the current best program preserves its strongest search pattern.** The current best combines a surrogate-ranked, diversity-aware archive with a broad degree-preserving move portfolio and quality-plus-novelty batch screening. It achieved a **2.10% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.9919 mean best-per-Hamiltonian score**. Its results include a −0.30% gap on r2g181 and gaps of +1.99%, +0.97%, and +0.63% for the best pools on the other Hamiltonians.
- **Diverse archives and relinking continue to produce strong individual results, but not a better mean gap than the current best.** Generation 120, *Bottleneck-Guided Network Search with Path Relinking*, achieved the best new-run result, a **0.63% best gap**, and a **2.32% mean gap**. Generations 116 and 118 also scored 0.98, with mean gaps of **2.46%** and **2.37%**. Their swaps, rotations, kicks, archive diversity, and relinking are consistent with the competitive patterns in the current best.
- **The current best’s explicit quality-and-novelty screening remains a useful distinguishing feature.** It selects up to six surrogate-promising, structurally separated candidates per batch, fills remaining quality slots, and reserves one LP evaluation for a candidate distant from the scored archive. This is more specific than surrogate screening alone, and the current best’s **2.10% mean gap** is lower than the new variants’ **2.32–2.75%** gaps.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **The new variants did not improve aggregate accuracy over the current best, despite full LP-budget use.** Generations 116–120 all used 2,000 LP calls per pool, yet their mean gaps ranged from **2.32% to 4.03%**, versus **2.10%** for the current best. This reinforces the prior finding that budget exhaustion measures throughput, not search quality.
- **Dual- and bottleneck-focused proposals did not consistently improve results across pools.** Generation 117’s surrogate-screened search had the strongest new combined score (**0.99**) and a **1.06% best gap**, but its mean gap was **2.75%** and weakest-pool gap **4.12%**. Generation 119’s dual-guided edge recombination had a **4.03% mean gap** and a **6.26% weakest-pool gap**, despite positive overall performance. Generation 116 also had a **4.18%** pool gap. These outcomes match earlier evidence that price- or bottleneck-guided moves can find strong cases without ensuring consistency.
- **A strong best case does not imply robust pool-level behavior.** Generation 120’s **0.63% best gap** coexisted with a **2.32% mean gap**; the current best likewise ranges from **−0.30%** to **+4.42%** across its pools. Best-case figures therefore mask meaningful seed and Hamiltonian variation.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best’s archive balances elite quality and structural coverage.** It starts from up to 30 low-energy candidates separated by a topological distance threshold, then retains separated high-quality scored networks before filling the archive. Parent selection favors the top six 48% of the time but also samples across the archive. This provides exploitation while preserving access to distinct regions.
- **Its move portfolio combines directed proposals with escape mechanisms.** `make_moves` uses LP-price/load-focused switches, archive-consensus switches, random two-switches, rotations, multi-switch kicks, and relinking toward a strong, distant archive member. The new results support this portfolio’s competitiveness: Generation 120’s related path-relinking and bottleneck-guided moves reached a 0.63% best gap, but its mean gap remained above the current best.
- **Batch screening and fallback logic help maintain search breadth and evaluation throughput.** The current best screens unseen, uncached moves by surrogate-energy change, enforces some separation among quality picks, reserves a novelty pick, and changes archive parents or applies random kicks when neighborhoods stall. It used all 2,000 calls in every reported pool, averaging 26.64 seconds. The other generations’ full-budget results show that these mechanics support sustained evaluation but do not by themselves eliminate weak runs.
- **Evaluation feedback is used selectively rather than as the sole search signal.** The current best uses LP detail to identify positive-price or high-load arcs, but also generates moves that do not depend on those signals. This is consistent with the contrast between its 2.10% mean gap and Generation 119’s 4.03% mean gap under a more explicitly dual-guided recombination approach.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best remains stronger in mean gap than all newly evaluated variants.** Its **2.10%** mean gap is below Generation 120’s **2.32%**, Generation 118’s **2.37%**, Generation 116’s **2.46%**, Generation 117’s **2.75%**, and Generation 119’s **4.03%**. This extends the previous comparison in which it also outperformed Generations 111–115 on mean gap.
- **New variants produce competitive best cases but weaker aggregate outcomes.** Generation 120 reached a **0.63%** best gap and Generation 117 reached **1.06%**, while the current best reached **−0.30%**. Generation 120’s best case is close to the current best’s better positive-gap results, but neither new variant matched its best reported result or mean gap.
- **The current best has the strongest reported cross-Hamiltonian best-of-pools score among these results.** Its mean best-per-Hamiltonian score is **0.9919**, from gaps of −0.30%, +1.99%, +0.97%, and +0.63%. The new summaries report per-Hamiltonian ranges or aggregate gaps but do not show a higher corresponding score; their pool variability includes gaps above 4%.
- **Runtime does not explain the accuracy ranking.** The current best averages **26.64 seconds**. Generation 117 is faster at **19.74 seconds** but has a **2.75% mean gap**; Generation 120 is slower at **44.31 seconds** with a **2.32% gap**; and Generation 116 averages **35.73 seconds** with a **2.46% gap**. Across these programs, proposal quality and consistency are more informative than runtime or LP-call count alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Allocate each screening batch by operator-specific success rather than treating all proposals equally.** Track smoothed LP-improvement rates separately for focused switches, archive-consensus switches, random switches, rotations, kicks, and relinking; reserve a minimum number of candidates for exploration by each operator, then allocate the remaining slots to the most productive ones. Keep the current quality-and-novelty screening within those allocations so adaptive exploitation does not eliminate diversity.

2. **Improve the novelty slot to target underexplored regions, not just distance from the nearest scored network.** Measure a candidate’s distance from several elite archive representatives and favor regions with fewer prior LP evaluations, using surrogate energy as a tie-breaker. This extends the current best’s explicit novelty reservation and may reduce weak pool outcomes without letting bottleneck-focused proposals dominate.

3. **Give archive relinking a protected, varied evaluation opportunity.** Periodically reserve an LP slot for a legal intermediate toward a strong, distant archive member, and vary the target among distant elites rather than always choosing from the same small quality-ranked subset. Relinking has produced competitive best cases, while the current best already has the infrastructure to generate advancing steps; protecting their evaluation tests whether those bridges improve consistency.

4. **Refine parent selection using archive quality, structural coverage, and recent activity.** Retain frequent selection from the top six, but use part of the remaining probability to favor structurally distant members that have been selected infrequently or have stalled less recently. This builds on the current elite-plus-diverse-archive strategy and can direct search toward promising regions that are otherwise underexplored.

5. **Keep bottleneck-guided moves as a bounded complement, and diversify how they are generated.** Limit their share of proposals, but try switches or rotations that alter high-price or high-load arcs alongside the existing random, consensus, kick, and relinking moves. Recent bottleneck-guided variants found strong individual cases but had weaker mean gaps, supporting controlled inclusion rather than allowing these signals to drive the search alone.
