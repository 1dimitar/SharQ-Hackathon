# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Surrogate screening plus a diverse archive remains the strongest overall design.** The current best, **Diverse Surrogate-Screened Network Search (Generation 14)**, scored **0.99**, with a **2.63% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.97 mean pool score**. It repairs and deduplicates starts, screens moves by surrogate energy, and searches an archive of varied strong networks. This supports the earlier finding that combining surrogate ranking with diversity works better than relying on bottleneck signals alone.
- **Multiple degree-preserving neighborhoods help cover different search regions.** Generation 14 combines two-switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks; earlier strong designs also used these neighborhoods alongside path relinking. Generation 14’s best-per-Hamiltonian gaps were **−0.30% to +2.20%**, while Generation 9’s path-relinking search had a **1.15% best gap** and Generation 13’s adaptive frontier had a **−0.30% best gap**. The varied moves can find exceptional solutions, though they do not eliminate pool-to-pool variation.
- **Diversity at initialization and during archive selection is associated with strong results.** Generation 14 chooses up to 30 surrogate-ranked starts while enforcing topology separation, then retains diverse archive representatives before filling with top-scoring networks. Its best result beat the known best by **0.30%**, while the earlier Generation 8 and Generation 10 diversity-aware searches reported mean gaps of **2.55%** and **2.53%**, respectively.
- **Adaptive exploration supports quality without a large runtime penalty.** Generation 14 increases rotation trials as parents stall and enlarges LP batches for stalled or ambiguous neighborhoods. It averaged **27.17 seconds**, faster than Generation 9 (**74.64 s**), Generation 12 (**70.08 s**), and Generation 13 (**35.02 s**), while retaining a **0.99** combined score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Using the entire LP budget does not ensure reliable convergence.** Generation 14 consumed all **2,000 LP calls** in every pool, yet gaps ranged from **−0.30% to +4.83%**. This reinforces the prior observation from Generation 13 and other searches that budget exhaustion alone is not evidence of consistently strong solutions.
- **Pool sensitivity remains substantial even for the top-scoring search.** Generation 14 ranged from beating the known best on r2g181 seed 2 to a **4.83%** gap on r3g109 seed 0. Its r3g028 pools were all above the known best, with gaps of **2.20–4.24%**. Strong best cases therefore do not translate into uniform performance across seeds or Hamiltonians.
- **Critical-link information is not enough on its own.** Earlier bottleneck- or dual-focused approaches performed poorly: Generation 1 had a **6.77% mean gap**, Generation 4 **7.16%**, and Generation 5 **6.60%**. Generation 14 uses positive-price or high-load links to focus some moves, but combines them with random swaps, rotations, kicks, surrogate screening, and archive diversity—unlike those less successful focused strategies.
- **A strong combined score can mask weaker average-pool results.** Generation 14’s **0.99** combined score and **−0.30% best gap** coexist with a **2.63% mean gap** and **0.97 mean pool score**. Its standout r2g181 result is not representative of all pools; for comparison, Generation 11 reported a higher **2.74% mean gap** and **1.99% best gap**, while Generation 13 had a **2.23% mean gap** and **−0.30% best gap**.

## Implementation Insights

- **Screen candidates before costly LP evaluation, but preserve both quality and structural variety.** Generation 14’s `screen_batch` filters locally seen and tool-cached topologies, ranks fresh moves by surrogate-energy change from the parent, chooses separated high-ranked moves, then fills the batch from the ranking. This maintains a quality-oriented screen without spending every call on near-duplicate proposals.
- **The archive explicitly balances elite exploitation with broader exploration.** It first keeps up to 24 networks separated by a topology-distance threshold, then fills to 32 with the best remaining networks. Parent selection chooses from the top six **48%** of the time and from the full archive otherwise. This fits the strong best-per-Hamiltonian outcomes, while the pool results show that archive diversity does not remove seed sensitivity.
- **Move generation combines focused and unfocused search.** The program exhaustively considers two-switches involving critical links, adds 70 random two-switch trials, samples rotations with trials increasing from 70 to as many as 350 as a parent stalls, and generates multi-switch kicks. This complements the older finding that critical-link guidance works better as one signal within a broader neighborhood portfolio than as the sole search mechanism.
- **Batch effort responds to ambiguity and stagnation.** The nominal LP batch is nine; it increases when the leading surrogate scores are close and can grow further for stalled parents, up to 24. This adaptive batching is part of Generation 14’s comparatively low **27.17-second** runtime, although every pool still exhausted its LP budget.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 14 is a top-tier result, but its metrics show distinct strengths and trade-offs.** It scored **0.99**, with **2.63% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, **0.97 mean pool score**, and **27.17 seconds** per run. Its best-per-Hamiltonian score was **0.9878**. The negative best gap comes from r2g181 seed 2 (**299,715** versus **300,620** known best); the other Hamiltonian bests remained **1.31–2.20%** above known best.
- **Mean-gap comparisons vary from the earlier global summary.** The previous insights reported Generation 13 at **2.64%** mean gap, whereas the current evaluation gives Generation 13 a **2.23%** mean gap and **−0.30%** best gap. On the current figures, Generation 13 has a lower mean gap than Generation 14, with the same best gap; Generation 14 is faster (**27.17 s** versus Generation 13’s **35.02 s**). Generation 14’s best-per-Hamiltonian score is **0.99**, compared with Generation 13’s reported combined score of **0.99**.
- **Other strong searches trade average gap, best result, and runtime differently.** Generation 9 reported **2.36% mean / 1.15% best** at **74.64 s**; Generation 10 reported **2.53% / 1.32%**; Generation 12 **2.68% / 1.79%** at **70.08 s**; and Generation 11 **2.74% / 1.99%** at **47.85 s**. Generation 14 is notably faster than these runs and has a better best gap than all except Generation 13’s reported **−0.30%**.
- **The current best’s average conceals wide pool variation.** Across its 16 evaluations, gaps ran from **−0.30% to +4.83%**. The weakest result was r3g109 seed 0 (**315,154**), whereas the strongest was r2g181 seed 2. This confirms the earlier pattern that surrogate-guided archive searches can produce near-optimal or better-than-known solutions, but their per-pool performance remains uneven despite full-budget search.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make LP batch selection archive-aware.** Keep ranking fresh moves by parent-relative surrogate-energy change, but also score their distance from the best archived networks and favor candidates that are both promising and structurally novel. This extends the current diversity filter beyond a batch-local rule and may reduce repeated exploration of regions already represented in the archive.

2. **Adapt parent selection to per-parent productivity.** Track each archive member’s LP evaluations and improvements, then reduce selection of repeatedly stalled parents while giving underexplored or recently productive members more chances. Preserve the current elite-selection probability as a baseline, since it supports strong best cases, but use stagnation and visit counts to respond to the wide pool-to-pool variation.

3. **Allocate neighborhood effort according to observed returns.** Record which of two-switches, rotations, and multi-switch kicks produce improvements, and adjust their sampling rates for each parent or search phase while retaining a minimum quota for every move type. The existing neighborhood portfolio is a strength; feedback-driven allocation could use the fixed LP budget more effectively than increasing rotation trials alone when a parent stalls.

4. **Use guided path relinking as a distinct stalled-search escape.** When a parent stalls, select a structurally distant elite archive member and generate valid intermediate networks by progressively applying degree-preserving switches that move the parent toward it; surrogate-screen those intermediates before LP evaluation. This complements the current kicks and rotations with a directed way to explore between strong regions.

5. **Calibrate surrogate screening against observed LP outcomes.** Track the relationship between each candidate’s surrogate energy and its exact LP score, and use that calibration to adjust screening—for example, retain some candidates from weaker-ranked energy bands when the surrogate is poorly predictive. The current surrogate screen is a core strength, but the substantial spread in results despite full LP budgets suggests testing whether its ranking can be made more reliable for different parents or search phases.
