# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Diverse, surrogate-screened topology search remains the strongest demonstrated approach.** The current best, **Generation 49 Surrogate-Screened Diverse Network Search**, scores **0.99 combined**, with a **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, **0.973 mean pool score**, and **0.9909 best-per-Hamiltonian score**. It repairs and deduplicates candidates, selects structurally diverse low-energy starts, and evaluates screened degree-preserving moves using the limited LP budget.
- **A varied move portfolio supports both focused improvement and escapes from local regions.** Generation 49 combines two-switches, three-arc rotations, and multi-switch kicks, with LP dual prices influencing proposal rankings. Its best-per-Hamiltonian gaps were **−0.30%** on r2g181, **+1.37%** on r3g028, **+0.97%** on r3g109, and **+1.65%** on r3g128. The new evaluations show similar best-case strengths: Generation 59 reached **+0.48%**, and Generation 63 reached **+0.48%**, though neither exceeded Generation 49’s aggregate score.
- **Archive diversity and adaptive search are repeatedly associated with strong results, but not reliably better aggregate performance.** Generation 64 combined consensus-edge guidance with dual prices and diverse archive search, reaching a **1.98% mean gap** and **1.31% best gap**. Generations 59–63 also used diverse starts and topology-changing neighborhoods and all scored **0.98 combined**, confirming the effectiveness of this general pattern while leaving Generation 49 ahead at **0.99**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **More sophisticated guidance did not displace the current best.** Generation 64 added consensus edges to dual-guided search but scored **0.98**, versus Generation 49’s **0.99**; its **1.98% mean gap** is better than Generation 49’s **2.79%**, but its **1.31% best gap** is worse than Generation 49’s **−0.30%**, and its best-per-Hamiltonian results were consistently **1.31–2.53%**.
- **Fast or adaptive variants did not consistently translate their implementation changes into aggregate gains.** Generation 62 used adaptive LP batch sizing and ran in **19.46 seconds**, much faster than Generation 49’s **28.95 seconds**, but achieved **0.98 combined**, a **2.80% mean gap**, and only a **1.60% best gap**. Its r3g128 runs still reached gaps as high as **4.59%**.
- **A strong result on one pool can coexist with substantial seed or Hamiltonian variability.** Generation 49 found **−0.30%** on r2g181 but had pool gaps up to **+5.12%**. The current evaluation’s individual results show the same pattern: its r2g181 seeds range from **−0.30% to +4.65%**, and r3g109 from **+0.97% to +5.12%**. Likewise, Generation 60’s best gap was **+0.63%**, but one pool reached **+5.41%**.
- **The previously observed LP-guided topology EDA remains a clear counterexample to iterative archive search.** Generation 55 scored **0.94**, with an **8.44% mean gap** and **5.04% best gap**, despite using all **2,000 LP calls**. Its link-frequency and demand-prior construction did not match the performance of iterative, surrogate-screened topology moves.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 49’s initial filtering avoids wasting the search budget.** It repairs candidates, checks validity, canonicalizes arc sets for deduplication, ranks starts by `tools.energy`, and selects starts separated by a symmetric-difference distance. This gives the search a varied, low-surrogate-energy starting archive before LP calls are spent.
- **The archive explicitly balances quality with structural variety.** The program retains up to **18** mutually separated LP-ranked networks, then fills to **30**; parent selection chooses among the top five **65%** of the time and samples the full archive otherwise. This combines exploitation with opportunities to leave elite but narrow regions.
- **The move ranking combines surrogate cost and dual information, while retaining exploration.** Generation 49 ranks proposals using `energy(net) - base_e - 0.02 * dual_gain`; its two-switch, rotation, and kick probabilities adapt to observed success but include a **0.25 additive floor**. The program also selects diverse high-ranked candidates for LP evaluation and sometimes adds a lower-ranked exploratory candidate.
- **Caching, validation, and canonicalization support full-budget search safely.** Generation 49 caches LP scores by canonical topology, checks validity, and avoids already-seen or tool-cached candidates. It used **2,000 LP calls** in every reported pool. Recent programs also exhausted their budgets, so the distinction is not simply budget use: proposal quality, archive behavior, and pool/seed variation remain important.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 49 remains the overall reference among these results.** It achieved **0.99 combined**, **2.79% mean gap**, **−0.30% best gap**, and **0.973 mean pool score**. The newest programs—Generations 59, 60, 62, and 63—each scored **0.98 combined**, with mean gaps of **2.61%, 2.90%, 2.80%, and 3.00%**, respectively. Generation 64’s **1.98% mean gap** is lower, but its combined score remained **0.98** and its best gap was **1.31%**.
- **The current best’s pool-level evidence shows both high upside and unevenness.** Across 16 current-best runs, the best known objective was beaten by **0.30%** on r2g181, while the worst gap was **+5.12%** on r3g109. Best-per-Hamiltonian results were particularly strong on r2g181 (**−0.30%**) and remained within **+1.65%** on all four Hamiltonians.

- **Runtime is not a reliable proxy for solution quality in these evaluations.** Generation 62 ran in **19.46 seconds** with a **2.80% mean gap**, while Generation 49 averaged **28.95 seconds** with a **2.79% mean gap** and the stronger combined score. Generation 64 averaged **38.56 seconds** and achieved a lower mean gap, but still did not improve the combined score. All these searches used the full **2,000-call LP budget**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reserve one LP evaluation in each batch for the most novel candidate.** Keep the current surrogate/dual-ranked choices, but also evaluate the valid, uncached proposal with the greatest symmetric difference from the archive, breaking ties by surrogate rank. This directly extends Generation 49’s successful diversity filtering and tests whether additional exploration can reduce its substantial seed-to-seed variability.

2. **Make parent selection respond to both archive quality and search history.** Preserve the current 65% bias toward elite parents, but use some of the remaining selections for structurally isolated or infrequently selected archive members; track whether their descendants improve and favor productive regions over time. This builds on the effective diverse archive while giving promising regions beyond the top five more opportunities to contribute.

3. **Reward move operators by LP-confirmed improvement per evaluation.** For each operator, track proposals screened, candidates sent to LP, and candidates that improve on their parent; update smoothed selection probabilities from the improvement rate while retaining a nonzero exploration floor. The current adaptive portfolio already performs well, so this refinement targets LP efficiency without removing the rotations, two-switches, or kicks that enable varied escapes.

4. **Add a bounded escape move for repeatedly stalled parents.** When a parent has failed to improve several times, occasionally choose a distant, high-quality archive member and apply a small number of degree-preserving exchanges that move the parent toward that member’s arc set. Validate, deduplicate, and screen these candidates through the existing surrogate/dual ranking so the escape mechanism supplements rather than replaces the proven local search.

5. **Use consensus edges as a limited proposal bias, not a replacement for search guidance.** Identify arcs recurring among high-quality, structurally diverse archive members and bias a small fraction of switches toward retaining or introducing them; continue generating ordinary moves with the existing exploration floor. Generation 64’s lower mean gap but weaker best gap suggests consensus may help consistency, while limiting its influence avoids overcommitting to a single region.
