# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 11, Demand-Weighted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate, is the current best by combined score and mean best gap.** It scored **0.82**, with an **11.39% mean best gap**, improving on the previous best-batch combined score of **0.81** (Generation 6) and its **14.16%** best gap. Its four gaps were **10.5%, 13.5%, 13.7%, and 7.9%**; network10_s3 was its strongest case.
- **Sparse, demand-supported two-hop rewards are the strongest observed path-term pattern.** Generation 11 has **211 quadratic terms**, versus **480** in Generations 6, 10, 13, and 16. It rewards only paths whose endpoint OD demand is positive and weights path support using normalized inbound/outbound traffic shares and a capped profile factor. This outperforms the broader 480-term variants in both combined score and mean best gap.
- **Direct-demand rewards remain a useful foundation, especially when paired with selective path rewards.** Generation 11’s negative demand-normalized direct-link coefficients plus selective two-hop bonuses beat the direct-only Generation 12 baseline (**0.80 score, 13.80% best gap**) on both score and best gap. This supports combining direct demand with carefully constrained path terms, rather than relying on direct demand alone.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Broader or differently scaled two-hop terms did not match Generation 11.** Generation 10 scored **0.80** with a **14.32%** mean best gap and 480 quadratic terms; Generation 13 scored **0.81** with a **12.45%** gap and 480 terms. Both trail Generation 11’s **0.82 / 11.39%**, suggesting that the fewer, traffic-share-filtered terms are more effective than the denser alternatives in these evaluations.
- **Additional endpoint, relay, and load adjustments have not reliably improved quality.** Generation 16’s endpoint-activity and relay-leg-fit weighting, with a 1.5 penalty and 480 terms, scored **0.79** and had a **15.02%** best gap plus a **40.21%** median gap. Earlier, Generations 5 and 7 also failed to beat Generation 6’s combined score despite more elaborate endpoint/transit or congestion heuristics.
- **Direct demand alone underperforms the current best on best-solution quality.** Generation 12’s clipped, 90th-percentile-capped linear rewards scored **0.80** with a **13.80%** best gap and **38.00%** median gap, compared with Generation 11’s **0.82**, **11.39%**, and **35.91%**. Its zero quadratic terms also indicate that simply favoring high-demand direct links did not guide the search as effectively as the selected two-hop formulation.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 11’s path construction is selective at both the OD and leg-support levels.** It excludes paths without positive endpoint demand, normalizes inbound traffic by destination totals and outbound traffic by source totals, and multiplies the two support shares through a capped square-root profile. This yields **211** interactions rather than the **480** used by several alternatives, while attaining the strongest current score.
- **Its coefficients are bounded and normalized.** Demand is clipped at zero, diagonal entries are cleared, and all demand is scaled by its maximum. Direct-link rewards are negative normalized demand; path rewards are a modest **0.12 × endpoint demand × profile** bonus. The fixed penalty is **1.0**. This contrasts with Generation 10’s penalty of **1.5** and Generation 16’s more involved endpoint/relay adjustments, neither of which produced better results.
- **Runtime and topology diversity are not the distinguishing factors.** Generation 11 built in approximately **0.00 seconds** and produced **64 distinct topologies** in every case, as did the other fast implementations. Its advantage is in the objective’s path filtering and weighting, not construction speed or diversity.
- **Feasibility is nearly, but not perfectly, complete for Generation 11.** It achieved **99% mean feasibility** (98% on s0 and s2; 100% on s1 and s3), slightly below the 100% rates reported for Generations 10 and 13. That small difference accompanies a material improvement in best gap and combined score.

- **Generation 11’s Demand-Weighted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate is the current leader:** it scored **0.82** with an **11.39% mean best gap**, improving on Generation 6’s previous best-batch score of **0.81** and its **14.16%** best gap. - **Combining direct-demand rewards with supported path rewards beat the direct-only alternative.** Generation 11’s direct-link rewards plus selective two-hop terms outperformed Generation 12’s clipped, capped linear-only formulation (**0.80** score, **13.80%** best gap). This is consistent with earlier results that direct-demand weighting is a robust base, while path terms can help when restricted to supported routes.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Broader two-hop formulations did not match Generation 11’s results.** Generations 10 and 13 each used 480 quadratic terms and scored **0.80** and **0.81**, respectively, below Generation 11’s **0.82**. Generation 13’s **12.45%** best gap was closer, but still worse than Generation 11’s **11.39%**.
- **More elaborate relay or endpoint adjustments have not reliably improved quality.** Generation 16’s endpoint-activity and relay-leg-fit weighting scored **0.79**, with a **15.02%** mean best gap and **40.21%** median gap. This continues the earlier pattern: added endpoint, relay, and congestion heuristics in Generations 5–9 did not consistently outperform simpler demand-conditioned rewards.
- **Direct demand alone produced weaker best and typical solutions than Generation 11.** Generation 12 scored **0.80**, with a **13.80%** mean best gap and **38.00%** median gap, versus Generation 11’s **0.82**, **11.39%**, and **35.91%**. Its zero quadratic terms show that direct-link rewards alone were less effective in this comparison.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 11’s path terms are selective by both endpoint demand and leg support.** It excludes paths without positive demand from \(i\) to \(k\), normalizes inbound traffic by destination totals and outbound traffic by source totals, and combines the shares using a capped square-root profile. The resulting **211 terms** accompany the strongest observed score.
- **The coefficients are normalized and restrained:** negative demand-normalized direct-link rewards are combined with a path bonus of **0.12 × endpoint demand × profile**, and the degree-penalty weight is fixed at **1.0**. By comparison, Generation 10 used a penalty of **1.5** and 480 terms; Generation 16 also used 480 terms with additional endpoint and relay adjustments, without improving on Generation 11.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 11 is ahead of the other newly evaluated programs on both combined score and mean best gap:** **0.82 / 11.39%**, versus Generation 13 at **0.81 / 12.45%**, Generation 12 at **0.80 / 13.80%**, Generation 10 at **0.80 / 14.32%**, and Generation 16 at **0.79 / 15.02%**. # META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep the direct-versus-path reward balance** while keeping Generation 11’s positive-endpoint-demand filter and traffic-share profile unchanged. Compare direct weights near the current `-1.0` (for example, `-0.8` and `-0.65`) with path coefficients around `0.08`, `0.16`, and `0.20`; this tests whether a modestly weaker path bonus improves the best gaps without losing the benefit of selective two-hop terms.

2. **Test alternative bounded demand normalization** without changing the interaction structure. Compare the current maximum normalization with normalization by the mean of positive off-diagonal demands, and with a clipped-maximum variant; this may prevent a few large demands from suppressing useful signal across other supported paths.

3. **Ablate and tune the path-support profile independently.** Keep the same supported paths, but compare `min(1, n * sqrt(in_support * out_support))` with a weaker cap scale and a profile exponent such as `0.75`; this isolates whether the current support weighting is well calibrated, while preserving the selective construction associated with the 211-term result.

4. **Compare relay-specific support weighting with the current profile, without broadening the path set.** For each eligible path, test a bounded factor based on the relay’s incoming and outgoing support for that particular `i → j → k` route, as well as the current factor; avoid adding general congestion or endpoint adjustments, which have not reliably helped.

5. **Target the remaining weak cases with a restrained path-reward ablation.** Evaluate the current surrogate and a zero- or reduced-quadratic-coefficient variant on `network10_s2`, the current worst case, and compare both best and median gaps; Generation 11’s selective paths beat the direct-only baseline overall, but this check can reveal whether path bonuses dilute direct-demand signal on difficult instances.
