<DESCRIPTION>
Prefer two-hop routes supported by both legs rather than one exceptionally strong leg. Currently, candidate ranking depends only on the sum of the legs’ demands, so a strong first leg can hide a poorly supported second leg.

Replace half of that sum with its geometric-mean counterpart. Balanced routes retain exactly their previous ranking score, while asymmetric routes receive less credit. Also apply a modest, bounded asymmetry discount to their rewards, including fallback routes. This preserves low-demand transit alternatives without treating every high-sum route as equally useful.

This targets misleading route rewards that can impair topology and exchange ranking as n grows. It adds no couplings and leaves degree penalties, cut corrections, and neighbor-service factors unchanged. Performance improvement remains to be tested.
</DESCRIPTION>
