# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: Pressure-Discounted Two-Hop QUBO Surrogate**
- **Implementation**: Normalizes nonnegative, non-self demand and assigns negative linear weights using a demand/square-root blend. For each positive-demand pair, it selects a small set of two-hop routes by pressure-adjusted affinity, adds a low-pressure alternative, and returns a fixed penalty weight of 1.25.
- **Performance**: Combined score is 0.89 (raw 0.83, post-processed 0.96); mean best and median gaps are 11.66% and 32.42%, with 91% feasibility and 383 quadratic terms on average.
- **Feedback**: The surrogate builds quickly and produces consistently strong post-processed results, reducing the mean gap to 4.38%. Before repair, median gaps remain around 29–37%, and the largest best-sample gap is 16.8% for network14_s0, so its strongest contribution is as a head start for the fixed post-processor.

- **Generation 8 scales and blends direct rewards deliberately.** It clips negative demand, clears the diagonal, normalizes by maximum traffic, and combines normalized demand with its square root using **0.75/0.25** weights. - **The reciprocal-link correction targets a specific structural waste.** Generation 8 penalizes paired \(i\to j\) and \(j\to i\) links, while reducing the penalty when traffic is substantial in both directions. # META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adaptively add one route candidate only when the top affinity-to-pressure scores are close.** Keep the existing two-or-four candidate rule, but include a fifth candidate when its rank is within a small relative margin of the last selected candidate. This tests whether ambiguous endpoint pairs benefit from extra route coverage while avoiding the large term growth that did not help denser variants.

2. **Discount two-hop rewards when either leg has weak demand support.** Multiply the current endpoint-demand support by a bounded factor based on normalized demand on \(i\to j\) and \(j\to k\), while retaining the existing node-pressure discount and sparse selection. This favors shortcuts supported by traffic on their constituent links without changing the direct-reward scaling that underpins the current best’s quality–size balance.

3. **Apply a bounded second-pass congestion adjustment to selected routes.** After route selection, accumulate weighted route use at each intermediate node and slightly reduce rewards for routes through the most heavily used nodes. Keep the adjustment small so it refines the existing pressure-aware ranking rather than replacing it; congestion-aware variants have produced strong repaired gaps, but larger interaction sets have not consistently improved scores.

4. **Strengthen constraint penalties selectively for frequently coupled links.** Count each link’s appearances in route-reward interactions and add a modest penalty only to the most frequently coupled links, leaving the existing size-scaled baseline unchanged elsewhere. This tests targeted feasibility improvements—particularly for weaker cases such as network15_s2—without uniformly suppressing useful rewards.

5. **Try one supported three-hop alternative for pairs with weak or ambiguous two-hop choices.** Require positive endpoint demand and support on all three legs, and discount the reward by pressure at both intermediate nodes; add at most one such alternative per qualifying pair. This explores extra path flexibility while respecting the evidence that broad route expansion or unconstrained three-hop routing can increase model size without reliably improving quality.
