<DESCRIPTION>
Rank two-hop routes using both absolute demand and endpoint-relative demand shares. A leg can be useful because it serves a substantial share of its source’s outgoing traffic or its destination’s incoming traffic, even when its absolute demand is modest.

Retain a bilateral bottleneck: the harmonic mean limits support when either leg is weak. Use this evidence consistently in transit-pressure estimation and route selection, with a bounded reward gate to avoid large changes in coefficient scale. Multiplying shares by n−1 before clipping keeps their interpretation stable across network sizes. This adds no couplings and preserves the existing sparse route budget.
</DESCRIPTION>
