# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best remains Generation 14, “stronger_two_hop,”** with score **0.82**, mean best gap **10.53%**, median gap **35.22%**, and **99% feasibility**. Its effective combination is normalized direct-demand rewards plus *selective, demand-gated* two-hop rewards that account for transit-node pressure and the demands on both route edges.
- **Pressure-aware two-hop rewards are more effective when carefully discounted than when merely added.** Generation 14 uses a node-pressure discount with a 0.75 floor and an edge-pressure factor `1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure`; its 480 quadratic terms yield a better best gap than the new Generation 19–25 variants, whose mean best gaps range from **11.98% to 14.19%**.
- **Generation 22, “edge_pressure_discount,” is the closest new result:** score **0.82**, mean best gap **11.98%**, median gap **34.81%**, and 99% pre-repair feasibility. It shares the leader’s use of edge-pressure discounting, but still trails Generation 14 by **1.45 percentage points** in mean best gap. This supports the value of pressure-aware route rewards, while showing that the complete Generation 14 formulation is stronger than the simpler variant.
- **Strong feasibility and sample diversity accompany, but do not explain, the best result.** Generation 14 and all five new programs generated 64 distinct topologies per case; the leader’s advantage is therefore more consistent with its reward formulation than with sample count.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Endpoint-pressure mixtures did not outperform direct-demand plus selective path rewards.** Generation 20 (“pressure_aware_two_hop”) uses 90% direct demand and 10% endpoint pressure, adds penalties for high-demand links sharing a node, and scores **0.81** with a **13.46%** best gap. Its median gap is **36.59%**, worse than Generation 14’s 35.22%.
- **Concave or square-root direct-demand rewards did not improve quality.** Generation 19 combines normalized demand, its square root, and endpoint traffic; Generation 23 also includes square-root demand and load-scaled path rewards. Both score **0.80** with **13.35%** and **13.46%** mean best gaps, respectively, versus the current best’s 10.53%.
- **More elaborate path adjustments did not reliably help.** Generation 23 adds transit-load scaling, route plausibility, and a four-path sharing factor but still has a **13.46%** best gap. Generation 25’s component-link support adjustment reaches **14.19%**. By contrast, Generation 14’s simpler demand gating and explicit node/edge pressure discounts perform better.
- **Weak two-hop rewards and generic endpoint weighting produced substantially worse typical samples.** Generation 25 has a **38.51%** mean median gap and Generation 23 **37.65%**, compared with Generation 14’s 35.22%. These results reinforce that merely rewarding plausible or supported paths does not ensure strong sampled topologies.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 14’s selective construction is a key implementation detail.** It skips nonpositive OD demand, creates a quadratic term only for a positive-demand `i → k` pair through `j`, and gives that term weight proportional to `-(through_demand / scale)`. This yields exactly **480 quadratic terms** and avoids rewarding unsupported routes.
- **Its pressure discounts are specific and bounded:** incident demand is computed from incoming plus outgoing traffic; node pressure is normalized by mean incident demand and floored at **0.75**. The path reward is then divided by node pressure and by `1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure`, discounting congested intermediates and route edges without eliminating their reward.
- **The best program keeps direct-link rewards simple and normalized.** Each non-self link receives `-demand[i,j] / max(demand)`; it does not add endpoint-load mixing, square-root demand, or same-node link-pair penalties. Across the new comparisons, these additions did not beat the leader.
- **Compactness and runtime are not the differentiator.** Generation 14 builds in **0.00s** with 480 quadratic terms. Generations 19–25 also build in under **0.01s** and mostly use 480 terms (Generation 20 uses 803), yet their best gaps are worse. The results favor term selection and weighting over construction speed or term count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:
