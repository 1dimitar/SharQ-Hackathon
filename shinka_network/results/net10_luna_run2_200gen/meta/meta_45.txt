# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Linear Topology Surrogate**
- **Implementation**: Assigns each directed link a linear weight equal to negative demand normalized by the matrix maximum, with no quadratic terms and a degree-penalty weight of 1.0. The evaluator receives the terms and runtime through a fixed entry point.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.80; mean best gap was 12.76%, median gap 40.37%, feasibility 100%, and the model used zero quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: All cases produced 64 distinct topologies; three had 100% valid samples before repair, while network10_s3 had 98%. Best gaps ranged from 10.7% to 15.9%, with network10_s3 worst, indicating the simple demand-only surrogate yields valid but suboptimal topologies.

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Two-Hop Surrogate QUBO**
- **Implementation**: Assigns negative linear weights to directed links in proportion to demand, then adds negative quadratic rewards for two-hop paths, discounted by transit-node pressure and direct edge demand. Returns a degree-penalty weight of 1.0; construction is efficient for the tested 10-node networks.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.81, with a 12.30% mean best gap, 36.41% mean median gap, 0.99 feasible rate, and 480 quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: The method produced topologies close to the reference best, but median gaps were substantially larger; the largest best gap was 15.6% on network10_s1. It generated 64 distinct topologies per case and built in under 0.01 seconds.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Generation 14 (“stronger_two_hop”), remains the strongest reference:** score **0.82**, mean best gap **10.53%**, median gap **35.22%**, and **99% feasibility**. Its simple normalized direct-demand reward paired with selective two-hop rewards for positive-demand OD pairs continues to outperform the newly evaluated variants on best-sample quality.
- **Bounded, pressure-aware allocation of two-hop rewards is effective, but the new allocation mix did not beat the best.** The current program, Generation 40 (“support_balanced_paths”), scores **0.82** with an **11.41%** mean best gap and **33.37%** median gap. It assigns each positive-demand OD pair a bounded budget, distributes it using geometric support and transit-node capacity, and mixes in a uniform share. Its median gap is better than Generation 14’s, but its best gap is **0.88 percentage points worse**.
- **Selective two-hop rewards with pressure discounts remain the clearest strong pattern.** Generation 14 discounts paths based on transit-node pressure and component-edge demand, while preserving the simple direct-link signal. Earlier summaries report a **10.53%** best gap, better than the demand-mixture variants and the current program.
- **High feasibility and diversity are consistently achieved across formulations.** Generation 40 has **100% mean feasibility**, while the current best has **99%**. Both produce **64 distinct topologies** and **480 quadratic terms**; these are reliable operational properties, but they do not by themselves determine gap quality.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Combining direct demand with concave-demand and endpoint-volume bonuses did not improve best-sample quality.** Generation 40’s linear term blends normalized demand, its square root, and endpoint traffic; its two-hop term uses supported-path weighting plus a uniform mix. Despite a **0.82** score, its **11.41%** best gap trails Generation 14’s **10.53%**.
- **The new support-balanced formulation still leaves a notable difficult-case gap.** In Generation 40, `network10_s2` is worst at **14.7%**, compared with **8.5%** on `network10_s1`. This is consistent with earlier results identifying `network10_s2` as a recurring weakness for multiple two-hop variants.
- **Other demand-weighted variants provide no consistent improvement over the simpler pressure-aware baseline.** Generation 39 (“two_hop_strength_025”) scores **0.82** with an **11.70%** mean best gap; Generation 41 scores **0.81** with **12.37%**; Generation 43 scores **0.80** with **14.39%**; and Generation 46 scores **0.82** with **11.04%**. Their differing demand mixtures and path weights do not displace Generation 14’s best gap.
- **Typical-sample quality remains substantially weaker than best-sample quality.** Generation 40’s median gap is **33.37%** despite its **11.41%** best gap. The other new variants likewise have median gaps around **33–37%**, reinforcing that valid, diverse samples often remain relatively costly.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 40’s direct rewards are more elaborate than the current best’s:** it normalizes demand, applies a weighted linear/square-root term, and adds an endpoint-volume component. Its resulting best gap is **11.41%**, versus **10.53%** for Generation 14’s simpler normalized direct-demand reward. The additional features improve neither reported best-sample gap nor score.
- **Generation 40 allocates a bounded indirect budget per positive-demand OD pair.** For each candidate middle node, it combines geometric support from the two component links with a square-root node-capacity discount, then blends normalized evidence with a **30% uniform allocation**. This yields **480 quadratic terms** and **100% mean feasibility**, but does not close the best-gap difference to Generation 14.
- **The current best’s path selection is selective and pressure-discounted.** Its two-hop rewards apply only when OD demand is positive and are weakened by both transit-node pressure and demand on constituent edges. Previous results associate this formulation with the strongest reported mean best gap (**10.53%**) while retaining **99% feasibility**.
- **Construction cost and model size are essentially invariant across the evaluated family.** Generation 40, like the other recent programs, builds in **0.00 seconds**, creates **480 quadratic terms**, and produces **64 distinct topologies**. The meaningful implementation differences are in reward construction and allocation, not runtime or term count.

## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 40 matches the leading score but not the leading best gap:** it scores **0.82**, with **11.41%** mean best gap and **33.37%** median gap. Against Generation 14, it is **0.88 percentage points worse** on the best gap but **1.85 points better** on the median gap; feasibility is **100% versus 99%**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep the indirect reward budget around `0.62` while keeping the current support allocation fixed.** Try `0.50`, `0.56`, and `0.68` to test whether the bounded two-hop incentive is too strong or too weak. Selective two-hop rewards are a consistent strength, but the current budget differs from Generation 14’s stronger-performing formulation.

2. **Replace the support-balanced allocation with a pressure-discounted path reward, preserving positive-demand OD gating.** For each eligible path, discount its reward by transit-node pressure and constituent-edge demand, as in the strongest Generation 14 pattern. This directly tests whether the current uniform-mixed allocation is sacrificing best-sample quality for broader path coverage.

3. **If retaining support-balanced allocation, reduce its uniform mix from `0.30` and test `0.10` and `0.20`.** The current mix gives weak-support paths a substantial share despite evidence that selective, supported paths are more effective. Keep the bounded budget and node-capacity factor unchanged to isolate the effect.

4. **Simplify the direct reward toward normalized linear demand.** Replace the current square-root and endpoint-volume blend with `-dn[i, j]`, or test a small concave contribution such as 5–10% while leaving the quadratic terms fixed. The best reported mean gap came from a simpler normalized direct-demand signal; broader direct-demand mixtures have not improved best-sample quality.

5. **Tune the transit-node pressure discount independently, especially for the difficult `network10_s2` case.** Keep the inverse-pressure form but compare mean-incident with maximum-incident normalization and modest floors such as `0.75` and `0.85`. This preserves the pressure-aware path preference associated with the strongest formulation while testing whether the current square-root capacity discount is miscalibrated.
