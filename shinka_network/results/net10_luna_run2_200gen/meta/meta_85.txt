# INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM SUMMARIES

The following are summaries of individual programs evaluated since the last meta update:

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Linear Topology Surrogate**
- **Implementation**: Assigns each directed link a linear weight equal to negative demand normalized by the matrix maximum, with no quadratic terms and a degree-penalty weight of 1.0. The evaluator receives the terms and runtime through a fixed entry point.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.80; mean best gap was 12.76%, median gap 40.37%, feasibility 100%, and the model used zero quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: All cases produced 64 distinct topologies; three had 100% valid samples before repair, while network10_s3 had 98%. Best gaps ranged from 10.7% to 15.9%, with network10_s3 worst, indicating the simple demand-only surrogate yields valid but suboptimal topologies.

**Program Name: Demand-Weighted Two-Hop Surrogate QUBO**
- **Implementation**: Assigns negative linear weights to directed links in proportion to demand, then adds negative quadratic rewards for two-hop paths, discounted by transit-node pressure and direct edge demand. Returns a degree-penalty weight of 1.0; construction is efficient for the tested 10-node networks.
- **Performance**: Combined score was 0.81, with a 12.30% mean best gap, 36.41% mean median gap, 0.99 feasible rate, and 480 quadratic terms.
- **Feedback**: The method produced topologies close to the reference best, but median gaps were substantially larger; the largest best gap was 15.6% on network10_s1. It generated 64 distinct topologies per case and built in under 0.01 seconds.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 87, “max_pressure_floor065,” is the current strongest balanced result:** score **0.84**, mean best gap **11.04%**, and mean median gap **28.90%**. Its median gap is the best among the compared programs, improving on Generation 64’s **31.61%** and Generation 80’s **32.21%**. This is notable even though Generation 87’s mean best gap is slightly worse than those two programs’ **10.77%** and **10.99%**.
- **Selective, demand-supported two-hop rewards remain a successful pattern.** Generation 87 adds a quadratic term only when `demand[i,k] > 0`, rewarding the path `i → j → k` in proportion to that through-demand. This continues the approach that performed well for Generation 64 and contrasts with broadly distributed path-reward budgets, which had weaker median gaps in Generations 74 and 78.
- **A floor on transit-node pressure may help typical-sample quality.** Generation 87 sets `node_pressure` to at least **0.65** and divides path rewards by that pressure and a component-edge load factor. Its **28.90%** median gap is better than Generation 64’s **31.61%**, although the evaluation results do not isolate the floor’s effect from the rest of the formulation.
- **Pressure-aware paths have repeatedly produced competitive results, but the outcome depends on the formulation.** Generation 70 achieved a **10.17%** mean best gap and **0.83** score but had a **34.13%** median gap. Generation 87’s stronger median and **0.84** score indicate a better balance in this batch.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Endpoint-activity blends did not beat the demand-local baseline.** Generation 74 scored **0.80** with **15.05%/36.76%** mean best/median gaps; Generation 78 scored **0.81** with **13.89%/35.22%**. Both trail Generation 87 on score and both gap measures.
- **Budget-sharing and uniform-share path rewards have been unreliable.** Generation 74’s capped, split-budget allocation and Generation 78’s uniform-share mix were associated with median gaps of **36.76%** and **35.22%**, respectively, versus Generation 87’s **28.90%**. The evaluations suggest these allocations can dilute the signal from individually demand-supported paths.
- **High feasibility has not consistently meant better solution quality.** Generation 87 is **69%** feasible before repair, versus **97–100%** for several newer variants, yet it has the best median gap in the compared results. Generation 74’s **100%** feasibility accompanied a **15.05%** best gap and **36.76%** median gap.
- **Improving best samples alone has not reliably improved typical outcomes.** Generation 80’s **10.99%** mean best gap nearly matches Generation 64’s, but its **32.21%** median gap is worse. Generation 70’s best gap of **10.17%** likewise coexists with a **34.13%** median gap.

## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 87 retains a simple normalized direct-link reward:** every off-diagonal `demand[i,j]` gets linear weight `-demand[i,j] / max(demand)`. This matches the effective demand-local baseline identified in Generation 64; more elaborate endpoint-activity blends in Generations 74 and 78 did not improve the gap profile.
- **Its two-hop terms combine demand selectivity with two pressure discounts.** The code skips nonpositive `demand[i,k]`, then uses weight  
  `-0.56 * (through_demand / scale) / (node_pressure[j] * (1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure))`. The transit-pressure floor is **0.65**, and edge pressure uses the direct demands on `i → j` and `j → k`. Generation 87 has **480 quadratic terms** and the strongest median gap among the compared programs.
- **The fixed degree-penalty weight is 1.0; feasibility remains a separate concern.** Generation 87 yields **64 distinct topologies per case**, builds in **0.00 seconds**, and has **69%** pre-repair validity. These results reinforce that validity rate and surrogate ranking quality do not move together.
- **The current program’s strongest evidence is for typical-sample performance, not uniformly better best-case performance.** Its median gaps range from **26.1%** to **31.2%**, while its best gaps range from **7.1%** to **14.6%**. Thus its overall advantage is reflected particularly in the median metric, not in every case’s best sample.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep the two-hop reward strength around the current `0.56`.** Try `0.46`, `0.50`, `0.62`, and `0.68`, keeping the direct-demand rewards and pressure discounts fixed. This isolates a key parameter in the best-performing formulation and may improve typical-sample quality without changing its demand-supported path structure.

2. **Test alternative transit-node pressure calibration.** Keep the current incident-demand measure but compare floors of `0.55`, `0.75`, and `0.85`; separately test normalizing incident demand by its mean instead of its maximum. Generation 87’s pressure-aware two-hop terms coincide with the strongest median gap, so calibrating their discount is a promising incremental change.

3. **Vary the edge-pressure discount independently.** Compare the current `1 + 0.5 * edge_pressure` denominator with `1 + 0.75 * edge_pressure` and `(1 + edge_pressure) ** 1.5`, leaving node pressure and path strength unchanged. This tests whether stronger suppression of links carrying substantial direct demand improves typical outcomes while preserving selective path rewards.

4. **Limit aggregate two-hop reward per OD pair without spreading it uniformly across paths.** For each positive-demand pair `(i, k)`, cap the sum of its path rewards relative to its direct linear reward, while retaining the current pressure-adjusted reward for each supported path. This could prevent indirect rewards from overwhelming direct demand while avoiding the broad budget-sharing patterns associated with weaker median gaps.

5. **Try a mild concave transform on normalized through-demand, keeping direct rewards linear.** Replace `x = through_demand / scale` in the quadratic reward with a blend such as `0.8 * x + 0.2 * sqrt(x)`. This preserves the successful demand-local signal and tests whether giving smaller supported demands relatively more influence can improve typical solutions, including the weaker `network10_s1` result.
