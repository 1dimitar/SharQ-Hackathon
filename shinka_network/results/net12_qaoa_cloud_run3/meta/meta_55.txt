# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best remains Generation 28, `targeted_high_demand_relays` (score 0.85).** Its calibrated direct-link reward—`0.75*demand + 0.25*sqrt(demand)` after normalization—preserves incentives for moderate-demand links while emphasizing heavy ones. Combined with pressure-aware relay ranking and targeted extra relay support for high-demand OD pairs, it achieved a **10.86% mean best gap** and **25.17% mean median gap**.
- **Targeting relay support beats broad or poorly calibrated support in these evaluations.** Generation 28 selects a compact set of relays, with up to two extras for high-demand pairs. The newer Generation 52 `bottleneck_relay_ranking` used 264 quadratic terms and scored **0.84** (10.58% best gap), while Generation 50 `gentler_relay_pressure` also used 264 terms and scored **0.83** (11.66% best gap). Neither surpassed Generation 28’s combined score.
- **Pressure-aware relay ranking is useful when paired with selective support.** Generation 28 ranks relays by balanced leg affinity discounted by node pressure, and scored **0.85**. Generation 52 also discounted relay rankings by pressure and achieved the closest recent best-gap result at **10.58%**, but its **4% feasibility** and **29.83% median gap** show that favorable best samples do not guarantee consistently good or valid samples.
- **Sparse, calibrated rewards can outperform both broader interactions and extreme sparsity.** Generation 28’s **304 quadratic terms** produced the strongest score. The new Generation 55 `focused_relay_qubo` reduced this to **24 terms** but scored **0.80** with an **18.04% mean best gap**; previous comparisons likewise found that Generation 47’s 18-term model scored 0.83 but had a weaker 14.01% best gap.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Adding congestion interactions did not improve on the best design.** Generation 49 `spare_capacity_relays` added sparse positive congestion interactions among busy same-direction links with a fixed weight of 1.5, yet scored **0.80**, with a **16.58% mean best gap**, **34.77% median gap**, and **17% feasibility**. Its best case gap varied from 12.7% to 20.4%.
- **Simply changing pressure treatment or adding more relay rewards did not resolve inconsistent solution quality.** Generation 50’s gentler pressure approach scored **0.83**, and Generation 52’s bottleneck ranking scored **0.84**; both had low raw feasibility (**9%** and **4%**) and median gaps above 29%. Pressure-aware ranking helps best-sample quality, but is not sufficient for reliable feasible samples.
- **Reciprocal-link rewards and separately applied degree penalties were weak in the tested configuration.** Generation 53 `capacity_pairing` scored **0.77**, with an **18.50% best gap**, **43.89% median gap**, and **9% feasibility**. Its 118-term model generated diverse topologies, but only 16% and 2% of solutions were feasible on the two cases.
- **Restricting relay rewards to a small high-demand subset can sacrifice overall quality.** Generation 55 rewarded only top-decile OD pairs and allowed up to two relays per pair; despite just 24 quadratic terms, it scored **0.80** and had a **32.25% mean median gap**. Its case-level median gap reached **37.2%**, with only 7–10% raw feasibility.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 28’s direct-link normalization and concave reward blend are central strengths.** It clips negative demands, clears diagonal traffic, normalizes by peak demand, then assigns each off-diagonal link `-(0.75*value + 0.25*sqrt(value))`. This retains a nonzero influence for moderate demands without letting them dominate heavy-demand links; the program achieved the best overall score, **0.85**.
- **Its relay selection combines affinity, pressure, and a controlled support budget.** Candidate relays are ranked using `sqrt((0.1+a)*(0.1+b)) / (0.75 + node_pressure[j])`, where pressure is based on the larger of total incoming and outgoing demand. Ordinary OD pairs receive limited relay coverage, while high-demand pairs can receive up to two extras. This produced **304 terms** and better overall results than the newer 264-term alternatives.
- **The current best distinguishes the quality of relay candidates without letting rank dominate.** Its relay reward uses a mild rank factor, `0.8 + 0.2*rank/(1+rank)`, and discounts the contribution again by relay pressure. The measured advantage is consistent with the overall result, though the evaluations do not isolate the effect of this factor from the other design choices.
- **Raw feasibility remains a limitation even for the best implementation.** Generation 28 had **20%** and **14%** pre-repair validity on network12_s0 and network12_s1, respectively, despite its top score. The newer programs commonly had even lower rates—Generation 52 had **4%**, and Generations 50, 53, and 55 averaged **9%**—so better best-sample quality has not translated into reliably valid sampling.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 28 remains the overall leader.** Its score of **0.85** is above Generation 52’s **0.84**, Generation 50’s **0.83**, and Generations 49 and 55’s **0.80**; it also has the lowest mean median gap among the newly summarized comparisons with reported figures (**25.17%** versus 29.83–34.77% for these programs).
- **Generation 52 is close on best-sample cost, but weaker on typical and feasible samples.** Its **10.58% mean best gap** is slightly below Generation 28’s **10.86%**, but its score is **0.84** versus **0.85**, its median gap is **29.83%** versus **25.17%**, and its feasibility is **4%** versus **17%**. This reinforces that best-gap performance alone is not a sufficient indicator of overall quality.
- **The new evaluations show a consistent weakness in feasibility and median quality.** Generations 49–55 reported mean feasibility of only **4–17%** and mean median gaps of **29.83–43.89%**. Generation 28 is not highly feasible either (17% mean), but its median gap and combined score are better than the recent alternatives.
- **Quadratic-term count does not predict score by itself.** The current best scored **0.85** with 304 terms; Generation 52 scored **0.84** with 264, while Generation 55 scored **0.80** with 24 and Generation 53 scored **0.77** with 118. These results support the earlier finding that targeted interaction selection and reward calibration matter more than minimizing term count alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Sweep the ordinary relay budget**, changing only `relay_count` from `min(n-2, max(2, n//4))` to `min(n-2, max(1, n//5))` and `min(n-2, n//3)` in separate variants. Generation 28’s targeted support is a strength, so this tests whether slightly less or more ordinary coverage can improve the weaker `network12_s1` gap without changing reward calibration or ranking.

2. **Test a higher high-demand cutoff**, raising `high_demand_cutoff` from the 80th to the 90th percentile while keeping the two-extra-relay cap and all other settings fixed. This checks whether concentrating additional support on the most exceptional OD pairs improves best-sample quality while preserving the selectivity associated with the current 304-term model.

3. **Try a bottleneck-sensitive relay ranking** in place of geometric-mean affinity: use `min(first_leg, second_leg) + 0.25 * max(first_leg, second_leg)`, then retain the existing pressure denominator, rank factor, and relay budgets. Recent pressure-aware ranking results support refining relay selection, while this variant specifically favors relays with two useful legs rather than one dominant leg.

4. **Tune pressure sensitivity without changing candidate selection**, replacing `(0.75 + node_pressure[j])` in the relay reward denominator with `(0.75 + node_pressure[j])**alpha`; test `alpha=0.75` and `alpha=1.25` separately. This isolates whether gentler or stronger pressure discounting improves on the best program’s pressure-aware design, avoiding the broader changes that made newer variants less consistent.

5. **Calibrate direct-link rewards independently**, testing `0.85*value + 0.15*sqrt(value)` and `0.65*value + 0.35*sqrt(value)` while keeping relay terms unchanged. The current `0.75/0.25` blend is a demonstrated strength; these controlled variants test whether emphasizing heavy demands or retaining more incentive for moderate demands can reduce the remaining gap without disrupting targeted relay support.
