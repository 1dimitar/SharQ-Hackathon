# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Sparse, ranked relay support remains the strongest observed design.** The current best, Generation 5 `sparse_relay_support`, scores **0.85** with an **11.36% mean best gap** and just **264 quadratic terms**. It selects a limited number of relays per positive-demand OD pair, ranking by two-leg affinity and node pressure, rather than rewarding all possible routes. Its case gaps are **+10.0%** and **+12.8%**.
- **Direct rewards that blend linear and concave demand signal are effective alongside sparse path support.** The current best uses `-(0.75*value + 0.25*sqrt(value))` for direct links and the corresponding positive blend for OD path support. This preserves preference for high-demand links while retaining reward for moderate demand. Generation 0 used a similar blend but scored **0.83** with **880 quadratic terms** and a **14.61%** mean best gap, so the reward shape appears useful, while sparse relay selection distinguishes the stronger current result.
- **Two-leg relay affinity combined with a pressure discount is a successful ranking signal.** Generation 5 ranks candidates by `sqrt((0.1+a)*(0.1+b)) / (0.75+pressure)`, favoring relays with plausible support on both legs while discounting heavily loaded nodes. Its pressure uses the larger of normalized incoming and outgoing traffic, with a 0.65 floor. This current-best configuration outperforms the newly evaluated pressure-aware sparse variants: Generation 13 scores **0.84** with a **9.92%** best gap, and Generation 15 scores **0.82** with a **13.00%** gap.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Broad two-hop support has generally produced larger models and weaker results than the current best.** Generations 10 and 12 use **880 quadratic terms**, scoring **0.79** and **0.74**, with mean best gaps of **18.48%** and **22.65%**. Generation 16 also has 880 terms and scores **0.78**, with a **16.43%** gap. Generation 5’s 264-term sparse model scores **0.85** with an **11.36%** gap.
- **Sparse support alone is not sufficient when relay selection or scoring differs.** Generation 15 uses only **176 terms**, but scores **0.82**, has a **13.00%** best gap, and only **5%** feasibility; its case gaps are **11.8%** and **14.2%**. Generation 13’s 220-term model performs better (score **0.84**, best gap **9.92%**) but still trails Generation 5’s score and has just **15%** feasibility. Thus term count is associated with compactness, but the current best’s particular ranking and reward combination matters.
- **Degree-penalty adaptation and penalty magnitude have not reliably solved feasibility or quality.** Generations 10 and 12 derive adaptive penalties yet have only **16–17%** feasibility and best gaps of **18.48–22.65%**. Generation 16 uses penalty **1.0**, with **17%** feasibility and a **16.43%** gap. Generation 5’s fixed penalty of **1.5** yields only **8%** feasibility, so these results do not show that penalty tuning alone drives best-solution quality.
- **Good best gaps do not imply consistently strong samples.** Generation 13 has a strong **9.92%** mean best gap but a **30.61%** median gap and **15%** feasibility. The current best likewise has a **25.86%** median gap and only **8%** pre-repair feasibility, indicating that high-quality best-found samples coexist with weaker typical samples.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best bounds path terms per demanded OD pair.** It skips zero-demand pairs and selects `min(n-2, max(2, n//4))` relay candidates. On network12 this yields **264 quadratic terms**, versus **880** in Generations 10, 12, and 16; the model is compact without sacrificing the best observed score.
- **Relay ranking incorporates both leg plausibility and bottleneck pressure.** For candidate relay `j`, the code computes affinity as `sqrt((0.1+a)*(0.1+b))`, then divides by `0.75 + node_pressure[j]`. Pressure is the maximum of incoming and outgoing demand, normalized and floored at 0.65. This differs from merely retaining a fixed number of relays: the chosen relays are ranked using both path legs and relay load.
- **The current-best reward and key construction are consistent across terms.** Demand is clipped to nonnegative values, the diagonal is cleared, and traffic is normalized by its maximum. Direct and two-hop rewards use the same linear/square-root blend; the two edge keys are sorted before insertion to canonicalize quadratic terms. The measured 264-term model reflects this bounded, deduplicated construction.
- **Evaluation exposes a gap between optimization quality and raw feasibility.** The current best returns penalty weight **1.5**, but only **10%** of `network12_s0` samples and **6%** of `network12_s1` samples are valid before repair. Its best gap worsens from **+10.0%** to **+12.8%** on `network12_s1`, alongside fewer distinct topologies (**62** versus **93**), while both cases have 264 terms and 6,012 circuits.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best remains the overall leader in combined score among the reported programs.** Generation 5 scores **0.85** with an **11.36%** mean best gap. Generation 13 is next at **0.84** with a **9.92%** gap—better on that metric alone, but lower in combined score—with **220 terms**, **30.61%** median gap, and **15%** feasibility. Generation 6 previously scored **0.84** with a **12.55%** best gap.
- **The new broad-support variants are materially behind the current best.** Generation 10 scores **0.79** (18.48% best gap), Generation 12 **0.74** (22.65%), and Generation 16 **0.78** (16.43%). Each has 880 terms, compared with 264 for Generation 5. Their reported network12 best gaps are also worse: about **+14.1–24.1%**, versus Generation 5’s **+10.0%** and **+12.8%**.
- **Among the new sparse variants, Generation 13 is closest, but results vary with the sparse-relay design.** Generation 13’s 220-term pressure-aware model reaches **0.84** and a **9.92%** mean best gap, while Generation 15’s 176-term variant scores **0.82** with a **13.00%** gap. Generation 5’s 264-term design still has the best combined score (**0.85**) and better median gap than Generation 13 (**25.86%** vs. **30.61%**).
- **Feasibility and median quality remain weak across the leading approaches.** Generation 5 has **8%** mean pre-repair feasibility and a **25.86%** median gap; Generation 13 improves feasibility to **15%** but has a **30.61%** median gap. The broad Generation 10/12/16 variants report **16–17%** feasibility but substantially poorer best and median gaps, so feasibility rate does not track best-found quality consistently.**

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Tune the relay budget with a small controlled sweep.** Keep the current affinity-and-pressure ranking and compare `max(1, n // 5)`, `max(2, n // 4)`, and `n // 3` relays per positive-demand OD pair, capped at `n - 2`. This tests whether a modest change from the best program’s 264-term model improves its weakest case without reverting to broad support.

2. **Give extra relay options only to the largest-demand OD pairs.** Keep the current relay count for most pairs, but add one or two top-ranked relays for pairs whose normalized demand exceeds a chosen threshold or falls among the largest-demand fraction. This targets the weaker `network12_s1` result while limiting the model growth that has hurt broad-support variants.

3. **Test a ranking score that more strongly requires both path legs.** Compare the current geometric-mean affinity with a minimum-leg-sensitive score such as `sqrt(min(a, b) * (max(a, b) + ε))`, leaving the pressure discount and relay budget unchanged. This isolates whether candidate relays supported by one strong leg but one weak leg are diluting the current sparse selection.

4. **Sweep the strength of the existing pressure discount.** Keep the current pressure definition and sparse ranking, but compare denominators of `(0.75 + pressure) ** α` for a few values around `α = 1`, applying each value consistently to relay ranking and quadratic weights. Pressure-aware sparse variants have been competitive, so this is a focused test of whether the current bottleneck discount is too strong or too weak.

5. **Tune direct-link and two-hop reward balance independently.** Preserve the linear-plus-square-root reward shape, but test small changes to the direct blend around `0.75/0.25`, then separately vary the quadratic multiplier around `0.25`. The blend is shared by successful designs, while broad support and penalty-only changes have underperformed; changing one reward component at a time will make the results interpretable.
